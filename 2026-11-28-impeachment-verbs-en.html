Proposed, impeached and removed are different verbs

Check the procedure before the date in impeachment news

KOENJAZHES

As political demands for impeachment grow louder, verbs matter. Drafting a proposal, a parliamentary vote to impeach and a Constitutional Court decision are separate stages. The November 27 News Factory political discussion covered party demands, scheduling and preparation of a motion. Neither side's political expectations establish that the next stage has already happened.

The first constitutional boundary

Article 65 of Korea's Constitution allows the National Assembly to vote to impeach specified public officials who violate the Constitution or other laws in performing their duties. Except for the president, a motion requires at least one third of all Assembly members to propose it and a majority of all members to pass it. Applying the higher presidential threshold to a judge's impeachment is incorrect. The intensity of political criticism is also distinct from the legal requirements.

Suspension is not final removal

The same article suspends an impeached official's exercise of powers until adjudication. Combining the interim effect of the vote with the judgment's conclusion confuses the Assembly's role with the Constitutional Court's. An impeachment decision removes the official from office without exempting them from civil or criminal responsibility. It is not the same process as a criminal determination of guilt.

A calendar needs a source and a status

Is a date an interviewee's demand, a party's formal decision or an agreed parliamentary schedule? This article explains the procedure; it is not breaking news asserting that a party's timetable is final. Evidence supporting the grounds for impeachment and their legal merits require separate examination of original documents, responses and adjudication records. Supporters and opponents alike should avoid completed verbs for unfinished procedures.
