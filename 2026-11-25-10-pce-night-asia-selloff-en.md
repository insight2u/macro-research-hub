# Asia shook first over a single late-night PCE print

## From the AI selloff to the Fed's rate path, the thread connecting world markets on November 25

November 25, 2026 | Into the World to Earn (U.S. & Rates)

Major Asian markets fell across the board on the 25th, as selling in artificial-intelligence-linked stocks resumed on Wall Street. Taiwan's Taiex lost 5%, with TSMC down 3.6% and MediaTek down 10%. South Korea's KOSPI fell 6.2%, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix plunging 9.9% and 11.1% respectively. U.S. stock index futures and oil prices also fell.[1]

## Why the relief did not last a day

A day earlier, the market had taken comfort from news that Nvidia was restoring Jensen Huang as its CEO. But the AP reported that the relief was tempered by questions over the chipmaker's corporate governance and President Donald Trump's influence.[1] Huang is scheduled to meet three U.S. House committees behind closed doors on December 1 over China-related allegations and export controls on chips bound for China, and to appear at a public Senate Banking Committee hearing on December 3. A U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission review of Nvidia's disclosures is also cited as a burden.[2] The market's reading is that the return was not the end of uncertainty but the start of the next schedule.

## The problem is not price but the funding structure

Chosun Biz analyzed that market concern is shifting from the size of AI investment to how it is financed. So-called "neocloud" companies, which buy Nvidia GPUs in bulk and rent out computing capacity, fund their investments with borrowed money. CoreWeave, a leading name, issued bonds maturing in 2032 last June at a coupon of 9.625%; their market yield has recently risen into the 15-16% range, meaning investors now demand a higher price for credit risk.[2]

Memory demand remains firm for now. Micron said 75% of its 2027 production is already sold. On the other hand, iM Securities laid out a worst-case scenario in which part of the 2028 U.S. data-center expansion slips to 2029 and some rerouted demand from China disappears, which could flip the 2028 DRAM balance from a shortage to a slight oversupply. That is a what-if scenario, not a settled forecast.[2]

## Tonight at 10:30 p.m., the input for December's rate decision

The next input for all these interpretations arrives at 10:30 p.m. Korea time tonight: the U.S. October Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index, the Fed's benchmark for judging inflation. The market expects a 3.8% year-on-year rise for headline PCE and 3.2% for core PCE, both higher than in September (3.6% and 3.1%).[2]

The Fed held its policy rate at 3.75-4.00% in October. CME FedWatch prices a 96.4% chance of a 25-basis-point hike in December and a 3.6% chance of a 50-basis-point hike. Governor Christopher Waller said a 50bp hike could be considered if monthly core CPI came in at 0.3%, and it did; New York Fed President John Williams has said a 50bp hike is not necessary.[2] One reading holds that an in-line PCE would bring little shock, while another warns that an upside surprise would raise fears of additional tightening early next year.[2] This article is market commentary, not investment advice.
