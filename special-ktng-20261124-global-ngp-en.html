The smoke-free transition at PMI, BAT and Imperial, read through their 2025 filings

DailyShot Macro Research | KT&G Special 4

As of: November 24, 2026

This is a structural comparison that places each company's 2025 full-year results on the same starting line; it is not a table that substitutes for the latest 2026 quarterly results. As KT&G seeks to widen its NGP footprint with lil and nicotine pouches, it is worth examining how to compare competitors' product classifications, profits and capital allocation.

The tobacco industry's transition can no longer be explained by promises about the future alone. Philip Morris International (PMI) disclosed that it earned 41.5% of its 2025 revenue from "smoke-free" businesses, while British American Tobacco (BAT) said "smokeless" accounted for 18.2% of revenue. Imperial Brands' next-generation product (NGP) revenue grew 13.7%, yet the company presents the sustainable value of traditional combustible cigarettes and NGP scale-up as the two pillars of its strategy. All three are pursuing a transition, but their speed, accounting boundaries and the role of the legacy cigarette business differ.

The numbers show the size of business performance but do not prove health outcomes. The three companies also classify their products differently. This article reads separately what each company's filings say about business change and what they do not yet say.

1. The same word "transition," different accounting boundaries

PMI calls products that deliver nicotine without combustion smoke-free products (SFP). They include heated tobacco, e-vapor devices and oral products. At the end of 2025 these products were sold in 106 markets, with an estimated 43.5 million adult users by the company's count. SFP shipment volume was 179.1 billion "equivalent units," classified as 155.1 billion units of heated tobacco, 20.7 billion units of oral products and 3.3 billion units of vapor products. These figures follow the company's own conversion rules and should not be read as the actual count of pouches.[1]

BAT groups heated products, modern oral and vapour into "New Categories," and also uses a broader "Smokeless" category that includes traditional oral. In 2025 New Categories revenue was £3.621 billion, up 7.0% at constant currency. Smokeless accounted for 18.2% of total revenue, and the company's counted smokeless consumers numbered 34.1 million.[2] BAT's smokeless consumer count cannot be read as the number of heated-tobacco and e-cigarette users alone.

Imperial calls vapes, heated systems and modern oral NGP. According to the company's brand materials, blu, Zone and Skruf, and Pulze, iD and iSenzia fall into that category.[3] FY2025 NGP net revenue was about £370 million, up 13.7% at constant currency. No equivalent metric for Imperial was identified that maps directly onto PMI's smoke-free share of total revenue or BAT's smokeless consumer count.[4]

Read over time, the metrics each company disclosed for 2025 show the change. PMI's smoke-free revenue share was 36.4% in 2023, 38.7% in 2024 and 41.5% in 2025. BAT's smokeless revenue share rose from 17.5% in 2024 to 18.2% in 2025. Imperial's NGP net revenue was £265 million in FY2023, £329 million in FY2024 and about £370 million in FY2025. Definitions and denominators differ across the companies, so these numbers cannot be read as a direct corporate ranking.[1][2][4]

2. PMI: the leader pulling "after combustion" into the center of revenue

PMI's total 2025 net revenue was $40.6 billion, of which smoke-free net revenue was $16.9 billion, or 41.5% of the total. Smoke-free revenue grew 15.0% on a reported basis and 14.1% at constant currency, and shipment volume rose 12.8%. Compared with total shipment growth of 1.4%, the portfolio shift contributed heavily to the company's growth.[1][5]

The growth engine is not one product family. The company reported equivalent heated-tobacco shipments up 11.0%, oral smoke-free products up 18.5% and e-vapor products up 100%. Within oral products, the 36% increase in nicotine pouches was the biggest driver. IQOS represents heated tobacco, ZYN oral pouches and VEEV vapor products. PMI said it applies a multi-category strategy of carrying all three product families in one market in 52 markets.[1]

A change as important as growth is that this business is moving onto a profit-generating footing. PMI announced that smoke-free accounted for about 43% of group gross profit. Twenty-seven markets passed the point where smoke-free was more than half of annual revenue, and eight passed 75%. This is a country-by-country revenue composition, not an indicator that consumers worldwide have stopped using combustible products.[5]

At its 2023 Investor Day, PMI set out an ambition of earning more than two-thirds of total net revenue from smoke-free products by 2030. That requires lifting the 2025 figure of 41.5% considerably higher. The profit contribution of oral and vapor products, complete switching by adult smokers and expansion into new markets are the keys to execution. This is a management aspiration, not an externally guaranteed result or a prediction of health outcomes.[1][6]

3. BAT: oral pouches lead, while heated and vapor move at different speeds

BAT's 2025 group revenue was £25.61 billion, down 1.0% on a reported basis but up 2.1% at constant currency. New Categories revenue was £3.621 billion, up 5.5% reported and 7.0% at constant currency. Smokeless revenue was 18.2% of group revenue, up 0.7 percentage points from a year earlier. This shows the growth segment expanding, but it does not mean the legacy business is disappearing at the same pace.[2]

By category, modern oral grew the fastest. BAT reported 2025 pouch volume of 12.2 billion units, up 47%. It expanded Velo Plus nationwide in the United States. BAT's annual report counts 34.1 million smokeless consumers, up 4.7 million from a year earlier. The company, however, retroactively restated its FY2024 comparison figure from 29.1 million to 29.4 million to reflect an improved consumer survey methodology in Germany.[2]

Not every category moved in the same direction. BAT's reported vapour volume was 538 million units, down 13% from a year earlier, and heated-product (HP) sticks fell 4% to 20 billion. Modern oral pouches, by contrast, rose 47% to 12.2 billion. This divergence shows that distribution, repurchase, regulatory compliance and category profitability matter as much as new-product launches.[2]

BAT's long-term ambitions are 50 million smokeless consumers by 2030 and more than half of group revenue from smokeless products by 2035. The company also reported New Category contribution of £442 million in 2025 at constant currency. That is a category-level contribution metric distinct from group operating profit, and the company's long-term targets are not independently verified outcomes either.[2]

4. Imperial: focus over scale, with traditional cigarettes as the cash anchor

Imperial's fiscal year ends on September 30. FY2025 tobacco & NGP net revenue was £8.316 billion, up 4.1% at constant currency, and NGP net revenue was about £370 million, up 13.7% at constant currency and 14.9% reported. The company disclosed free cash flow of £2.7 billion, underpinned by strong cash generation from the legacy cigarette business.[4]

The product strategy takes the form of building scale in chosen categories. The U.S. modern oral product Zone launched in 2024 and had widened distribution to about 100,000 stores by the FY2025 results announcement. U.S. NGP net revenue was £70 million, up 69.8% at constant currency; Europe was £280 million, up 8.8%. The region bundling Africa, Asia, Australia and Central and Eastern Europe was £18 million, down 30.8%.[4]

Under its 2030 strategy, Imperial presents the sustainable value of combustibles and NGP scale-up as two simultaneous pillars. It is closer to a strategy of securing pricing and cash flow from the legacy business to invest selectively in new products. A target in the same format as PMI's — a specific smoke-free share of total net revenue by a specific year — was not identified in these materials.[4][9]

5. What the numbers explain and what they do not

PMI has grown its non-combustion category to 41.5% of group revenue. BAT reports an 18.2% smokeless revenue share and 34.1 million users, showing oral-pouch growth alongside declines in heated and vapor products. Imperial's NGP growth rate is high, but traditional cigarettes, distribution and cash generation remain the foundation of the business.

That difference cannot be converted straight into "who made the healthier transition." Product categories, the distinction between gross and net revenue, currency adjustments, overlapping user counts and consumer-count methodologies differ across companies. PMI counts smoke-free users reflecting multi-product users. BAT publishes its own adult consumer survey figures and changed past comparison figures after revising its methodology. Imperial does not present an equivalent total user count.[1][2][4]

The engineering property of "no combustion" does not mean "harmless," either. The World Health Organization explains that heated tobacco products produce an aerosol containing nicotine and toxic chemicals, and that current evidence is insufficient to conclude they are less harmful than conventional cigarettes. WHO also states that all tobacco use is harmful and that there is no safe level of exposure. Companies' product classifications and harm-reduction claims must be distinguished from independent public-health evidence.[10][11]

Three questions remain on the transition's report card. First, does revenue shifting to non-combustion products translate into actual profit? Second, does user growth mean complete switching by adult smokers, or does it include dual use and new users? Third, how do companies invest in each category amid country-by-country regulation, illicit products and tax changes? Filings give relatively many numbers for the first question but do not adequately answer the second and long-term health outcomes.

6. Conclusion: replacing combustion starts in revenue, but does not end there

The filings of PMI, BAT and Imperial show that the non-combustion business is no longer a side experiment but one that changes capital allocation, brand portfolios and growth expectations. Yet the three companies are not heading to the same destination at the same speed. PMI is shifting the center of revenue by expanding product families and markets; BAT is managing rapid oral-product growth alongside slowdowns in other categories; and Imperial is building scale in chosen NGP markets while sustaining cash flow from the legacy business.

Going forward, repeated disclosures must be tracked not only for revenue and shipment volume by category but also for profit, country-by-country regulatory change, the boundaries of user counts and the size of the legacy combustible business. The "smoke-free transition" is the name companies have given a business strategy. The conclusion that it equals an improvement in public health is possible only when separate, independent evidence accumulates.
