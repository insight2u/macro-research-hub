A stabilization fund: money prepared is not money spent

Read the verbs before comparing the size of a policy

KOENJAZHES

A market sell-off brings both criticism that the government is doing nothing and demands for much larger intervention. Maebul Show's economic discussion also questioned the speed and scale of stabilization measures. Before judging, establish the facts. Financial Services Commission material on a November 22 meeting already includes preparations for a stock-market stabilization fund, looser share-buyback limits and monitoring of illegal short selling.

Preparation and intervention are separate stages

Announcing preparations, securing funding commitments and buying shares in the market are not the same action. A potential fund size is not an amount already spent today. Conversely, failure to deploy the entire amount does not mean no action occurred. An assessment should record announcement and implementation dates separately.

Buybacks are not public-fund purchases

The documents describe relaxation of daily company buyback limits from November 24 through February 23, 2027. This gives firms more room to buy their own shares with their own money and judgment. It neither guarantees a corresponding share-price increase nor forces every company to buy. Cash positions, actual disclosures and completed purchases need separate checks.

Illegal-trade surveillance is not a blanket ban

The authorities also announced three months of intensive monitoring of illegal short selling. Watching unlawful trades differs from prohibiting all short selling. It would be wrong either to say the announcement ignored short selling or to claim it imposed a comprehensive ban. Any broader change covering lawful trades requires its own official confirmation.

Whether it was enough is a different question

The existence of measures does not establish their adequacy. The editors ask about timing, execution size, the channels of market stress and who ultimately bears the risk. One day's rebound cannot prove success, just as one day's fall cannot prove failure. This article explains the verbs of policy. It does not present a broadcast participant's loss as our reporting or assert coordinated manipulation by foreign investors.
