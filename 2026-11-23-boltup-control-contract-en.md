# A 51-billion-won stake deal is not the same as charging-business success

## The asset being acquired is a shareholding

LG Uplus's November 20 DART filing describes the acquisition of 20 million shares in LG Uplus BoltUp from Kakao Mobility. It lists a price of KRW 51 billion and KRW 2,550 per share. The seller and the company issuing the acquired shares are different entities. This is not an acquisition of Kakao itself.[1][2]

Yonhap also reported the stake purchase that day. But the price does not establish how many additional chargers will be built or how much charging prices will fall. It is consideration for shares, not a new-charger construction budget or a valuation of the entire enterprise.[3][1]

## A contract date is not proof of closing

The filing gives November 19 as the transaction date and expressly defines it as the contract-signing date. The board decision is dated November 19 too. Neither the filing date nor a news publication date should replace that date. A contract disclosure alone does not independently confirm completed closing.[1]

The disclosed post-acquisition holding is 40,000,001 shares, or 100%. The purpose is to increase a major subsidiary stake and make it wholly owned. The filing says its contents and schedule may change with the parties' agreement and subsequent developments. The 100% figure describes the disclosed post-acquisition structure.[1][2]

## Control and operational performance are different questions

Wholly owned status changes who owns the equity. Charger utilization, repair response and operating costs remain separate business questions. One ownership percentage cannot measure all of them.

The filing alone does not quantify future profit improvement, its timing or a reduction in customer prices. Later business results and service changes will be needed to test expectations. Buying more equity and turning that decision into operating results are different stages.

## Charging guidance is not evidence of this contract

The video below is Hyundai Canada's 2024 guide to charging the IONIQ 5. It is manufacturer guidance for that model and Canada's electrical and charging environment. It does not verify Korean installation specifications, current prices or BoltUp's performance. It is not an announcement or closing record for this stake contract.[4]

Different charging methods provide background to service operations. But begin this acquisition with the shares, consideration, signing date and disclosed ownership structure. The charging business's results are the next question, not a result already proven by the price tag.
