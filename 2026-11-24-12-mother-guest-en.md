# "Mother and a Guest": a story and the position from which we see it

A source-based introduction to the 1961 film · Culture

## Begin with the archive, not an invented viewing

Shin Sang-ok's "Mother and a Guest" is a 1961 film. The Korean Film Archive and Korean Movie Database (KMDb) list Choi Eun-hee, Jeon Young-seon and Kim Jin-kyu among its cast, and credit Lim Hee-jae's adaptation of Ju Yo-seop's work. This is not a fresh full-film review: it introduces the film through those institutions' records, commentary and official viewing link.[1][2]

The accompanying Korean Classic Film channel video is directly linked from the KMDb film page. The checks here covered the page's link, the video title and the publishing channel. They do not establish full playback or subtitle quality. Access conditions may vary by region and service state.[2][3]

## An original work and an adaptation are not identical lists

The archive explains that the film adds the Korean character for "room" to the original story's title. Its relationships unfold when an art teacher lodges in a home shared by a mother, daughter and mother-in-law. KMDb places young Ok-hee at the center of communicating the adults' feelings.[1][2]

The question is not only how the plot ends. Who knows what, and through whom does information travel? Knowing that a child stands between the adults invites viewers to distinguish spoken information from what characters understand. This is a question offered before viewing, not this article's direct analysis of a particular scene.

## Attribute the interpretation

KMDb's commentary reads the central pair's feelings as indirectly conveyed through Ok-hee's viewpoint, contrasted with the relationship between the maid and egg seller. That remains the institution's interpretation. It is not rewritten as a viewer's personal experience, and its extended wording is not reproduced.[2]

Viewers can use that starting point to examine how the images communicate emotion. Who can speak directly, who must speak through someone else, and how do household relationships limit choices? Looking at the social order within the work can be more useful than simply condemning characters through today's assumptions.

## An old program is not a current booking

The archive's film information belongs to a past program. Its screening format and introduction are reference material, not evidence of current cinema times or seats. A separate 2020 accessible-film program notice likewise documents a past screening period, not present availability.[1][4]

These dates matter with classics. A production year, an old retrospective and a current platform link can appear together. This introduction offers an institutional route to the work without inventing a viewing experience or an unchecked service condition.
