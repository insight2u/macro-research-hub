What is KEPCO? Between consolidated profit and parent-only loss

A retailer, a group with generation subsidiaries, and a public enterprise funding long-lived networks

Published 22 November 2026 · Individual source and reporting dates are specified in the text

한국어English简体中文日本語

Calling Korea Electric Power Corporation a power-plant company hides its networks and retail business. Calling it a company that collects electricity bills overlooks its generation subsidiaries and fuel procurement. Government ownership alone does not explain where public responsibilities and shareholder returns can diverge.

KEPCO combines these roles. Forecast growth in AI and data-center demand presents a sales opportunity, but also a need for network investment and financing. More electricity sales do not automatically mean better profit or cash flow. The important questions are which costs the company bears, how much it recovers through tariffs and when it must raise money for investment.

1. Start with the company map

Korea Electric Power Corporation, or KEPCO, is headquartered in Naju. Its Korean stock code is 015760. Domestic transmission, distribution, electricity purchases and retail are central to its business. Generation must be understood together with its principal subsidiaries.

The company's official history distinguishes the establishment of the integrated Korea Electric Company in 1961, its relaunch as KEPCO in January 1982, and its domestic stock listing in August 1989. Its fiscal 2025 annual report describes the separation of generation operations into six subsidiaries on April 2, 2001: Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power, Korea South-East Power, Korea Midland Power, Korea Western Power, Korea Southern Power and Korea East-West Power. The report identifies all six as wholly owned. This structure explains the relationship between a generator's sales and the parent's power-purchase costs.

Entity Main activity Analytical question
KEPCO parent Electricity purchases and retail, transmission and distribution Do sales cover purchased power, network costs and financing burdens?
Principal generation subsidiaries Nuclear, hydro and thermal generation, among other sources What are fuel costs, availability, maintenance and settlement conditions?
Consolidated group Presents KEPCO and consolidated subsidiaries as one economic group What are the combined results and financial burdens of generation, retail and overseas businesses?
Korea Power Exchange Market operation, dispatch, metering and settlement Which market rules and system constraints shape transactions?

KPX is not the same organization as KEPCO. The parent's separate results are not the same accounts as the group's consolidated results. Nor can transactions between parent and subsidiaries simply be added on both sides as external group revenue.

It is also important not to confuse KEPCO with electrical-equipment manufacturers. Its investment can be an order for a transformer or cable supplier. For KEPCO, the same project is a long-lived asset that first requires funding. The recipient of an order and the party financing it face different calculations.

2. Government ownership explains the role, not a guaranteed return

KEPCO's official shareholder table is dated December 31, 2025. At that date the government held 18.20% and Korea Development Bank 32.90%, totaling 51.10%. These are dated disclosed holdings, not a live shareholder register.

The public role has two sides. Reliable supply and network expansion are foundations of economic activity. But electricity tariffs cannot easily be determined from the company's profit alone. Household burdens, industrial competitiveness, inflation and cost recovery across generation and retail are all involved.

The annual report warns that tariffs may not reflect changes in fuel and other costs fully or promptly. A cost-adjustment mechanism is not a guarantee of immediate, complete pass-through. A market expectation that tariffs will eventually rise is not an approved tariff schedule.

Government ownership therefore does not guarantee a share price, dividend or level of profit. The policy responsibilities of a public enterprise and shareholder returns can overlap without always moving in the same direction.

3. Read the latest figures by scope and period

KEPCO's 6-K filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission on November 12, 2026 reports the third quarter and the first nine months of 2026. The figures are preliminary and prepared under K-IFRS. The company states that they have not been audited or reviewed by its independent accountants and may differ from final results.

The following converts the filing's rounded KRW billions into KRW trillions. The original rounding still applies.

Measure July–September 2026 January–September 2026
Consolidated revenue KRW26.852tn KRW73.169tn
Consolidated operating profit KRW1.644tn KRW6.557tn
Parent-only operating profit/(loss) KRW-0.099tn KRW2.024tn

The group made an operating profit in the quarter while the parent recorded an operating loss. That is not a contradiction. Consolidated accounts include consolidated subsidiaries and adjustments to internal transactions; separate accounts show the parent on its own.

A positive parent-only operating result for January–September also does not erase the third-quarter loss. A year-to-date profit and a deterioration in the latest quarter can both be true. Mixing scope and period can produce apparently contradictory headlines.

The level and direction of results must also be separated. The same filing puts 2025 third-quarter consolidated operating profit at KRW5.652tn and the first nine months at KRW11.541tn. The corresponding 2026 figures are lower. Remaining profitable and earning less than a year earlier are compatible statements.

Yonhap's November 12 report describes higher fuel costs at generation subsidiaries and increased purchases from independent producers, alongside lower electricity sales revenue, during January–September. That is an account of the reported period. It does not establish future fuel prices or the date of the next tariff adjustment.

4. Separate the accounts to understand where burdens arise

The parent purchases electricity from generation subsidiaries and independent producers and sells it to customers. Generation subsidiaries procure fuel and operate plants. The distribution of earnings and costs across these stages can therefore affect the difference between consolidated and separate results.

A generator's revenue must be read alongside fuel, maintenance, generation and settlement conditions. For the parent, the relationship between purchased-power costs and retail revenue matters. Consolidation adjusts internal transactions and brings together income and costs involving parties outside the group. This framework does not attribute the entire difference in a particular quarter to one cause; detailed disclosures are needed for that.

A practical method is to label every number: whose accounts, which period and which measure of profit? Consolidated revenue, parent-only operating profit and consolidated net income cannot be compared without those labels. Operating profit is not the bottom line after all interest and taxes.

Group earnings matter to investors, but the parent's position also matters to understanding network and retail investment and financing. A consolidated profit alone does not establish that all aspects of the parent's business have improved.

5. Profit, debt and cash are different measures

Operating profit is not the same amount of cash left over. Collection and payment timing, taxes, interest, capital expenditure and debt repayment all require separate attention. Profit measures performance over a period; liabilities are a balance at a point in time. A profitable quarter does not instantly eliminate a large liability balance.

KEPCO's fiscal 2025 annual report records about KRW18.7tn of 2025 capital expenditure relating to generation, transmission and distribution facilities. This is not transmission-grid investment alone. It should not be relabeled as actual 2026 spending or a multiyear future network plan.

The timing of spending on long-lived assets also differs from the timing of expense recognition. Operating and investing cash flows must therefore be considered alongside borrowing and repayment. Investment consumes cash now while creating future supply capability. Large expenditure is neither proof of waste nor a guarantee that all future sales will materialize.

Yonhap reported total liabilities of KRW209.6tn and borrowings of KRW132.8tn at the end of the third quarter of 2026. Total liabilities and borrowings are not identical categories and should not be added together. The important continuing question is how improved earnings translate into relief from financing burdens.

The annual report also explains currency exposure. Much of revenue and cash is in won, while fuel purchases and foreign-currency debt service require foreign currency. Lower international fuel prices do not therefore guarantee an equal reduction in won-denominated costs. Exchange rates, contracts and purchasing and settlement lags matter together.

6. AI demand becomes an opportunity only under certain conditions

More AI facilities can increase electricity sales and the need for networks. But local demand must progress from an investment plan to a site, permits, a connection agreement and operation. Announced data-center capacity is not electricity already sold.

KEPCO's May 2025 long-term transmission and substation plan addresses the gap between requested supply dates and construction lead times, delays, local acceptance and coordination with local governments. Selling more electricity requires both access to power and a network and financial structure capable of delivering it where needed.

The next results and project announcements can be examined through the following conditions.

Area Condition for improvement Burden to check
Electricity sales Planned demand becomes contracts and operating load Does sales revenue adequately cover purchase and supply costs?
Fuel, currency and generation mix Won-denominated costs and supply conditions improve Could maintenance, purchase lags or foreign-currency exposure offset the gain?
Tariffs and recovery Approved tariffs and contracts recover costs appropriately Is this actual application rather than an announcement or expectation?
Network investment Necessary facilities are completed on schedule Can permitting, local discussions, advance investment and financing be delivered?
Financial position Operating cash, investment and repayments become better balanced Are debt maturities and financing costs being overlooked in favor of quarterly profit?

This is not a prediction of the share price or a tariff-increase date. It tests what else is needed before higher demand turns into sustainable earnings and cash.

Conclusion: KEPCO carries both the cost and the timetable of Korean power

KEPCO owns generation subsidiaries, purchases and retails electricity, and must invest over long horizons in transmission and distribution. It is both a public enterprise and a listed company, responsible for national infrastructure while exposed to fuel, currency and financing costs.

The starting point is not a single optimistic or pessimistic headline. Separate consolidated from parent-only accounts, distinguish the quarter from the cumulative period, and follow operating profit with cash, investment and borrowing. How much more electricity AI creates demand for is one question. When KEPCO can connect that demand, and with what financial results, is another.
