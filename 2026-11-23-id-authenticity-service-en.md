# A genuine ID does not make every transaction safe

## Separate the names before the functions

More detailed ID checking at Kakao Pay and Toss does not mean that the announcement is about issuing a new identity document on a phone. A release posted by Korea's Financial Supervisory Service on November 22 says the two platforms can use the government's resident-registration-card authenticity service from Monday, November 23. It concerns verification infrastructure for remote customer identification in electronic financial services. This article explains the announced scope; it does not certify that every process is already working in every user's app.[1]

## What can be checked

Reporting on the interior ministry's announcement says the service can check ID authenticity including photo information, as well as the validity of details such as name, resident registration number and issue date. The existing verification relay used by financial companies is being connected to electronic financial services. Naver Pay is listed separately as a planned December expansion, not part of the other two platforms' November 23 start.[2]

This adds a verification tool to platforms' customer-identification processes. It is not advice to send a fresh photograph of an ID to an unfamiliar person or respond to someone offering to conduct the check on your behalf. The announced institutions and process should not be transformed into a reason for disclosing personal data elsewhere.

## A different question from obtaining a mobile ID

The description of the interior ministry's mobile-ID guide distinguishes issuing through an IC resident registration card from issuing through a QR process at a community service center. Those are procedures for obtaining a mobile identity document. The new announcement instead concerns the infrastructure a financial-service company can use to check a resident registration card presented during customer identification. What document a person obtains and what a company can verify are different questions.[3][1]

A mobile-ID issuance guide should therefore not be presented as a separate application process for the newly announced authenticity service. The linked video is background for distinguishing the functions. Only its description of issuance methods is used; current screens in every app and each individual's issuance eligibility have not been checked.[3]

## Keep a verified fact within its scope

ID authenticity is a useful safeguard, but it does not by itself validate an investment decision or the promises made by a counterparty. That distinction follows from what an authenticity check examines; it is not an allegation of a defect in the service. Establishing that a document is genuine and understanding a transaction remain separate tasks.

When assessing the technology, ask what is checked and by whom before accepting a broad claim of safety. The announcement establishes an expansion of verification infrastructure to two platforms, with a separately stated next step. It is not a reason to treat handing over more personal data as an automatic price of progress.
