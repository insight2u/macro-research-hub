PCE at 3.8%; tariffs still need a final sentence

Separate released inflation from anticipated policy

KOENJAZH

October US PCE inflation was reported on November 25 at 3.8% year on year and 0.3% month on month. Core inflation was 3.3% and 0.3%. We compared Yonhap and CNBC. Released figures and undecided policy must remain separate.

In line with forecasts is not the same as comfortable

Headline annual inflation matched expectations but exceeded September’s 3.6%. Yonhap reports core annual inflation rising from 3.1% to 3.3%. Meeting a forecast does not mean meeting the inflation target, and the Federal Reserve’s next decision remains a separate event.

A proposal is not an effective tariff

On November 25 USTR published determinations and proposed tariff actions for 14 economies, including Korea, for public comment. For Korea it proposes up to 15%, net of MFN rates and existing Section 301 tariffs, with product exceptions. This is not a blanket extra 15 percentage points. Other tariff measures require separate checks. Comments are due January 5, 2027; hearings are scheduled for January 25. The announcement itself is not immediate implementation.

Two channels into economic decisions

Our interpretation is that inflation raises questions about continuing financing costs, while tariff uncertainty complicates export prices and production locations. One cannot add them together to calculate Korea’s growth loss. Currency, pricing power and overseas production matter.

A weak opening is not the close

On November 25 the Dow closed at 50,182.95 (+0.33%), the S&P 500 at 7,182.78 (-0.25%) and Nasdaq at 23,585.80 (-0.62%), according to Yonhap at 06:12 KST. Nasdaq recovered most of an intraday 4.6% fall. The latest row in the Cboe daily file was November 24, 26.36, so no November 25 VIX close is asserted. NYSE is closed November 26 and closes early at 13:00 ET on November 27. Not investment advice.
