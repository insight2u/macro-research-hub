Samcheong and Brothers Home: a state deciding people could be discarded

Labels such as delinquent or vagrant cannot replace warrants and trials. Do not let the words education and welfare conceal a state that classified citizens, confined them and forced them to work. Investigation findings point beyond a few cruel officials to public power and institutions.

Independent editorial criticism · Revised 26 November 2026

한국어 · English · 日本語 · 简体中文

Unlawful detention called education

The commission’s June 7, 2022 decision identified Samcheong, which confined about 40,000 people in military units, as a large-scale human-rights violation. Warrantless detention, beatings and forced labour violated bodily liberty, warrant requirements and legality in punishment. Continued protective custody after martial law also infringed the right to trial.[1]

State responsibility behind a welfare institution

The August 23, 2022 Brothers Home decision found forced confinement and labour, abuse, sexual violence, deaths and disappearances. The state knew conditions but failed to provide relief before the 1987 prosecution investigation. The inquiry’s 1960–1992 scope requires examination of each period; it is no excuse to subtract responsibility during Chun’s rule.[2]

Leaving the gate did not restore freedom

Samcheong survivors faced surveillance, stigma, discrimination and trauma. Brothers Home survivors faced the task of restoring identities and family relationships. Recommendations for apology, redress and long-term care address lives severed by the state. Our measure is the rights taken away, not the claimed success of a crackdown.[1][2]
