Samcheong Education Corps and Brothers Home: Stigma Was Not a Warrant

What connects the two different cases is a structure of forced internment. Periods of harm and legal judgments must be read separately.

Detention Under the Name of Social Purification

On June 7, 2022, the Truth and Reconciliation Commission uncovered the truth of the Samcheong Education Corps victimization case. It judged that apprehension based on martial law proclamations, internment in military units, forced labor, and brutal treatment infringed constitutional rights. Labels such as purification education or labor service cannot change the fact that individuals were deprived of their freedom. The investigation decision also examined harm including surveillance and social stigma after discharge.

Harm That Did Not End Upon Leaving Facilities

The August 23, 2022 decision on Brothers Home likewise addresses arbitrary detention, forced labor, and brutal treatment, along with the state's management and supervision responsibility. The investigation decision also recorded identity changes, severing of family ties, disappearances, and difficulties in recovery. Due process cannot be omitted simply because the state labeled people as vagrants. Nor does the fact that 'welfare' appears in a facility's name erase rights violations inside it.

Why Not Confine Them Only to Chun Doo-hwan's Term

The period under investigation in the Brothers Home case spans both before and after Chun Doo-hwan's term in office. Therefore, writing that everything that happened at the facility occurred during a specific president's term distorts history and responsibility. Samcheong Education Corps and Brothers Home are also not cases of the same facility or the same system. What the two share is a structure in which state classification and crackdowns led to deprivation of freedom, not that every course of events was identical.

Redress for Harm Begins by Asking About Standards of Selection

The Samcheong Education Corps decision recommended expanding victim categories, redress measures, and trauma healing. The Brothers Home decision also called for apology, restoration of honor, restoration of identity and family ties, and long-term support. These were recommendations at the time of those decisions and do not mean all measures have been fulfilled as of 2026. Today's task is to verify again which systems and budgets each recommendation led to. Victims' lives must not be closed off like the ending of a narrative.
