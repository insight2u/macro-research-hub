The same Baekje, two different records

A weekend guide to reading Samguk Sagi and Samguk Yusa side by side

KOENJAZHES

A new exhibition at the Seoul Baekje Museum compares the perspectives of two historical works. Seoul says 'Samguk Sagi and Samguk Yusa: Recording Baekje' runs in Special Exhibition Rooms 1 and 2 from November 27 to February 21, 2027. Bringing old books together is both an achievement of preservation and a starting point for reading their differences.

What is on display

Seoul's notice lists the complete nine-book, 50-volume Oksan Seowon edition of Samguk Sagi, printed around 1573, alongside old editions of Samguk Yusa and restored woodblocks. Samguk Sagi is an official dynastic history; Samguk Yusa also contains myths, legends and Buddhist records. Neither can stand in for every voice of the past.

Questions to ask before an old book

Who wrote the work, and in what period? What did they preserve or omit? Is the object an original manuscript or a later printed edition? These distinctions change a museum visit. Four sections compare the books' prefaces, structures and characteristics. Asking how the record was created and transmitted offers more than simply admiring its age.

Before visiting

The published notice says admission is free and the exhibition is closed on Mondays and January 1. This is a visitor's guide based on published information, not an account of a personal visit. Check the museum's current operating notice before setting out. Respecting the authority of a historical work and asking how that authority was formed can go together.
