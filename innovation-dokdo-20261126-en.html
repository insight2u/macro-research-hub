Cho Kuk's planned return to Dokdo: facts and questions after entry refusal

한국어 · English · 日本語 · 中文 · Español

2026.11.26 · English

Cho Kuk, head of the Rebuilding Korea Party's Innovation Policy Institute, says he will visit Dokdo again after being refused entry to Japan. On November 18 he announced a trip with party members and citizens subject to sea conditions. On November 22 he proposed chartering a boat around the year-end and New Year period and taking applications. A proposed visit is not a completed visit: the material checked as of November 26 establishes the plan, not its completion.[1][2]

A planned third visit after the airport refusal

Cho arrived at Tokyo's Haneda Airport on November 17 for a joint seminar with Meiji Gakuin University and other activities, but returned without entering Japan. The next day his institute announced another Dokdo visit and plans to hold the canceled seminar on South Korea's welfare-state future in Seoul with Japanese academics early next year. Neither announcement establishes that the event or visit has already happened.[1]

The party said this would be his third Dokdo visit if it takes place. Secretary-general Jung Choonsaeng and lawmakers Kim Sunmin and Kang Kyungsook delivered a protest letter to Japan's embassy on November 18. It said that if earlier Dokdo activity influenced the decision, that would be serious, and requested the legal grounds and circumstances. A conditional allegation should not become an established causal link.[1]

Domestic law is the official explanation; Cho suspects another motive

At its November 19 briefing, South Korea's Foreign Ministry said Japan had explained that the measure followed its immigration-control and refugee-recognition law. Seoul said it provided consular assistance, conveyed regret through diplomatic channels, sought accurate facts and requested prevention of similar incidents.[3]

At a book event in Boseong on November 22, Cho said that after roughly ninety minutes of questioning he received a refusal notice citing his criminal record. He nevertheless questioned whether that was the whole reason, referring to other people's cases. His description of the written reason and his assessment of a political motive are different levels of evidence.[2]

The checked material therefore does not establish that his Dokdo visit caused the refusal. Nor does Japan's legal explanation alone establish that every question about consistency and procedure has been answered. Reporters asked the ministry when and to whom advance visa advice was actually delivered and whether exceptions had been considered, but the briefing did not supply verified records answering those points.[3]

The 2024 visit and the current case are separate

As party leader, Cho visited Dokdo on May 13,2024 and criticized the then government's Japan policy. After Japan protested, Seoul said the following day that it rejected the protest. This records that visit and the governments' responses, not proof of the actual grounds for the 2026 refusal.[4]

The video below is reporting of Cho's remarks during the 2024 visit. It is not footage of the newly proposed trip, nor independent verification of every historical and policy assertion in his speech.

Keep the territorial issue and procedural questions distinct

The proposed trip is a political response to the refusal and a proposal for public participation. Departure and landing will require checking the actual schedule, weather and sea conditions. This article has no evidence that reservations or applications are currently open.

The useful follow-up is a verifiable record: the decision document, advance-notification records, explanations confirmed through diplomacy, comparable applications of the law and an actual visit schedule. The party's suspicions should remain attributed suspicions, government explanations should retain their limits, and unfulfilled promises should remain plans.
