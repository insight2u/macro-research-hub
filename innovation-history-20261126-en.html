The Rebuilding Korea Party: from a rapid breakthrough to the test of independence

한국어 · English · 日本語 · 中文 · Español

2026.11.26 · English

The Rebuilding Korea Party's history cannot be reduced to its 2024 election breakthrough. It includes 12 proportional seats soon after founding, its founder's final conviction and loss of his seat, a leadership resignation over handling sexual misconduct, a return after a pardon, electoral defeat and a change of leadership. It asks whether a party gathered around a person's name can be judged by organizational responsibility and policy results.

March 2024: founding and registration are different dates

The party held its founding convention at Kintex in Goyang on March 3,2024 and selected Cho Kuk as leader. It emphasized judgment of the then government and prosecution reform. The legal registration date is different: the National Election Commission's central-party registration notice is dated March 7.[1][2]

The party's official introduction records 24.25 percent of the proportional party vote and 12 elected members in the April 2024 general election. Gyeonggi's election authority also recorded 24.25 percent and 12 seats in material published in March 2025. This is an election result, not a later opinion-poll rating or a measure of its current local organization.[3][4]

Cho's name enabled rapid mobilization. That also posed an early question: what sustains the organization when its founder faces political or legal problems?

From opposition to policy design

On August 20,2024 the party launched its Innovation Policy Institute, then with Cho as board chair and Seo Wangjin as director. It proposed research on constitutional change toward a Seventh Republic and a state more actively guaranteeing social rights, including education, work, care, health and housing.[5]

An institute's establishment and a declaration of goals are verified activities. How far they produced legislation, budgets, administration or better lives requires separate examination. Opposition messages and seat counts do not alone demonstrate implementation.

December 2024: the founder's absence

On December 12,2024 the Supreme Court upheld Cho's two-year sentence in cases concerning admissions misconduct and interference with an inspection. Some charges ended in acquittal, but the entire case did not. He lost his seat and the party operated under acting leader Kim Sunmin.[6][7]

In August 2025, the government included him in its Liberation Day special pardon and restoration of rights, effective August 15. This was not an acquittal on retrial and did not automatically restore the lost parliamentary seat. Final judgment, pardon and current position must remain separate facts.[8]

September 2025: a crisis of responsibility toward victims

On September 7,2025 Kim and the party's supreme council resigned over their response to internal sexual misconduct. Kim apologized to victims and acknowledged delays without adequate organization or manuals. Victims raised concerns about passive handling and secondary harm, while a party official denied intentional delay.[7]

This cannot be recorded merely as a change of leadership. Safety, independent investigation and action, prevention of further harm and receptiveness to criticism also matter. Resignation does not prove that recovery was completed.

On September 11 the party selected Cho, then institute director, to head its emergency committee. The party's governing body called for prompt victim protection and support and organizational reform. On November 23 a party convention elected him leader again.[9][10]

2026: defeat and the Shin Jangsik leadership

On June 4,2026 Cho announced his resignation as leader, taking responsibility for the June 3 election results. He also lost the Pyeongtaek-eul parliamentary rerun. Pardon and a return to leadership did not automatically mean renewed electoral endorsement or a parliamentary return.[11]

On July 25 the party elected Shin Jangsik with 96.7 percent approval. Shin emphasized reform and building the party's own base as a condition for effective cooperation. The election authority's current party register also identifies him as leader.[12][13]

Cho must therefore not be labeled current party leader, nor former acting or floor leaders copied into current positions. He heads the Innovation Policy Institute; on November 20 the party announced his appointment to chair its Vision Lab for future direction and reform.[14]

The next chapter needs implementation records

The party said full Vision Lab operation would follow a November 29,2026 event marking 1,000 days since founding. As of November 26, this is a future plan. Creating a body and demonstrating its results are different claims.[14]

The next history will require more than forceful criticism. It should track the laws and budgets behind prosecution reform and social rights, procedures for internal harm and criticism, and a lasting local and membership base beyond the founder. Independence remains a goal while it is a convention slogan. It becomes a result when reflected in the organization and citizens' lives.
