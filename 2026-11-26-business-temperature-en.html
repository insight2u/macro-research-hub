Factories cool, domestic suppliers hold up

Inside the November business-sentiment average of 103.5

KOENJAZH

The Bank of Korea reported manufacturing CBSI of 104.8, down 0.8 points, and nonmanufacturing CBSI of 102.5, down 0.7. Its Economic Sentiment Index fell 0.9 points to 99.9. One number cannot describe every business.

An index is not a growth rate

All-industry CBSI fell 0.7 points to 103.5. CBS reported that export-oriented firms lost 2.7 points while domestic-oriented firms gained 1.5 points to 105.3. The benchmark of 100 is based on a long-run average. A reading of 103.5 does not mean output or sales rose 3.5%.

Domestic firms are not the same as household spending

The report links stronger domestic sentiment to semiconductor and auto supply chains and lower inventories. A company selling components to another Korean company is a domestic supplier. Its improvement does not establish a boom for shops, restaurants or households.

Questions behind the average

Our reading is to check customers, inventories and the separate effects of exchange rates on costs and receipts. Diverging export and domestic sentiment makes orders and cash collection worth examining. This is editorial analysis, not a model forecasting next month’s earnings.

Keep the dates attached

The morning check will look for later releases. November survey readings are not live November 26 data. The November 25 News Factory programme discusses the preceding day’s KOSPI rebound and the pace of capital-market reform. Share prices, sentiment and institutions run on different clocks. The programme is not used as primary statistical data, and its previous close is not relabeled as November 25’s close.
