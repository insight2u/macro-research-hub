# 'Growth for all' begins with how export gains travel

First industry-and-company working-group meeting, November 23, 2026 · Policy

## A meeting is not a measured result

The industry and small-business ministries said they held the first meeting of the industry-and-company inequality working group on November 23. Their question is whether export gains spread through the wider industrial ecosystem. The meeting is verified; successful narrowing of disparities is not.[1]

The release says semiconductor-led exports have improved macroeconomic indicators without sufficiently spreading benefits across industries and companies. That is the government's assessment. It is not a statistical finding that every small firm's sales fell or every large company's profits rose. Those conclusions need data broken down by industry and business size.

## Participation needs contract terms

Proposed areas for examination include greater small-business participation in major national projects and stronger cooperation between larger and smaller businesses. The release does not set individual eligibility, allocated orders or contract values. Participation and financial support are not automatic entitlements.[1]

A useful evaluation goes beyond counting entrants: under what conditions do firms participate, when are they paid for technology, and do they retain capabilities for future competition? These are editorial tests, not invented details of an unpublished program.

## Three timetables should not become one

The group plans weekly meetings to gather views and develop measures. The release distinguishes immediate action, institutional changes or implementation during the following year, and issues needing public discussion. Agreed priorities are intended for the 2027 economic-growth strategy.[1]

Items mentioned at a first meeting should not all be called settled policy. Later announcements must show which ideas enter which category and when they will be implemented. Otherwise both responsibility and timing become unclear.

Reducing disparities concerns the channels through which growth reaches businesses. A worthwhile goal still cannot replace measured results. Project design and implementation reports, rather than a meeting's title, will show whether the channels change.

A May forecast is not November's realized result, nor is this footage of the task force. Growth persistence and distribution across companies remain different questions.
