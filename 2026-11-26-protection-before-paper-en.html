Safety before a statement declining punishment

A domestic-violence reform promise and the conditions for leaving

KOENJAZH

President Lee Jae-myung said on November 25 that the law would be changed so domestic-violence offenders could be held accountable even when a victim did not want punishment. This is a promise of reform, not proof that a new rule is already operating.

Was the choice free?

SBS reports concerns that fear of retaliation can lead victims to submit statements against their true wishes. YTN’s October 25 coverage also examined protection gaps. These are background reports from different dates, not a new judicial finding about an individual case.

Between a legal sentence and daily life

Changing prosecution conditions and making everyday life safe require separate checks. Our perspective connects housing, income, childcare and protection from contact. A promised amendment does not mean a victim can immediately move or become financially independent.

How to test a public promise

The next questions concern the final bill’s covered offences and procedures, enforcement responsibility and resources. Do not assume every domestic-violence offence has identical prosecution conditions. This article is not individual legal advice or a personal safety plan.

Reporting has responsibilities too

A withdrawn complaint does not establish that violence was minor. Examine the conditions under which choices were made before blaming the person choosing. The morning recheck will look for later legislation or policy announcements without counting promises as delivered protection.
