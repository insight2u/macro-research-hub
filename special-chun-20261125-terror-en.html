KAL Flight 858 and Aung San: Do Not Mix Perpetration and Political Exploitation

Official determinations on perpetrators of terrorism and criticism of investigations or political use are separate questions.

To Compare, First Treat Them as Different Incidents

KAL Flight 858 and the Aung San bombing are neither the same incident nor the same investigation. The reason for addressing both incidents in one article is to read separately the questions of determining perpetrators, verifying investigations, and domestic political use. Not shifting criticism over one issue into proof of another issue is the standard for comparison.

KAL 858: Record the Investigation Conclusion Accurately

The NIS Truth Commission investigation results carried in a Korea Policy Briefing on October 24, 2007, introduce the conclusion that KAL Flight 858 was an incident carried out by North Korean agents. They also convey the judgment that there were no clues supporting suspicions of planned fabrication or prior knowledge by the Agency for National Security Planning. Criticism of investigative problems or political use is a subject of review separate from that conclusion on perpetration. The fact that criticism existed cannot be written as proof of a staged plot.

Aung San: Center the Record of Sacrifice

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs records that during President Chun Doo-hwan's visit to Burma in 1983, 17 diplomatic envoys and attendants were martyred in a North Korean bomb attack. This figure is the scope of diplomatic envoys and attendants honored in that Ministry of Foreign Affairs material. It is not expanded into a total casualty figure including victims of other nationalities. Connecting the president's movements or circumstances of survival as evidence of conspiracy requires separate investigative and diplomatic records.

Even Criticism of a Regime Has Evidentiary Boundaries

How power explained and used security incidents is an important question. However, the claim that a regime gained political benefit and the claim that it carried out an incident require different proof. Erasing this difference turns judgments about victims' deaths and actual perpetration into tools of political strife. Even to maintain strong criticism, unverified claims of a staged plot should not be added to an article.

Sources and scope of verification

Court judgments, public investigation decisions and institutional records have different authors and dates. Current unpaid balances, individual assets and subsequent changes in law are outside the scope of this verification. This is not a full-book or complete-film review.
