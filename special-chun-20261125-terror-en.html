Do not use the tragedy of terrorism to absolve dictatorship

Condemning North Korean terrorism does not require silence about state violence under Chun. Protecting people from external threats and refraining from torture are not bargaining chips. Victims are not political assets to establish a regime’s legitimacy.

Independent editorial criticism · Revised 26 November 2026

한국어 · English · 日本語 · 简体中文

Keep responsibility for the attacks and the losses clear

The Academy of Korean Studies attributes the November 29, 1987 destruction of Korean Air Flight 858 to North Korean agents Kim Hyon-hui and Kim Sung-il and records the deaths of all 115 aboard. Segye Ilbo reported that 17 South Korean delegation members died in the October 9, 1983 Rangoon bombing. Do not turn these verified losses into instruments for unrelated suspicions.[1][2]

Families’ right to know comes before propaganda

At a March 16, 2022 meeting with families, the Foreign Ministry described attempts to examine an object believed to be wreckage, talks with Myanmar and delays in dispatching investigators amid deteriorating local conditions. That is a 2022 account, not the state of the search in 2026. Families deserve continuing, verifiable explanations rather than a one-off political slogan.[3]

Security cannot erase crimes at home

An interpretation of electoral effects is not the same proposition as a claim that the government created the attack. This article does not present that unproven claim as fact. Nor is there reason to suspend criticism of rebellion, insurrection, torture or press control. Our judgment is that crimes by an external actor do not cancel crimes by domestic power.[4]
