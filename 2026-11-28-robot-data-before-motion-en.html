The next robot bottleneck is the record, not the arm

A proposed Korean standard asks what makes robot-learning data shareable

KOENJAZHES

Robots cannot easily share experience if the same movement is recorded in incompatible ways. On November 27, the Korean Agency for Technology and Standards announced a proposed KS standard for robot-manipulation learning datasets, with a 60-day comment period. Before asking what a robot can do, the proposal asks how to record what it learned.

Collecting video is not enough

The agency says the proposal covers requirements for planning, collection, preprocessing and storage, alongside a structure for recording the environment, task and process. It connects sensor information such as video and sound with the robot's state and actions. A video of a moving hand alone cannot adequately show which command produced which outcome.

A standard is not a performance guarantee

A common structure could lower barriers to sharing and reusing data. It does not make the data accurate or ensure identical performance on another robot. The agency says it also plans separate standards for quality verification and evaluation, including completeness and accuracy. Compatibility of format and quality of content are different tasks.

Still a proposal, with competitiveness to follow

The government plans to collect comments and pursue adoption in the first half of next year. This is not an already effective obligation or a finalized certification scheme. Companies still need to weigh the cost of producing compliant data against the benefits of reuse. Robot competition does not end with a demonstration. Productivity requires records that let machines learn again, including from failed movements.
