Growth is rising, but the money left to spend fell

Reading income, spending and disposable income separately in Korea's household survey

KOENJAZHES

A stronger economy and tighter household budgets can coexist. In Korea's third-quarter 2026 Household Income and Expenditure Survey, average monthly household income rose 0.8% from a year earlier to 5.48 million won. Disposable income, however, fell 1.7% to 4.306 million won. The first figure suggests recovery. The second reveals a squeeze.

Not all income reaches the shopping basket

The survey summary published by KDI puts household expenditure at 4.204 million won, up 5.1%. Consumption expenditure rose 2.9% to 3.03 million won, while non-consumption expenditure increased 11.0% to 1.174 million won. To assess spending power, we need to see what remains after taxes, social insurance and other non-consumption outlays. A small increase in total income does not, on its own, mean life has become easier.

Put the distribution beside the average

Employment income rose 3.6% to 3.487 million won, but business income fell 1.4% to 975,000 won and transfer income declined 8.5% to 845,000 won. Average income in the bottom quintile fell 2.7% to 1.277 million won; the top quintile's rose 1.4% to 11.746 million won. No single real person represents the average household. The quarter felt different depending on the source of income.

Connecting forecasts to the household budget

The Bank of Korea's growth outlook forecasts production, whereas the household survey observes household income and expenditure. Their periods and populations differ, so neither should be used to dismiss the other. The next questions are how widely growth reaches jobs and incomes, how non-consumption costs change, and how outcomes differ across income groups. An increase in a nominal amount is not automatically an increase in purchasing power.
