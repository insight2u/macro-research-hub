## An old song can have a recent upload

A platform's upload date is easy to notice first. It is not necessarily a work's birth date. Lee Moon-sae's official album page lists "Red Sunset" as track seven on his 1988 fifth album and credits Lee Young-hoon for both lyrics and composition. The linked recording is a distributor-supplied YouTube Topic track. Its 2023 upload is not evidence of a new song release.[1][2]

This is not a review of a newly attended concert. It compares the official album information with the recording's distribution description, asking what information accompanies older popular music on today's platforms. It links the supplied track rather than reproducing lyrics or downloading and republishing the audio.

## Put a song back inside its album

The official fifth-album listing also includes "A Poem for a Poem," "Gwanghwamun Love Song" and "When Standing Under the Shade of a Roadside Tree." Encountering a track in album order is different from hearing it alone in a playlist. Sharing an album, however, does not prove that every song has one theme or mood.[1]

Careful listening can begin by distinguishing creative roles. The song circulates under Lee Moon-sae's name, but the official information explicitly credits Lee Young-hoon's lyrics and composition. Separating performance from authorship prevents the work's history from collapsing into one name. The introductory page does not establish current rights ownership or royalty allocation.

## Keep conflicting dates visible

The official album page gives September 15, 1988 as its production date. The distributor's recording description instead gives October 1, 1988 as the release date. This article does not silently correct one with the other: production and distribution-release metadata differ. The common supported description is a track on the 1988 fifth album.[1][2]

The video is an audio track on Lee Moon Sae - Topic. Its description names Mound Media as provider and includes 예전미디어 and VOSTOK licensing credits. It is not described here as concert footage or an official music video. Accessing a rights-supplied recording differs from watching a staged performance.[2]

## Revisiting is not proof of a current hit

Returning to a familiar song does not establish a chart resurgence or new commercial success. This article has not checked current rankings or sales and does not use those labels. Nor does it assume everyone in a generation shares the same memory.

Before reducing older music to "nostalgia," this introduction puts the original album, creators and present listening route alongside one another. A well-known title still benefits from the small effort of distinguishing a source's dates and format.
