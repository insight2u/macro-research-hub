A grid law can save time. It cannot replace trust.

Consultation and compensation are part of the project, not external distractions

Published 22 November 2026 · Policy announcement: 20 November

한국어English简体中文日本語

Do not describe a 2025 law as newly enacted in 2026

The national backbone grid expansion law was promulgated in March 2025 and first took effect on September 26 that year. Later amendments appear in the current text. Cabinet approval, initial commencement and current provisions are distinct records. A future timetable in a 2025 release is not a process starting today.[6][7]

The law has residents as well as a committee

Article 8 of the current text concerns the national grid expansion committee. Article 12 covers public notice, inspection and residents' opinions. Chapter 5 provides special rules on support and compensation. National needs and regional burdens sit within the same legal structure. 'Faster' is not equivalent to 'all procedures removed'.[6]

The operator's plan includes social acceptance

KEPCO's May 2025 plan lists advance information sessions, resident-friendly substations and local-government cooperation among construction tasks. This is not proof that every measure has been completed everywhere. It does show that the operator's own planning links acceptance and delivery schedules.[5]

The fastest route is not to hide the burden

Places hosting lines may carry burdens different from the benefits received by electricity-using industries. Early discussion of alternative routes, compensation, local benefits and disclosure may reduce later conflict risk. That is our project-risk interpretation, not a claim that money settles every disagreement. Materials, construction and testing remain after procedural improvements. Legal and physical calendars need to be read together.
