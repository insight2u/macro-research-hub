Record Chun’s crimes against citizens, not a balance sheet of his rule

When economic growth is weighed against dictatorship, citizens who were killed or injured become costs on a ledger. The starting point must be different: Chun Doo-hwan was a ruler whose responsibility for military rebellion, insurrection, murder for the purpose of insurrection and bribery was established by conviction. Economic performance never licensed the destruction of life or constitutional rights.

Independent editorial criticism · Revised 26 November 2026

한국어 · English · 日本語 · 简体中文

Taking power does not erase the crime

In case 96Do3376 on April 17, 1997, the Supreme Court held that those who seized power through military rebellion and insurrection remained punishable even after changing the constitution and governing. Chun’s life sentence and KRW220.5 billion forfeiture order became final. Calling a coup successful describes its outcome, not its legality.[1][2]

A state that used violence and blocked the truth

Truth and Reconciliation Commission findings record unlawful confinement and forced labour under Samcheong, and state responsibility for involvement in abuses at Brothers Home and failure to provide relief. Press control and the cover-up of Park Jong-cheol’s death also denied citizens knowledge of state violence. These distinct events need not be collapsed into an invented single direct order. The documented actions themselves defeat an apology for dictatorship.[3][4][5][6]

A pardon is not the victims’ forgiveness

Chun received a special pardon on December 22, 1997. Constitutional Court case 97HeonMa404 dismissed a challenge because the citizen applicants lacked a direct, personal infringement of rights. It did not validate the dictatorship. A political decision to remit punishment cannot erase criminal responsibility or public memory.[7]

The conclusion must be repair, not the ruler’s reputation

Our editorial judgment is clear: move the focus from the ruler’s achievements and excuses to victims’ rights. Restoring reputations, compensation, care, disclosure and prevention are ways to place the state under its citizens. Accurate criticism of dictatorship is a minimum duty of democracy.
