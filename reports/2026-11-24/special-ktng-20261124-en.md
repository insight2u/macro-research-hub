# Turning a monopoly legacy into competitiveness: how KT&G and JungKwanJang are building their next markets

DailyShot Macro Research | Special edition, November 24, 2026
Sources checked: November 22, 2026, KST

I have worked at KT&G's Management Research Institute and the Market Research Department of its Marketing Headquarters.

Tobacco and red ginseng. A product that damages health and one people seek for health management share a corporate history. That contrast alone misses an important part of KT&G's story: how an organization once responsible for state-controlled production and sales became a private-sector company, competed with international tobacco companies, developed new devices and entered global health-food markets.

This feature focuses on that ability to change. It examines KT&G's overseas cigarette business, the growth of lil under Lim Wang-seop, Bang Kyung-man's international experience, and the next challenges for KGC and JungKwanJang alongside domestic and international competitors. Corporate achievements deserve assessment, but tobacco's health harms must not disappear behind a success story.

## 1. From state-sold tobacco to a company competing worldwide

KT&G traces its origins to Sunhwaguk, a state tobacco manufacturing office established in Joseon in 1883. This does not mean today's legal entity has existed in the same form since then. The organizational history runs through the post-liberation Monopoly Bureau and Monopoly Administration, Korea Monopoly Corporation in 1987, Korea Tobacco & Ginseng Corporation in 1989, and privatization and a name change in 2002.[1]

Tobacco connected personal consumption with public finance early on. During the National Debt Repayment Movement of 1907, a proposal called on people to stop smoking and contribute the savings toward repaying national debt. Today's tobacco taxes and regulation still reflect a long relationship between the state and consumers.[2]

International operations stand out among the achievements since privatization. KT&G reported approximately KRW 6.5796 trillion in consolidated revenue and KRW 1.35 trillion in operating profit for 2025. Overseas cigarette revenue reached KRW 1.8775 trillion, or 54.1% of total cigarette revenue. The company described this as the first year overseas cigarette revenue exceeded domestic cigarette revenue.[3]

The denominator of 54.1% is cigarette revenue, not total group revenue. Keeping that scope intact does not diminish the achievement: a company with roots in a state monopoly expanded a major source of revenue in competitive overseas markets.

## 2. Lim Wang-seop's execution: turning doubts into results with lil

The growth of lil involves products, technology and people. KT&G launched lil in 2017. According to Hankyung Business's New Year interview published in 2023, it overtook Philip Morris Korea to rank first in Korea's heated-tobacco market in the first quarter of 2022. That is an achievement at that time, about five years after a late entrant's launch, not a claim about today's ranking.[4]

Lim Wang-seop, then head of the NGP Business Division, led that development. The interview describes a development organization growing from eight people to around 90, and a process of understanding the market as a technology-based platform competition before bringing products to market. Such results lay behind the publication's descriptions of him as having a "Midas touch" and being a "trusted hand."[4]

The lil AIBLE developed under his leadership was selected among Clarivate's global Top 100 New Brands in 2022. KT&G also broadened its overseas reach through product supply and sales cooperation with PMI. On Invention Day in 2024, Lim received a commendation from the Minister of Trade, Industry and Energy on the company's behalf. The recipient of the corporate award was KT&G, not Lim personally.[4][5][6]

The execution that turned an idea into a product and a small team into development capability deserves credit. Lim moved to KGC as president and CEO in March 2025, taking charge of domestic operations. KGC currently has separately responsible CEOs: Lim for domestic business and Ahn Bin for overseas business. The question is how the brand and organizational growth experience demonstrated with lil will help JungKwanJang reach its next customers.[7]

## 3. Bang Kyung-man's strength: applying overseas experience to the next growth phase

KT&G's current president and CEO is Bang Kyung-man. He joined in 1998, held major positions including head of branding, global operations and strategic planning, and became CEO in March 2024.[8]

The company says that during his tenure leading global operations, the number of markets expanded from around 40 countries to around 100, and overseas cigarette revenue exceeded KRW 1 trillion for the first time. Direct experience of international expansion is a strength.[8]

KT&G's three core businesses are overseas cigarettes, NGP and health-functional foods. Identifying what is sold and where is more accurate than treating "global" as a separate product business. Strong group results must not, however, be attributed to the health-functional-food business alone. Each business needs its own scorecard.

## 4. JungKwanJang's global ranking and changing Korean consumption belong in the same picture

KGC describes Samjeonggwa (삼정과), established within Naejangwon of the Korean Empire's Royal Household Department (궁내부 내장원) in 1899, as its historical foundation. That does not mean state management of ginseng first began that year. The current company was established in 1999 when the red-ginseng business was separated from Korea Tobacco & Ginseng Corporation.[7][9]

The brand also has international achievements. In its August 2026 announcement, KGC said JungKwanJang ranked first for the 12th consecutive year in Euromonitor's global retail market for ginseng and red-ginseng brands. The company cited a 44.3% share based on 2024 sales. This concerns the retail market for ginseng and red-ginseng brands, not the entire health-functional-food market.[10]

In Korea, competitors include Nonghyup Red Ginseng's Hansamin and Dongwon F&B's Cheonjiin. A wider competitive set includes companies selling other ingredients and product categories, such as Chong Kun Dang Health (종근당건강), Korea Eundan (고려은단) and Amway's Nutrilite, all seeking spending from the same consumers. Nutrilite is a vitamin, mineral and dietary-supplement brand. Ranking among red-ginseng brands and competitiveness across the whole nutritional-supplement market are different measures.[7][11][12]

Consumption is changing too. Reporting on a Korea Health Functional Food Association survey put red ginseng's share of purchases at 25.9% in 2021 and 16% in 2025. The same survey put Korea's 2025 health-functional-food market at approximately KRW 5.9626 trillion. That number must not be presented as the 2026 market size.[11][13]

KGC's strengths include brand trust, production infrastructure and overseas distribution. Reporting on the association's survey cited Korea Ginseng Corporation as the leading producer in 2024, with health-functional-food production valued at KRW 672.3 billion. Production value is not consumer purchases or company revenue. The company says it exports JungKwanJang to around 40 countries and has expanded sales channels through Sprouts, Costco and Amazon in the United States.[10][11]

The next task is clear: preserve trust in red ginseng while reaching more customers through stick-format products, non-red-ginseng ingredients and new sales channels. That is why Lim's domestic operations and Ahn's overseas operations need to be assessed together.

## 5. Tobacco and cannabis: a second war, or a combination of capital?

The future of global tobacco companies is more complicated than survival in a declining industry. PMI reported that 41.5% of its more than US$40 billion in 2025 net revenue came from businesses it classifies as smoke-free. IQOS, ZYN nicotine pouches and VEEV vaping products belong to that portfolio. Non-combustible does not mean harmless.[14]

BAT operates glo and Vuse, while JT is expanding Ploom. JT's 2028 break-even target concerns market-level brand contribution for its related new-product business. It must not be read as the group's overall break-even target. Imperial is developing blu, Pulze and Zone while also describing, in its 2025 materials, withdrawals from vaping in some markets because of regulation and taxation. Companies are preparing for the future in different ways.[15][16][17]

Competition is not the only relationship with cannabis. Canada legalized recreational cannabis on October 17, 2018. That does not mean the same law applies across North America.[18]

On November 3, 2026, Organigram and Aurora announced an acquisition agreement based on a fully diluted equity value of approximately C$561 million. An indirect BAT subsidiary is also supporting the transaction with an investment of approximately C$187.5 million. This is an agreement subject to conditions including shareholder, court and regulatory approvals, not a completed acquisition. The companies' stated completion target is January 2027.[19]

The case supports an interpretation that tobacco capital views cannabis as an adjacent business in which technology and distribution experience can be combined, rather than solely as a competitor. One transaction does not, however, demonstrate that cannabis will replace tobacco or that every drug market will grow. Competition between legal and illegal markets, consumer demand, taxes and regulation still matter.

## Assess achievements with numbers, and the future with its conditions

The next chapter for KT&G and KGC depends on competitiveness rather than historical monopoly rights. Experience in overseas growth, the organization and technology behind lil, and JungKwanJang's trust and distribution network are concrete foundations for that competitiveness. They also explain why Lim's execution and Bang's international experience deserve a favorable assessment.

The unit of assessment must remain clear. Do not mix group and business revenue, production and consumer purchases, historical and current rankings, or an acquisition agreement and a completed transaction. Praising a company properly begins with describing its achievements accurately.

---

Smoking and nicotine use carry health risks and addiction. Health-functional foods are not treatments for disease. Purchasing and smoking recreational cannabis are prohibited in South Korea, and Korean nationals may also face punishment for related conduct abroad. This is industry and company analysis, not a recommendation to use tobacco or cannabis, buy products or invest.[20][21]
