The winter rates passed 30%, and house prices: the size of the 1997-98 and 2008 records

Rates, house prices and builders during the foreign-exchange and financial crises, read again by date

KOENJAZHES

On November 26, when the base rate rose to 3.25%, more people went looking for records of past rate shocks. In late 1997 corporate bond yields passed 30% and the next year national house prices fell 12.4%. The 2008 financial crisis was not the same shock, and prices absorbed it slowly. This article sets out the differences between the two periods and today using confirmed records.

December 1997: market rates at 30%

On December 23, 1997 the won passed 2,000 per dollar and some rates approached the legal ceiling of 40% (JoongAng Ilbo). On the 26th the three-year corporate bond yield closed at 27.00%, down 2.0 points (Maeil Business), and on the 30th it passed 30% again (Hankook Ilbo). Commercial paper and call rates also stayed high. These are market rates, not what households paid on loans. A July 1998 Hankook Ilbo report put lending rates then in the 16% range.

1998: house prices down 12.4%

KB Financial recorded that national house sale prices fell 12.4% in 1998. A report says jeonse prices fell 18% (Dailian). Housing construction volume dropped 49% from the year before in 1998, and 426 of 3,600 builders were declared bankrupt (Korea Economic Daily Market Insight). Prices stayed weak through 2001 and recovered from 2002, about four years in all.

2008: a slow adjustment

The shock of the 2008 financial crisis reached house prices gradually. Seoul price growth fell from 5% in 2008 to 2.7% in 2009 and turned to a 1.2% fall in 2010. With oversupply on top, Seoul prices went through a long adjustment until 2013 (Korea Economic Daily Market Insight). If 1998 was a one-year drop, 2008 was a slowdown spread over several years.

Comparison with 2023

According to KB Financial, national house sale prices fell 4.6% in 2023, the largest fall since 1998 (12.4%). They had risen 15.0% in 2021, and the direction changed within two years. Even in the same direction, the size is a little over one third of 1998.

Today's numbers and how to read them

The Bank of Korea base rate became 3.25% on November 26, 2026. The average bank mortgage rate in September was 4.88%. These are not levels that compare directly with 1997-98. What the past records show is the order rather than the level: when rates rose, deals fell, supply fell and builders were shaken first. The structure and rules of today's housing market differ from then. This article is not investment advice and does not forecast the direction of prices.

Scope of verification

Figures in this article were checked against public agency statistics and dated press reports. Figures taken from an article are as reported there and may need further checking against the original data. Interpretations give grounds and limits together and are not settled forecasts.
