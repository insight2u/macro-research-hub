# Back to the 4,600s: the day a one-day rebound was erased in a day

## The numbers of the November 25 KOSPI plunge, and one check still due tonight

November 25, 2026 | Economy Thermometer (Macro & Markets)
Lead image: an editorial illustration made with the DailyShot family characters. Not an actual market photograph.

![DailyShot family character illustration](https://insight2u.github.io/macro-research-hub/images/family/family-01-still.png)

On the 25th, the KOSPI closed at 4,697.51, down 308.52 points (6.16%) from the previous session. On a closing basis, that is the lowest level in about ten months, since January 13 (4,692.64).[1] The index gave back in a single day the entire 297.49-point (6.32%) rally posted the day before on news of CEO Jensen Huang's return to management.

## The numbers today's market left behind

The index opened at 4,809.00, down 3.94%, and a sell sidecar (a temporary halt on program sell orders) was triggered eight minutes into the session. Losses deepened in the afternoon, touching 4,672.55 intraday. That is 50.22% below the intraday peak of 9,385.59 recorded on June 19.[1]

By investor type, foreigners net sold 1.8994 trillion won on the main bourse, the largest amount since November 6 (2.6892 trillion won), rising to 2.2817 trillion won when the alternative exchange Nextrade is included. On the same day, retail investors net bought 1.4232 trillion won, and "other corporations," which reflect share buybacks, net bought 476.9 billion won.[1]

Samsung Electronics fell 9.87% to 170,700 won, its lowest since March 31 (167,200 won), and its market capitalization dropped below the 1,000-trillion-won line. SK Hynix plunged 11.1%.[1][2] As of 3:30 p.m., the won-dollar exchange rate stood at 1,343.2 won, up 0.8 won.[1]

## What changed in a day, and what did not

The immediate backdrop was overnight weakness in U.S. technology shares. On the 24th (local time), Nvidia fell 5.86% and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 7.88%.[3] The fact that yesterday's gain and today's loss are similar in size suggests that it was not earnings forecasts that changed in a day, but rather that investor sentiment read the same news differently.

The continuity of the selling is also worth checking. Between November 1 and 24, foreigners net sold 13.9112 trillion won on the KOSPI, and over that period the index fell 12.6%, from 5,730.74 at the end of October to 5,006.03 on the 24th.[3] Today's plunge sits on the extension of that trend. These figures are press tallies; this research did not independently recalculate them from exchange records.

Authorities said on the 22nd that they had begun preparations to activate the stock market stabilization fund, the first such preparation in 18 years since 2008, with more than 10.7 trillion won of funds being discussed.[4][5] Preparation is not the same as actual deployment, and Money Today read the move as a "signal" to the market rather than an injection of funds.[5]

## The weight of a 50% fall from the peak

According to Mirae Asset Securities, since 2000 the KOSPI has fallen 50% or more from a peak only three times before this year: the dot-com bust, the 2003 credit card crisis, and the 2008 global financial crisis.[1] The comparison does not mean this decline will follow the same path as those three episodes. Their causes and recoveries differed, and where this decline ultimately bottoms cannot yet be confirmed.

## One check remains tonight

The market's next observation point is the U.S. October Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index, due at 10:30 p.m. Korea time tonight. The market expects a 3.8% year-on-year rise and sees the print feeding into the Fed's December rate decision.[3] Depending on how the actual figure differs from expectations, tomorrow's open in Asian markets could look different. This article is a summary of market conditions, not a recommendation to buy or sell.
