# This week's first market number is on the clock

## A closure is market information too

Monday's market coverage usually begins with prices. This week, start with the hours during which trading is available. Japan Exchange Group lists Monday, November 23 as Labor Thanksgiving Day, a market holiday. The NYSE is closed for Thanksgiving on Thursday, November 26 and closes its core equity session early, at 1 p.m. Eastern Time, on Friday, November 27. This is a calendar explanation checked on November 23, not a forecast or a trading recommendation.[1][3]

## Japan has an exception to the futures exception

Readers familiar with Japanese derivatives trading on holidays need a second check. JPX's November 20 notice says there will be no derivatives holiday trading on November 23 either. It cites group-wide disaster-recovery testing scheduled for November 21 through 23. A date-specific exception takes priority over a general description of the system.[2]

The linked futures introduction on the JPX channel also explains near the beginning that holiday trading does not take place on every holiday and that investors should check with their securities firm. Only that operational warning is used here. Its historical charts and trading examples are not evidence for this week's outlook, nor is the link an invitation to trade futures.[4]

## Friday afternoon in New York is Saturday morning in Seoul

The NYSE's November 27 core equity close is 1 p.m. Eastern. Converting New York's time zone on that date to Seoul gives 3 a.m. on Saturday, November 28. The usual 9:30 a.m. Eastern core opening corresponds to 11:30 p.m. in Korea on Friday, November 27. Reading an American Friday as the early hours of Friday in Korea puts the session on the wrong day.[3]

These times concern NYSE core equity trading. The exchange separately lists a 1:15 p.m. early close for eligible options and a 5 p.m. close for late sessions on specified markets. None should be silently turned into the order-entry hours offered by a particular Korean broker, or into a universal closing time for all American products.[3]

## Separate the questions before placing an order

Check the exchange and product first, then the local date and time zone, and finally the broker's rules for orders and cancellations. Core and extended sessions can have different conditions even when both are called US stock trading. A market-holiday table is not a promise about every settlement or foreign-exchange service. It cannot tell you which way prices will move, but it can prevent a decision from being trapped outside available trading hours.

This edition does not fill the calendar with an unannounced policy outcome or an unchecked price. The information established here is the exchanges' published schedule. Keeping operational hours separate from a price forecast is the starting point for using it.
