# Oysters and strawberries face another quality test at the border

Field-visit announcement, November 23, 2026 · International economy

## Competitiveness after production

The Industry Ministry said its trade chief visited an oyster producer-exporter in Tongyeong and strawberry farms and a distribution center in Jinju on November 23. Discussions went beyond overseas demand. Oysters raised questions about non-tariff barriers and new markets; strawberries brought quarantine, customs and logistics into focus.[1]

This is a report about two products' export operations, not a forecast for a particular country or the entry into force of a trade agreement. Destination-specific quarantine rules, tariffs and current clearance eligibility require separate checks before a contract.

## Different bottlenecks for fresh products

For oysters, the release highlights capacity to address non-tariff barriers and diversify markets. Lower tariffs do not automatically remove health and quarantine requirements. The document does not establish that a particular market has relaxed its rules or issued a new approval.[1]

For strawberries, maintaining quality through production and distribution is central. The ministry emphasizes freshness, reliable logistics and transport costs. Overseas interest and consistent saleable quality on arrival are different achievements. The export contract also depends on time beyond the farm gate.[1]

## Read the period and scope before the growth rate

The ministry cites January–October oyster exports of about USD 70 million, up 8.4% from a year earlier, and strawberry exports of USD 61.1 million, up 15.3%. It identifies agricultural export information and trade-statistics sources. These are product-level period figures, not revenue growth at the particular businesses visited.[1]

An increase in value is not necessarily the same increase in volume. Unit prices, product mix and destinations can all matter. The release does not decompose all of them, so export-value growth should not be rewritten as higher farm profits.

## Support programs have conditions

The attachments describe short-term export insurance against nonpayment, exchange-rate products and guarantees supporting production finance. Each addresses a different risk. The existence of a program does not mean every loss is covered or every applicant receives the same limit. Eligibility, policy wording, limits and contract terms need checking.[1]

Quality, documents, transport and finance connect local production with an overseas consumer. Support should be assessed by whether those links become more reliable, not simply by counting visits. Turning interest into a lasting market, and sales into sustainable income, takes more than one headline number.

## Related video and reading context

KBS's February 12, 2024 Damyang strawberry report is a historical case on varieties, production and demand at home and abroad, not footage of the Jinju farms or Tongyeong oyster business visited this time. Different local operations should not be collapsed into one claim about producer profits.
