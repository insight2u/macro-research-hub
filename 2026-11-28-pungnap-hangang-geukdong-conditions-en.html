Hangang Geukdong by the Pungnap Fortress: checking the conditions for a 32-year-old complex

Complex outline, heritage rules, the progress of two neighbors and transaction records, from public data

KOENJAZHES

Hangang Geukdong in Pungnap-dong, Songpa, is an 895-home complex that received its occupancy approval in July 1995. In the same neighborhood Pungnap Miseong cleared its redevelopment plan review in December 2025 and Pungnap Geukdong in November 2026. Whether the conditions they met apply unchanged to this complex has not been confirmed. This article sets out what public records show.

Outline: 895 homes, nine buildings, approved in 1995

Hangang Geukdong stands at 38-6 Toseong-ro, Songpa. It received its occupancy approval on July 31, 1995, and has nine buildings, 895 homes and up to 18 floors. Parking is 877 spaces, about 0.97 per home. Its floor area ratio is listed at about 226%. Geukdong Construction built it. It is in its 32nd year. We found no public record of an application for zone designation or of the safety diagnosis result. Maeil Business, on December 7, 2025, described it as a nearby complex that is pursuing rebuilding.

What the fortress imposes: height and excavation

Near the Pungnap earthen fortress, buildings face an angle-of-elevation rule: nothing may be built higher than an imaginary line drawn at 27 degrees from a point 7.5 meters high at the heritage boundary (Korea Economic Daily). The ground yields Baekje-era relics, so a survey of buried heritage is needed before construction. The paper said finds can delay a project even after work starts and that application differs by complex. In 2024 Newstomato attributed the lower prices to this rule and put the price of an 84 square meter unit in the 1.1 billion won range.

Two earlier cases: Pungnap Miseong and Pungnap Geukdong

Pungnap Miseong is the first rebuilding complex to win an easing of the angle rule, on condition of a trial excavation. It goes from 275 to 413 homes and from 11 to 23 floors, with a floor area ratio of 250% or less (Korea Economic Daily, Maeil Business). Seoul passed the plan on December 5, 2025. Pungnap Geukdong, completed in 1987, received a D grade in its December 2021 safety diagnosis. On November 13, 2026 a plan to go from 415 to 598 homes, up to 40 floors, 340% or less, with 81 public homes, passed with amendments. It used the station-area rebuilding floor-area special. Hangang Geukdong's ratio is about 226% now, so any similar special or easing would need its own review.

Transaction record: 59 square meters at 1.625 billion won on October 31, 2026

A site compiling Ministry of Land transaction data shows the latest deal for a 59 square meter unit as 1.625 billion won on the 8th floor on October 31, 2026. With the August 10 deal it is shown as the highest since 2020 for that size. The top 84 square meter deal was 1.615 billion won on May 4 and the top 114 square meter deal was 2.005 billion won on March 7. About 65 deals were recorded in two years and around 30 sales in the last year, few for 895 homes. With few deals, one or two can move an average a lot. These are reported transactions and say nothing about future prices or the project.

What to watch

First, whether the safety diagnosis result and any zone designation application appear in Seoul and Songpa notices. Second, which Pungnap fortress zone the complex falls in and how the angle rule applies; this is settled in Cultural Heritage Administration reviews and the Seoul plan. Third, the later steps for Pungnap Miseong and Pungnap Geukdong, such as the project implementation approval and excavation results, which set a timetable for the neighbors. Fourth, the base rate rose to 3.25% on November 26, a figure needed for interest during relocation and construction. This article is not investment advice and recommends no transaction.

Scope of verification

Figures in this article were checked against public agency statistics and dated press reports. Figures taken from an article are as reported there and may need further checking against the original data. Interpretations give grounds and limits together and are not settled forecasts.
