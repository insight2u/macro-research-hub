# Seventy-three bank contract clauses: a correction request is not a completed correction

Announcement on November 23, 2026 · Personal finance

## An interest rate is easier to compare than an exit condition

Imagine signing up for a financial service online, then being told to visit a branch with a written request to cancel it. On November 23, the Fair Trade Commission cited restrictions on customers' cancellation methods among problematic bank terms. The announcement does not mean every bank has already changed every contract. The commission has requested corrective action from the Financial Services Commission.[1]

The review covered 1,034 bank contracts and 516 savings-bank contracts introduced or amended in 2025, a total of 1,550. The commission judged 73 clauses unfair, grouped into 17 types: 65 bank clauses across 12 types and eight savings-bank clauses across five types. Contract counts and clause counts are different units. They cannot be combined into a percentage of banks with unfair terms.[1]

## The contract extends beyond the sign-up button

The problems were not limited to prices. Some clauses allowed banks to change, suspend or restrict services for broadly framed reasons, including internal circumstances or business-policy judgments. The commission highlighted conditions customers could not reasonably predict and that left room for discretionary decisions by the provider.[1]

Other examples allowed fees without specific criteria or put operating policies ahead of the contract. Customers need to know how stable the terms they accepted will remain. Alongside a promotional interest rate, it is worth reading the reasons for changes, notification methods and how fees are determined.

Cancellation can also be asymmetric. A provider's reasons for ending a relationship may be broad while a customer's exit route is narrow. The commission criticized both vaguely defined termination grounds and examples requiring an in-person written cancellation. The release anonymizes its examples; there is no basis for attaching guessed bank names to them.[1]

## Regulatory procedure and individual disputes are different

Under the process described, financial authorities pass reported contract terms to the commission, which reviews fairness and requests corrections. Financial authorities then take follow-up measures such as recommendations or orders to individual companies.[1]

The announcement does not automatically cancel a customer's contract or establish compensation. The service's current terms, change notices and facts of an individual dispute require separate examination. This article does not decide a legal case or promise a successful claim.

The commission also said it plans sequential correction requests for specialized credit finance and financial-investment businesses within the year. Those are future steps. Counting sectors still under review as already corrected would misstate the policy's progress.[1]

Convenience is not measured only by the speed of joining a service. Does the provider explain changes, notify customers of unfavorable conditions, and offer a reasonable way to leave? The question beside the rate table is whether the contract also provides a fair exit.
