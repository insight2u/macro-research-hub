# How Putin remained at the centre of power, from intelligence to the presidency

November 25, 2026 edition | World and history | Historical analysis prompted by the November 24 broadcast

Putin's career is often reduced to the story of an intelligence officer becoming president. That single line leaves out the steps between. He moved through a university, local government, the presidential administration, the leadership of a security service and the premiership before becoming acting president. Later, even when his title changed from president to prime minister, he did not leave the centre of power.

The history segment of Maebul Show on November 24 retold that path for a broad audience. Personal traits and anecdotes attract attention, but understanding long rule requires looking at verifiable career milestones and the institutions through which power moved. This article follows those links by comparing the Russian presidency's official biography with Britannica's independent account.

## From East Germany to local administration

The presidential biography says Putin was born in Leningrad in 1952, graduated in law from Leningrad State University in 1975, was assigned to the KGB and worked in East Germany from 1985 to 1990. This establishes the outline of his career. It does not prove which secret missions he carried out.

After returning in 1990, he worked in university external relations and in roles connected with the Leningrad city council. In 1991 he became responsible for international relations in St Petersburg; by 1994 he had reached the position of first deputy mayor. Britannica identifies his relationship with Anatoly Sobchak as an important link in his political rise.

His intelligence background alone cannot explain all that followed. Local government provided administrative experience and relationships before his move to central power. The path is better read as the combination of practices learned in one institution with a personal network developed in another.

## Promotion accelerated after the move to Moscow

According to the official biography, Putin entered the presidential property-management administration in 1996. In 1997 he became a deputy head of the presidential administration and head of its supervision and inspections body. He became director of the Federal Security Service, the FSB, in 1998, added the role of Security Council secretary in 1999 and was appointed prime minister that August.

The point is not simply the speed of promotion. These posts brought him through state property, administration, supervision, security and national-security policy. He did not suddenly appear from outside politics. His career brought him closer to central government's core functions.

Britannica explains that Yeltsin appointed him prime minister while seeking a successor. But a career chronology alone cannot establish private motives or private promises. Interpretation of why he was chosen needs to be separated from the facts of when he held each post.

## Acting president in 1999 and election in 2000 were different events

When Yeltsin resigned on December 31, 1999, Putin became acting president. He won the presidential election on March 26, 2000 and was inaugurated on May 7. A summary saying he was president “from 1999” includes the acting period. It is not the same as the date of his elected presidency.

The distinction shows how succession worked. Before winning a presidential election, he was already exercising presidential powers. Succession and election were not a single event; one followed the other.

Britannica discusses the strengthening of presidential control, pressure on regional power and major business figures, and pressure on the media after he took office. The official presidential biography mainly lists appointments and elections. Sources about the same person differ in what they record and what they leave unexplained. An official source is useful for dates but cannot replace an independent assessment of power.

## Leaving out the premiership hides the structure of long rule

Putin left the presidency to become prime minister in 2008, while Dmitry Medvedev became president. Putin returned to the presidency in 2012. The official biography records these changes. Britannica assesses that he was still regarded as the Kremlin's principal power during his premiership.

Continuity of a presidential title differs from continuity of political influence. Leaving 2008–2012 blank, or simply counting it as a presidential term, obscures the structure. Who was the constitutional head of state and who retained political power must be examined separately.

South Korea's foreign ministry also gives May 2012 as the start of Putin's current presidential tenure in its Russia country profile. It is identifying the beginning of the current continuous presidency, not denying his earlier career.

## Read a biography without turning it into a heroic tale

It is easy to remember stories attributing every action of a former intelligence officer to secret training, or explaining his hold on power through personal courage alone. But claims about the cause of his gait, private conversations and involvement in crimes require separate evidence. They are not reproduced here.

Nor should a leader's biography stand for an entire country. Sanctions, energy and the costs of war require their own evidence. A KIEP analysis published in October 2026 describes how supply security and domestic economic pressures interact with sanctions coordination in exceptions to sanctions on Russia. One person's will is not enough to explain every international outcome.

Putin's career shows movement between institutions, an opening through succession and continuity of power despite changes in office, rather than just one dramatic arrival. Following that path makes the interaction between institutions and concentrated power clearer than a personal myth does.
