Press consolidation: dictatorship took more than broadcasting stations

Do not soften the replacement of media institutions and suppression of criticism into industrial efficiency. Under Chun, press control deprived citizens of opportunities to identify and challenge state violence. Silence was not consent, and a government statement was not the same as truth.

Independent editorial criticism · Revised 26 November 2026

한국어 · English · 日本語 · 简体中文

Voices lost behind the word reorganisation

The National Archives describes the 1980 absorption of TBC into KBS and KBS’s acquisition of a substantial shareholding in MBC. An organisation chart is not enough. Why were competing editorial judgments and channels for scrutinising power reduced?[1]

A law of control is not an order of free journalism

The Archives’ account identifies thorough suppression and control through the Basic Press Act. When power determines whether a news organisation can survive and operate, censorship begins before a sentence is edited. Public ownership alone does not guarantee service to the public.[2]

Citizens paid the price of concealed truth

The false announcement and cover-up of Park Jong-cheol’s death were challenged through an autopsy, testimony, reporting and priests’ disclosures. Without independent scrutiny, even a victim’s death can be imprisoned inside the government’s account. This is why we criticise press control as part of state violence. What must be restored is citizens’ right to know, not only media history.[3]
