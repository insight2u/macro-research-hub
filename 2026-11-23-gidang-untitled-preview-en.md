# An untitled work leaves room for your first observation

## Before the title tells you what to see

A title is a convenient entrance to an artwork. Read it first and you may arrange the colors and forms around its words. What happens when that entrance is left open? Gidang Museum's collection exhibition, Untitled: Deliberate Absence, starts with that question.

Reporting the Seogwipo public museums' November 20 announcement, Seogwipo TV and Tamna Maeil say the show is scheduled for November 24, 2026 through February 21, 2027 in Gidang Museum's special exhibition gallery. The November 23 article date is not the opening date. This is a pre-opening preview, not a review of a visit.[1][2]

## One shared title does not make identical works

The reported selection brings together around twenty works from the collection and entrusted holdings, all titled Untitled. It includes abstract painting by Kang Tae-seok, Korean painting by Ko Kyung-hee, Hwang Chang-bae and Yook Keun-byung, and three-dimensional work by Lim Choong-sup. A shared title does not make their materials or expression identical.[1][2]

The exhibition's stated approach is to treat the absence of a descriptive title as a deliberate choice, drawing attention to color, form and expression. That is the exhibition's perspective, not a universal explanation of every artwork called Untitled.[1]

## Your title records a response

The reports say an art-lounge activity will invite visitors to invent titles and share their responses during the exhibition. That does not mean participants replace an artist's official title.[1][2]

One possible approach is to note a color or form that holds your attention, imagine a temporary title, then look again. This is a viewing suggestion, not analysis based on firsthand inspection of the exhibits. When another visitor chooses different words, ask what caught each person's eye before deciding which response is right.

## Keep this preview separate from another exhibition's guide

For a visit, check Seogwipo's official museum information separately. Its general page describes Monday closures and public-holiday exceptions. That visitor-information page is not a detailed notice for this exhibition.[3]

The MMCA video below guides viewers through Haegue Yang's 2020 exhibition and discusses titles as clues. It does not show the works or installation of Gidang's forthcoming show. It offers a separate point of comparison: following a title's clues, or starting with your own observations before a work called Untitled.[4]
