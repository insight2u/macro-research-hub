## When one voice meets another writer's song
Separating the writer from the performer makes Kim Kwang Seok's work clearer. Kang Seung-won wrote the words and music of About Thirty. It became one of Kim's signature songs, but crediting him with its composition would blur both musicians' contributions. The distributor-supplied YouTube Topic recording credits Kang as composer and writer; Im Jin-mo's IZM review confirms the distinction.[1][2]

Making another person's song convincing in one's own voice is not the opposite of creation. Rather than remove the songwriter from the picture, we can ask how a written song and the singer who delivers it meet in a single work.

## The fourth album is more than melancholy
Bugs dates Kim's fourth album to June 25, 1994. Its track list places Get Up, Where the Wind Blows and About Thirty on the same record. One famous track should not determine how we hear the whole album.[3]

In his March 2005 IZM review, critic Im Jin-mo reads the album as an encounter with the uncertainty of entering one's thirties, without reducing it to resignation. He considers Kim's own compositions alongside songs by other writers and Cho Dong-ik's arrangements, and draws attention to the singer's control of contrasting intensities. These are the critic's judgments, not a claim that Kim wrote every song.[2]

Borrow that contrast for today's listening. After About Thirty, return to Get Up. How do stillness and movement feel different within the same album? Must a sad song mean surrender? The invitation is to hear the distance between songs, rather than start with a prescribed answer.

## Release dates and upload dates keep different time
The 1994 album, a 2014 remaster and YouTube uploads in 2022 are different events. The Topic recording linked below is titled 2014 Remastered Version. Its description carries a 2014 With 33 Music phonogram notice and a December 11, 2014 release date. The video was uploaded in July 2022. That is not the song's original release date.[1][3]

When digital pages show different dates, first ask which event each date describes. Today's video view count does not measure the song's popularity in 1994. A remaster label alone also cannot establish how the sound has changed.
