From Ilhae Foundation to Sejong: Lineage of a Name and Evidence of Responsibility

Institutional history is a starting point for reporting. Do not automatically extend past ties to power into present-day illegality.

Name Changes Recorded in the National Archives of Korea

The institutional history of the National Archives of Korea records the establishment of the Ilhae Foundation on December 1, 1983, the name change to the Ilhae Research Institute on August 27, 1987, and the name change to the Sejong Institute on May 4, 1988. It also presents the course of changing to the Sejong Foundation on September 19, 1996. This material is a record showing institutional history during specific periods and is not material explaining the entire current organizational chart.

Why Read the Foundation Within the History of Power

When reporting on the relationship between political power and a foundation, one must examine the stated purpose of establishment, channels through which money was gathered, actual decision-making, and use of funds separately. Changes in name can serve as a guide to finding those questions. However, institutional history alone cannot prove coercion in fundraising or crimes by a specific individual. That is why unverified hearing remarks or amounts are not cited in this article.

Continuity and Rupture Must Be Verified Through Records

Merely because part of the same name remained or institutional lineage continued cannot be taken to mean that current members bear past responsibility unchanged. Conversely, one cannot view ties of funding and operation as completely severed merely because a name was changed. It is necessary to verify in financial, corporate, and personnel records what continued and what changed. This passage is an editorial analysis presenting standards for future reporting.

Do Not Fill Gaps in Records with Suspicions

What is presented as established in this article is the institutional history contained in the National Archives of Korea. Specific illegality in past fundraising and present-day institutional operations are separate subjects of verification. While asking what institutional traces power in the Chun Doo-hwan era left behind, it does not append a phrase about current illicit accumulation without basis. Specifying the scope of verification is the first step in asking about responsibility concretely.

Sources and scope of verification

Court judgments, public investigation decisions and institutional records have different authors and dates. Current unpaid balances, individual assets and subsequent changes in law are outside the scope of this verification. This is not a full-book or complete-film review.
