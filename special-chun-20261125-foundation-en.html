Ilhae Foundation’s KRW59.85 billion: scrutinise fundraising power before the public-interest label

Large corporate sums flowed to a foundation bearing the ruler’s name. Before calling the money public-spirited, ask whether companies could refuse. Reducing Ilhae to a history of institutional renaming erases the unequal relationship between power and money.

Independent editorial criticism · Revised 26 November 2026

한국어 · English · 日本語 · 简体中文

KRW59.85 billion was the figure submitted to parliament

On September 5, 1988, MBC cited material submitted to the parliamentary Fifth Republic inquiry: 56 businesspeople and others had contributed KRW59.85 billion during 1984–1987. It reported KRW5.15 billion from Hyundai’s Chung Ju-yung and KRW4.5 billion each from Samsung’s Lee Kun-hee and POSCO’s Park Tae-joon. These are historical contributions, not a current asset valuation.[1]

A witness’s claim of voluntariness does not end scrutiny

In the December 8 hearing report, former Korea Chamber of Commerce and Industry chairman Chung Soo-chang said there were company allocation criteria but payment depended on companies’ wishes. Lawmakers questioned coercion and links to favours. Neither the denial proves free choice nor lawmakers’ questions constitute final criminal judgments.[2]

Look at the freedom to refuse, not the word donation

Our criticism is direct: fundraising under an authoritarian ruler cannot be assumed equivalent to ordinary private charity. Examine allocation rules, accounts, decisions and use of funds. Renaming the institution cannot dissolve that question, and past crimes cannot automatically be assigned to present staff. Public interest means public scrutiny, not a ruler’s commemoration.
