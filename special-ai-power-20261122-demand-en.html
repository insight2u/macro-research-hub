AI electricity: separate estimates from scenarios

485, 950 and 1,000TWh are not the same kind of number

Published 22 November 2026 · Policy announcement: 20 November

한국어English简体中文日本語

Start with the denominator

The phrase 'electricity used by AI' can merge different categories. Data centres also run non-AI workloads. Growth at AI-focused facilities differs from growth across all data centres. The IEA's Key Questions report gives 2025 electricity-consumption growth of 17% for all data centres and 50% for AI-focused centres. Swapping those rates distorts both scale and speed.[4]

The past is estimated; the future is conditional

In that report, 485TWh is an estimate of global data-centre electricity use in 2025. Its 950TWh figure is a central projection for 2030. WEO 2026 instead puts 2030 consumption at about 1,000TWh in STEPS. Without identifying document and scenario, saying the IEA 'confirmed an increase from 950 to 1,000' is inaccurate. Averaging them into another number is no solution either.[3][4]

Does better efficiency reduce total demand?

The IEA describes improving efficiency per AI task alongside growing use of energy-intensive video generation and reasoning. Electricity per task can fall while total use rises as frequency and the task mix change. One query's energy use cannot simply be scaled into an industry-wide forecast; efficiency improvements alone do not prove grid investment unnecessary.[4]

Four labels every chart needs

Our suggested labels are coverage, reference year, document and scenario. For example: 'all global data centres / 2030 / WEO 2026 / STEPS / approximately 1,000TWh'. With these labels, numbers become tools for comparison rather than slogans. A global consumption projection cannot directly establish the needs of one Korean generation technology or the earnings of an individual business.
