If we copy successful people's habits, will we succeed too? If we collect the traits of successful companies, can we identify the next winner? A wealth of numbers and examples does not make a conclusion trustworthy. Today's book is less about calculating quickly than about spotting where judgment can go wrong.

### Think about the aircraft that never returned

A Hankyoreh review of *How Not to Be Wrong* leads with an example of survivorship bias involving aircraft. When deciding where to add armor by examining bullet holes in aircraft that returned from combat, one set of evidence is missing: the aircraft that did not return. Even abundant data can lead us astray when we use only observable outcomes to explain the whole picture.

Consider an everyday parallel. Interviews about success leave out people who made the same choices and failed. A comparison of products that have survived for years leaves out those that disappeared. These are editorial questions extending the book's example, not findings about a particular company or product.

### Mathematics changes the question, not just the answer

The publisher's description on YES24 says the book addresses linearity, inference, regression, expectation and existence. The point is not to memorize mathematical terms. It is to check the assumptions, ask whose data were collected and consider whether a trend observed over one interval can safely be extended indefinitely.

The book may therefore interest readers who work with numbers as well as those who read and write arguments based on them. Before accepting a graph's conclusion, ask about the sample and the conditions left out. This article compares public descriptions and reviews; it is not a review based on reading the entire book.

### Where do the recommendations and responses come from?

In April 2026, the University News Network reported that Hanyang University president Lee Ki-jeong recommended the book. His stated reasoning emphasized accurate interpretation and logical judgment rather than simply knowing more in the age of AI. That provides a reason to revisit a 2016 title through a 2026 university recommendation.

There is also a caution about difficulty. In a 2022 review on Sungkyunkwan University's reading platform Ogeoseo, the contributor using the name 여룬 found the book difficult but full of fresh and interesting material. The contributor also felt the explanation of regression analysis was insufficient and looked it up elsewhere. One account cannot stand for all readers. It is a reminder that the book may suit readers willing to investigate unfamiliar concepts, rather than those expecting an effortless introduction.

### About the author

According to the YES24 author profile, Jordan Ellenberg earned a doctorate in mathematics at Harvard and has combined mathematical research with writing for general readers. He also wrote the novel *The Grasshopper King*. That record offers another clue to a book that explains mathematics beyond calculation exercises.

### Today's reading question

In the numbers you currently find most convincing, which people and cases are missing?
