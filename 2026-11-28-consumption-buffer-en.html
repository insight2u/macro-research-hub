Spending more is not the same as living better

A 70.4% propensity to consume and a shrinking household surplus

KOENJAZHES

Rising consumption does not always mean consumers have more room to breathe. In Korea's third-quarter 2026 household survey, average monthly consumption expenditure rose 2.9% to 3.03 million won, while the surplus fell 11.2% to 1.276 million won. A heavier shopping basket may come with a thinner financial cushion.

A ratio can reveal constraint rather than enthusiasm

The average propensity to consume measures consumption expenditure as a share of disposable income. It rose 3.2 percentage points to 70.4%. Reading this only as a sign that consumers eagerly opened their wallets would be misleading. We need to examine both consumption, the numerator, and disposable income, the denominator.

The average cannot stand in for every industry

Higher average household consumption does not mean sales volumes increased for every good and service. Nominal expenditure combines price and quantity changes. Businesses should examine spending by category and customers' incomes rather than treating the overall consumption growth rate as growth in their own market. A trend among one age group is not a measure of all households' purchasing power.

Repeat purchases need more than attention

For customers facing high living costs, predictable total spending may matter more than novelty. This is a business interpretation prompted by the survey, not a law explaining every consumer's choices. Advertising response and actual repeat purchases must be checked separately, as must revenue and sales volume. Small customer calculations sit beneath the large headline about a consumption recovery.
