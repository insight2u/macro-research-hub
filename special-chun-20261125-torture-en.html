False Explanations Attached After Death

The Korea Democracy Foundation records the course of Park Jong-cheol's death from police torture on January 14, 1987. The police's initial explanation and the fact of torture could not coexist. At the heart of the incident lies not only violence that took a life, but also the organizational response that tried to hide its cause. False explanations cloud investigations while making it difficult to speak publicly about the victim's death.

A Process of Coming to Light Rather Than a Single Announcement

The foundation's records explain that suspicions of cover-up and fabrication continued even after torture was acknowledged, and that the revelation by the Catholic Priests' Association for Justice on May 18 drew strong repercussions. Truth came to light not through the first official announcement, but through testing and overturning it. One can see why checking state explanations from outside, through medical professionals' testimonies, religious revelations, and citizens' demands, is important.

Distinguishing Regime Responsibility from Direct Orders

Criticizing torture and cover-ups by state organs under the Chun Doo-hwan regime is different from fabricating direct orders that have not been obtained. Based on these materials alone, it is not stated that Chun Doo-hwan personally ordered individual acts of torture. Cover-ups by relevant agencies, responsibility for running the regime, and acts established at the scene must be examined in their respective records. Breaking down responsibility is specification, not absolution.

Questions Remaining for Today's Institutions

When investigative bodies monopolize explanations of an incident, who can verify those explanations? The history of torture and cover-up leads to questions of independent oversight, preservation of records, and safety for whistleblowers. This is an editorial interpretation reading the case records. Rather than compressing a victim's death solely into being a catalyst for democratization, the acts that took that life and truth must be recorded separately.
