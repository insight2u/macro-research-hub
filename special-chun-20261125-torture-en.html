A regime that tortured Park Jong-cheol to death and tried to bury the truth

Calling Park Jong-cheol’s death an investigative accident repeats the language of the perpetrators. Unlawful arrest, police torture and an organised cover-up are documented. Responsibility cannot end with the hands that tortured him, because institutions also worked to hide the violence.

Independent editorial criticism · Revised 26 November 2026

한국어 · English · 日本語 · 简体中文

The life taken in Room 509

Police unlawfully arrested Seoul National University student Park Jong-cheol to ask about the whereabouts of his wanted senior, Park Jong-woon. He died under torture in Room 509 of the Namyeong-dong interrogation facility on January 14, 1987. The Academy of Korean Studies records beatings, electrical torture and water torture. An investigation is not authority to torture a human being.[1][2]

A false announcement began the cover-up

Police presented the death as simple shock. On January 19 they admitted water torture but arrested only two investigators. On May 18, Father Kim Seung-hun disclosed that five officers had participated and senior security officers had minimised and manipulated the case. The sources also describe organised involvement by the interagency countermeasures meeting.[1][2]

Democracy was not a gift from a torture regime

Demands for truth and anger at the cover-up helped drive the June uprising. There is no need to add an unverified personal order from the president to question systemic responsibility. We reject an account of democratisation as merely the regime’s concession. Restore the place of citizens who risked themselves to reveal the truth.[1][2]
