# One week for 820 trillion won: the sub-subcommittee facing the budget deadline

## From the 162-trillion-won Future Response Fund to special activity expenses, the issues left in quiet talks

November 25, 2026 | A Bite of Yeouido, a Plate of Diplomacy (Politics & Budget)

The legal deadline (December 2) for passing next year's budget bill, sized at 820.9 trillion won, is now a week away. On the 25th, the National Assembly's Special Committee on Budget and Accounts opened a closed-door "sub-subcommittee," without announcing its time or place, and began reviewing held-over items.[1]

## Where the process stands

The budget committee's review period is 30 days; if it fails to vote within that period, the budget bill is automatically referred to the plenary session on December 1. The sub-subcommittee is an informal consultative body attended only by a small group - budget committee chair Lee Kwang-jae, the floor negotiators of the two parties, and officials from the budget office - where last-minute bargaining over contested items takes place.[1]

The legal deadline is the statutory standard for passing the budget, but missing it does not in itself mean the budget fails. That said, criticism that the quality of review deteriorates as the deadline approaches has recurred every year, and that is the backdrop of this round of talks.

## The biggest issue: the 162.3-trillion-won Future Response Fund

At the center of this review is the Future Response Fund, a new fund of 162.3 trillion won. The bill establishing its legal basis is currently pending in the National Assembly's fiscal planning committee. On the evening of the 24th, four budget and fiscal committee negotiators from both parties (lawmakers Chung Tae-ho, Yoo Sang-bum, Oh Gi-hyung and Bae Jun-young) held a first closed-door meeting but found no common ground.[1]

Positions differ. The Democratic Party says the related bills must clear the fiscal committee first, while the People Power Party demands cuts to individual projects and a return of the money to existing accounts. After criticism that a large new fund was heading straight to a closed-door subcommittee, the budget committee decided to hold an additional formal budget subcommittee meeting on the 26th to discuss the fund. But because that presupposes the fiscal committee's bill deliberations, whether a normal review can begin remains unclear.[1]

## The 8.25-billion-won special activity expenses

At the budget subcommittee on the 24th, the two parties clashed over the presidential office's special activity expenses of 8.25 billion won, and the review was suspended. The Democratic Party argued for keeping the original amount to support the president's office, while the People Power Party demanded a cut, saying last year's spending had not been properly settled.[1]

Democratic Party lawmaker Chung Tae-ho said, "Many items require confidentiality, so please understand that we cannot provide detailed materials," while People Power Party lawmaker Yoo Sang-bum said, "We pointed this out during the settlement review, and the uncooperative attitude has been repeated in the budget review."[1] What is confirmed is that the two sides' claims differ; judging which claim is right would require examining the submitted materials.

## What to watch in the remaining week

The first gate is whether the Future Response Fund actually comes up as an agenda item at the formal budget subcommittee on the 26th, and whether the fiscal committee's deliberation of the founding bill proceeds. What remains after that is whether the process meets the committee's review deadline (automatic referral to the plenary on December 1) and the legal deadline (December 2), or misses them. This article lays out the structure of ongoing negotiations; it is not commentary endorsing any party's claim.
