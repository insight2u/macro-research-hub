# The Last Emperor: China's upheavals, heard through Sakamoto's music

## Begin with a timeline, then listen
Before the spectacle of the Forbidden City, open a timeline. Bernardo Bertolucci's The Last Emperor (1987) follows Puyi across the end of the Qing, Japan's domination of Manchuria and political upheaval in the People's Republic. Reading that history alongside Ryuichi Sakamoto's music brings the distance between the imperial title and the person inside it into focus. That is this article's editorial perspective.

## 1912: abdication did not end history
Britannica dates Puyi's birth to 1906 and accession to 1908. The 1911 revolution and his abdication on 12 February 1912 are distinct events. The end of an empire cannot be reduced to a child's personal fall. As China moved from dynasty toward republic, the imperial symbol retained political significance after losing formal power.

The film can open a route into this history. Its dialogue, designed spaces and compressed sequence of events are not historical evidence in themselves. A dramatization may provoke inquiry; independent historical sources must establish the facts.

## 1932 and 1934: Manchukuo, Japan and the recovered throne
The University of Guelph's Manchurian timeline places the expansion of Japanese occupation after the 1931 Mukden Incident, the creation of Manchukuo in 1932, and Puyi's installation as its emperor in 1934. Foundation and imperial accession were not the same event. Britannica likewise describes a Japanese-controlled puppet state, with Puyi's imperial tenure running from 1934 to 1945.

A restored title did not mean restored sovereignty. That distinction moves the discussion beyond a story of a lost dynasty regaining its throne. How the film portrays Puyi's knowledge and choices must still be separated from historical research. One person's tragedy must not obscure the larger structure of Japanese invasion and domination.

## After 1959 and into the Cultural Revolution
Puyi was captured by Soviet forces in 1945, returned to China in 1950 and was pardoned in 1959. Mao launched the Cultural Revolution in 1966; it lasted until 1976. Puyi died in 1967. He did not live through the entire movement.

This chronology prevents a straight line from abdication to an uncomplicated life as an ordinary citizen. Society entered another political upheaval. A new name or role cannot by itself close the story as liberation. This article stops at that historical horizon. It does not claim the film already explains subsequent reform and opening or China today.

## Sakamoto: a composer is not a stand-in for an imperial state
Japan's colonial domination and a Japanese musician's participation in an international film in the 1980 s belong to different analytical levels. Sakamoto's own filmography lists The Last Emperor under 1987. The Academy's record names three 1988 Original Score winners: Ryuichi Sakamoto, David Byrne and Cong Su. Calling the whole soundtrack Sakamoto's solo work would be inaccurate.

The linked recording of The Last Emperor (Theme) is supplied by Universal Music Group on the Ryuichi Sakamoto Topic channel. Its description credits his composition and production and carries a 1987 Virgin phonogram notice. Its 2008 release metadata does not change the film's original year.

Try the official trailer, then listen to the theme without images. Does the palace's scale remain, or does personal isolation come forward? These are proposed listening questions, not a claim to have newly verified the placement of this cue in a particular scene. Emotional music is not a pardon for historical power. The film can lead into history; the music can return attention to the people caught within it.

## Criticism and scope
David Thomson's complete Criterion essay attends to small, quiet moments beside the epic, and to Storaro's light and Scarfiotti's spaces. His interpretation is placed alongside the timeline, not used as a substitute for historical evidence.
