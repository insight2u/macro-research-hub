November 28, 1943: negotiations moved before the map

Military strategy and the postwar order at the opening of the Tehran Conference

KOENJAZHES

Roosevelt, Churchill and Stalin met in Tehran on November 28, 1943. The US Office of the Historian dates the conference from November 28 to December 1. How to end the war and how to shape the world afterward shared the negotiating table. Before reducing today's borders to a single conference, we should examine the stages of decision-making.

Operational commitments and political designs

The historian's account identifies coordination of the northern France invasion and a Soviet Eastern Front offensive as major achievements. The Soviet Union's agreement in principle to enter the war against Japan after Germany's defeat and plans for an international organization were also discussed. Military cooperation and postwar influence were intertwined.

Discussion did not make every detail final that day

The account distinguishes the discussions from the details of the Japan-war bargain, which were finalized at Yalta in 1945. Formalization of decisions concerning Poland's borders also continued at later conferences. Compressing discussion, agreement in principle, final detail and implementation into one verb erases these layers of time. That is why contemporary records and later explanations should be read together.

Questions, not predictions, for the present

A handshake between great-power leaders does not represent everyone affected. The conference also issued a declaration supporting Iran's independence, sovereignty and territorial integrity. Whether smaller states are protected between declarations and actual policy is a separate question. Today's diplomacy is not a copy of 1943, but who sits at the table and who bears the consequences remain useful questions.
