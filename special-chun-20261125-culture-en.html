Questions Raised by Books and Films, Boundaries Set by Rulings

How to read 12.12: The Day and 26 Years alongside historical records. Do not turn scenes in works into proof of fact.

2026.11.25 · English edition · translated from the Korean source edition checked at 20:10 KST

Persuasiveness on Screen and Historical Proof Are Different

Films show complex periods of events through characters' choices and the tension of scenes. That persuasiveness has the power to open interest in history, but it is not proof that a specific scene actually occurred. In official information from the Korea Film Archive, 12.12: The Day is confirmed as a work directed by Kim Sung-su with screenplay credits for Hong In-pyo, Hong Won-chan, Lee Young-jong, and Kim Sung-su. 26 Years is a film directed by Cho Geun-hyun based on an original work by Kang Full. Not mixing up the original creators and makers of the two works is the most basic starting point.

Two Layers for Reading the Night of the Mutiny

To understand December 12 using 12.12: The Day as an entry point, one must distinguish the commanders in the drama from actual historical figures. Next, one can check what the Supreme Court ruling viewed as mutiny. The judgment that arresting a commander without due process and rebelling against military command authority is a historical and legal standard separate from the emotion of a scene. Lines in the drama are not transferred as statements by actual figures merely because they were impressive. When writing the questions of a work alongside the answers of records, the gap between dramatization and fact can also be explained.

The Question of Harm Remaining Across Generations

The fictional retribution narrative of 26 Years opens the question of how harm and responsibility are passed down to the next generation. It should not be read as the course of actual events or a recommended solution. It can be connected by asking whether trials, public investigations, and victim redress were sufficient. Chun Doo-hwan's finalized conviction, his subsequent pardon, and victims' recovery are each separate issues. Emotions stirred by a work should serve as an occasion to look more closely rather than erase these differences.

Three Books Are Different Entrances for Reading

Ko Na-mu's Chun Doo-hwan, Still Alive is a reportage published by Bookcomma in 2013. Jeong Ah-eun's Chun Doo-hwan's Last 33 Years was published by Sideway in 2023. Chun Doo-hwan Other-History Biography, compiled by Jeong Il-young and Hwang Dong-ha, is a 2017 book from Geurimssi. These reflect the scope of bibliographic data and publisher introductions reopened and checked this time. Approaches can be divided into questions tracing the figure and surrounding relationships, questions asking about responsibility after leaving office, and questions reading newspapers of the time alongside a later perspective. This categorization is a reading guide from the editorial team and does not mean the entire text of the books was analyzed.

The Weight of Saying One Has Read a Book

The books selected for this project are Chun Doo-hwan, Still Alive, Chun Doo-hwan's Last 33 Years, and Chun Doo-hwan Other-History Biography. The current scope of verification is publication information, tables of contents, and existing project materials. Because this is not a book review written after reading the entire book, it does not newly attribute specific allegations to authors or invent direct quotes without page numbers. Once the original texts are obtained, claims in the work, testimonies of interviewees, and commentary by editors can be reviewed separately. Depth of reading is revealed not by the number of sensational anecdotes, but by how a single claim is cross-checked against records.
