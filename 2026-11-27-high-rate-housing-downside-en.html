The high-rate era is here: what past rate-hike cycles did to home prices, and the outlook for next year

Statistics from 1998 to 2022, the circumstances of each period, and how today differs

KOENJAZHES

On November 26 the Bank of Korea raised its base rate from 3.00% to 3.25%. The same day, Seoul apartment prices rose just 0.01% for the week. "Rates up, prices down" is only half true. Prices fell sharply in 2022, yet in 2006 they rose strongly while rates were climbing. This article goes through the statistics of past high-rate periods, the circumstances at the time, how today differs, and the outlook for next year. The outlook is a set of conditional scenarios and does not predict a fall or a rise.

What the market is signaling now

In the Korea Real Estate Board survey for the fourth week of November (as of the 23rd), Seoul apartment prices rose 0.01% on the week, extending the streak to 94 weeks, but the gain was smaller than the previous 0.03%. Songpa (-0.50%), Gangnam (-0.34%) and Seocho (-0.30%) kept falling, and Gwangjin and Yeongdeungpo each slipped 0.01%, turning negative. Seongbuk (0.33%), Nowon (0.27%) and Seodaemun (0.25%) rose. Prices are splitting by district. Jeonse (lump-sum deposit) rents rose 0.14% in both Seoul and the metropolitan area, holding the previous pace. The bond market shows a similar pattern. On the 26th the gap between three-year and ten-year Korean Treasury yields narrowed to 16.8bp, the smallest since March 2025. In the video, an economist said the long-short yield gap moves ahead of the economy and has been shrinking fast, and read that as the bond market expecting slower growth next year. But that day's narrowing came largely from short-term yields rising after the dot plot hinted at more hikes. Signs of slowdown and expectations of tightening are mixed, so the signal is hard to read one way. The September leading index (cyclical component) from the National Data Agency fell 0.1 point to 104.1, its first drop in 11 months, pulled down by the KOSPI and the yield gap.

The record: five periods when rates met home prices

In the 1998 foreign-exchange crisis, Seoul apartment prices fell 14.6% from the end of the previous year (Korea Research Institute for Human Settlements). From October 2005 to August 2007 the call-rate target rose from 3.25% to 5.00%, yet Seoul apartments rose 23.46% in 2006 (Korea Real Estate Board historical series). In the 2008 global financial crisis, Seoul apartment transaction prices fell 10.21% for the year. Seoul apartment prices then fell four years in a row from 2010 to 2013, by 4.5% in 2012 (KB) and 1.28% in 2013 (Korea Appraisal Board). The base rate went from 0.50% in August 2021 to 3.50% in January 2023. For 2022 the Real Estate Board reported apartment price falls of 7.56% nationally, 9.68% in the metropolitan area and 7.70% in Seoul, while the transaction-price index fell 22.09% in Seoul and 16.84% nationally, the largest drop since the series began in 2006. Actual deal prices fell far more than the official price index. 2008 asks the same question. The Bank of Korea raised its rate from 5.00% to 5.25% on August 7, 2008, then cut it six times, from October 9 through February 2009, to 2.00%, a total of 3.25 percentage points. The economist in the video cited this case: the hike came when growth was good and prices were rising, and a financial crisis followed soon after, which he used to stress how uncertain the future is. By the record, the first cut came about two months after the hike, so the direction flipped within two months.

The circumstances: why the same rate hikes gave different results

In 2022 the problem was speed. Rates rose 3.00 percentage points in about a year and a half, which cut buyers' borrowing power and raised the interest bills of existing borrowers. At the end of February 2022, household loan balances at the five major banks had fallen two months in a row, the first time since 2013. As deals dried up, urgent sales pulled prices down, and in December 2022 national housing prices fell 1.98% from the month before, the largest monthly drop since the series began in 2003. In 2006, by contrast, demand and expectations of further gains were strong, so prices rose even as rates rose. The 1998 fall came less from interest rates than from corporate restructuring, mass unemployment and a collapse in incomes. In short, rate hikes alone did not cause crashes. The falls were deepest when speed, income shocks, a freeze in transactions and weak price expectations came together.

How today differs from 2022

First, speed. The Bank raised rates by a total of 0.50 percentage points in July and August this year to 3.00% and added 0.25 in November. That is gentler than the cumulative 3.00 points of 2021-2023. Second, loan rates. The average new mortgage rate at banks rose from 4.32% in May to 4.66% in August. The video from Chaebusim notes that average mortgage rates are close to 5% and the highest exceed 7%. Third, the economy. The Bank of Korea raised its growth forecast for this year from 3.3% to 3.9% and put next year at 4.1%. That is unlike 1998, when restructuring and unemployment cut incomes. The same video also points out that private consumption is weak and construction investment is sluggish. High growth does not settle what a buyer can afford in interest. Fourth, supply. From January to August Seoul apartment completions fell 23.3% while starts rose 34.3%. Next year's metropolitan-area move-ins are about 120,000 homes, 72% of the ten-year average. One weight on the other side: metropolitan apartment prices have risen 6.65% this year (as of November 16), the steepest since 2021, so rate-sensitive areas have more room to correct. The economist in the video noted that the Bank of Korea's forecast leans heavily on semiconductor exports, while stock prices and the yield gap point to slower activity next year, a wide gap between the two. The Korea Economic Daily also reported that next year's growth depends on the chip cycle more than this year's. Samsung Electronics and SK hynix have fallen about 55% on average from their peaks (JoongAng Ilbo). The economist explained that when stocks fall, households have less room to spend and save, which links to the worry of a weaker wealth effect. That is why the stock market is a variable in home-price outlooks. Two of seven board members also voted to hold this time, another sign of split views.

Outlook for next year: three paths

Institutional forecasts lean upward. The Construction & Economy Research Institute expects national home prices to rise 2.0% and jeonse prices 4.5% next year, and major brokerages and research institutes expect metropolitan prices up 3-5% and jeonse up about 8%. On that basis the editors lay out three paths. First, a correction path: if loan rates rise further, lending rules stay in place and stocks stay weak, corrections in high-priced areas such as the three Gangnam districts and in heavily leveraged areas spread to neighbors along the Han River. Second, a flat path: gains hover near zero, deals thin out and buyers who wait stay in jeonse and monthly rent. KB Real Estate reported that monthly rent makes up 70% of rental deals. Third, a renewed-rise path: a shortage of move-ins and income expectations around the semiconductor belt support prices again. A broad 2022-style fall is the path where rate hikes speed up and an income shock arrives together, and today's indicators do not show those conditions. The Chaebusim video sees high loan rates as downward pressure that blocks money from reaching property through next year, but says property would also get room to breathe if stocks improve. It reads the fork between the correction and renewed-rise paths through the same variables. Which path prevails should be checked against the indicators below. Views on the rate path differ. The economist in the video says this is the last hike of the cycle and the Bank of Korea could cut in the second half of next year. The Bank's six-month conditional forecast (dot plot), released the same day, had 7 of 21 dots at 3.75%, 8 at 3.50% and 6 at 3.25%, so 15 sit above the current rate. Brokerage forecasts for the final rate also range from 3.25% to 3.75% (Money Today). The cut forecast is one expert's personal view and points the opposite way from the dot plot. He sees the first half of next year as the hardest period and says those with cash on hand may find an opportunity, which is his opinion. Applied to home prices, the editors note that earlier expectations of cuts would favor the flat or re-rise paths, and further hikes would favor the adjustment path.

An article within the article: the mistaken idea that more M2 means higher home prices

"More liquidity lifts home prices" is an old belief. In the 1988-2026 table kept in Notion, Seoul apartment prices and M2 do move in the same direction over the long run. But in the same table M2 growth was never negative in 39 years, while Seoul prices fell in 11 of them: 1992, 1993, 1998, 2004, 2008, 2010-2013, 2022 and 2023. In 2022 M2 grew 5.3% and Seoul still fell 7.2% (table basis; the official apartment statistic shows a 7.70% fall). The video sees M2 as a figure that bundles corporate and household loans, so the money that actually enters housing depends on where household mortgage lending goes. Long-run levels and year-by-year direction should be read separately.

Indicators to watch

Watch Seoul's weekly rise and the number of falling districts in the Korea Real Estate Board survey, the Bank of Korea's new-loan household rates, the monthly increase in household loans, transaction volume, jeonse growth and metropolitan move-in volume. The first signal is the weekly rate turning from zero to negative and falling districts spreading beyond the Han River belt. Add the gap between three-year and ten-year Treasury yields, the leading index (cyclical component) in the National Data Agency's industrial activity release, and the Bank of Korea's dot plot and board remarks, and shifts in the rate direction can be read earlier.
