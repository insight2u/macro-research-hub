The high-rate era is here: what past rate-hike cycles did to home prices, and the outlook for next year

Statistics from 1998 to 2022, the circumstances of each period, and how today differs

KOENJAZHES

On November 26 the Bank of Korea raised its base rate from 3.00% to 3.25%. The same day, Seoul apartment prices rose just 0.01% for the week. "Rates up, prices down" is only half true. Prices fell sharply in 2022, yet in 2006 they rose strongly while rates were climbing. This article goes through the statistics of past high-rate periods, the circumstances at the time, how today differs, and the outlook for next year. The outlook is a set of conditional scenarios and does not predict a fall or a rise. It also checks the double-digit rates of 1980, 1997-98 and 2008 by indicator, and examines the chance that the low rates of 2020 return, prolonged high rates and the housing-bubble debate, with grounds and counterarguments.

What the market is signaling now

In the Korea Real Estate Board survey for the fourth week of November (as of the 23rd), Seoul apartment prices rose 0.01% on the week, extending the streak to 94 weeks, but the gain was smaller than the previous 0.03%. Songpa (-0.50%), Gangnam (-0.34%) and Seocho (-0.30%) kept falling, and Gwangjin and Yeongdeungpo each slipped 0.01%, turning negative. Seongbuk (0.33%), Nowon (0.27%) and Seodaemun (0.25%) rose. Prices are splitting by district. Jeonse (lump-sum deposit) rents rose 0.14% in both Seoul and the metropolitan area, holding the previous pace. The bond market shows a similar pattern. On the 26th the gap between three-year and ten-year Korean Treasury yields narrowed to 16.8bp, the smallest since March 2025. In the video, an economist said the long-short yield gap moves ahead of the economy and has been shrinking fast, and read that as the bond market expecting slower growth next year. But that day's narrowing came largely from short-term yields rising after the dot plot hinted at more hikes. Signs of slowdown and expectations of tightening are mixed, so the signal is hard to read one way. The September leading index (cyclical component) from the National Data Agency fell 0.1 point to 104.1, its first drop in 11 months, pulled down by the KOSPI and the yield gap.

The record: five periods when rates met home prices

In the 1998 foreign-exchange crisis, Seoul apartment prices fell 14.6% from the end of the previous year (Korea Research Institute for Human Settlements). From October 2005 to August 2007 the call-rate target rose from 3.25% to 5.00%, yet Seoul apartments rose 23.46% in 2006 (Korea Real Estate Board historical series). In the 2008 global financial crisis, Seoul apartment transaction prices fell 10.21% for the year. Seoul apartment prices then fell four years in a row from 2010 to 2013, by 4.5% in 2012 (KB) and 1.28% in 2013 (Korea Appraisal Board). The base rate went from 0.50% in August 2021 to 3.50% in January 2023. For 2022 the Real Estate Board reported apartment price falls of 7.56% nationally, 9.68% in the metropolitan area and 7.70% in Seoul, while the transaction-price index fell 22.09% in Seoul and 16.84% nationally, the largest drop since the series began in 2006. Actual deal prices fell far more than the official price index. 2008 asks the same question. The Bank of Korea raised its rate from 5.00% to 5.25% on August 7, 2008, then cut it six times, from October 9 through February 2009, to 2.00%, a total of 3.25 percentage points. The economist in the video cited this case: the hike came when growth was good and prices were rising, and a financial crisis followed soon after, which he used to stress how uncertain the future is. By the record, the first cut came about two months after the hike, so the direction flipped within two months.

What older generations remember: which indicators carried the 10% and 30% rates

The double-digit rates that older generations lived through differed widely by indicator, so the indicator and date are separated here. First, the 30% range of 1980 was the interest on a government-promoted savings product. In January 1980 the Ministry of Finance raised rates on the workers' asset-building savings plan (jaehyeong savings) so that the annual yield including subsidies ran from 27.4% for one year to 33.5% for three years (Maeil Business, January 14, 1980). That is the rate on a product with set eligibility and preferential terms, not an ordinary bank deposit. Second, the 30% range of the 1997-98 foreign exchange crisis was a financial-market rate. On December 26, 1997 the average rate in the on-exchange call market was 31.74% a year, and 29.06% on the 27th (Bank of Korea figures, JoongAng Ilbo, December 27). On December 30 the corporate bond secondary yield passed 30% (Hankook Ilbo, December 31), and on December 23 the commercial paper (CP) secondary yield reached 38.36% (Munhwa Ilbo, December 24). The monthly average call rate (overnight) peaked at 25.34% in January 1998 (compiled from Bank of Korea economic statistics). Ordinary loans were lower. The record average rate on new bank loans was 17.01% in January 1998 and the record time-deposit rate was 17.85% the same month (compiled from Bank of Korea statistics). The Bank of Korea's average bank lending rate (excluding overdrafts) was 16.47% in June 1998 and 14.93% in August (Hankook Ilbo). The 30% loans were overdraft loans to some companies: in January 1998 the Bank of Korea told banks to cut overdraft rates, which had reached 33% a year, by 2 to 3 percentage points (Maeil Business, January 23). The three-year Treasury yield also stayed well above 15% in 1998 (KDI Economic Information and Education Center). The policy-rate regime (call rate targeting) only began in May 1999, so there is no base rate series of the same name for that period. Third, in the 2008 financial crisis the policy rate and lending rates moved in different directions. The base rate peaked at 5.25% on August 7 and began to fall on October 9 (Bank of Korea base rate history). But under the credit squeeze, the average new mortgage rate hit 7.58% in October, the highest since the series began in September 2001 (compiled from Bank of Korea statistics). The top fixed mortgage rate at commercial banks passed 10% for the first time in early October (Yonhap, October 5). Compared with today, the average mortgage rate of 4.88% in September 2026 is the highest since April 2012, and the fixed-rate average passed 5% for the first time since the series began (Bank of Korea). It is still below the 7.58% of October 2008 and far from 1998. Part of why current rates feel high is the memory of the low rates of 2020.

The circumstances: why the same rate hikes gave different results

In 2022 the problem was speed. Rates rose 3.00 percentage points in about a year and a half, which cut buyers' borrowing power and raised the interest bills of existing borrowers. At the end of February 2022, household loan balances at the five major banks had fallen two months in a row, the first time since 2013. As deals dried up, urgent sales pulled prices down, and in December 2022 national housing prices fell 1.98% from the month before, the largest monthly drop since the series began in 2003. In 2006, by contrast, demand and expectations of further gains were strong, so prices rose even as rates rose. The 1998 fall came less from interest rates than from corporate restructuring, mass unemployment and a collapse in incomes. In short, rate hikes alone did not cause crashes. The falls were deepest when speed, income shocks, a freeze in transactions and weak price expectations came together.

How today differs from 2022

First, speed. The Bank raised rates by a total of 0.50 percentage points in July and August this year to 3.00% and added 0.25 in November. That is gentler than the cumulative 3.00 points of 2021-2023. Second, loan rates. The average new mortgage rate at banks rose from 4.32% in May to 4.66% in August. The video from Chaebusim notes that average mortgage rates are close to 5% and the highest exceed 7%. Third, the economy. The Bank of Korea raised its growth forecast for this year from 3.3% to 3.9% and put next year at 4.1%. That is unlike 1998, when restructuring and unemployment cut incomes. The same video also points out that private consumption is weak and construction investment is sluggish. High growth does not settle what a buyer can afford in interest. Fourth, supply. From January to August Seoul apartment completions fell 23.3% while starts rose 34.3%. Next year's metropolitan-area move-ins are about 120,000 homes, 72% of the ten-year average. One weight on the other side: metropolitan apartment prices have risen 6.65% this year (as of November 16), the steepest since 2021, so rate-sensitive areas have more room to correct. The economist in the video noted that the Bank of Korea's forecast leans heavily on semiconductor exports, while stock prices and the yield gap point to slower activity next year, a wide gap between the two. The Korea Economic Daily also reported that next year's growth depends on the chip cycle more than this year's. Samsung Electronics and SK hynix have fallen about 55% on average from their peaks (JoongAng Ilbo). The economist explained that when stocks fall, households have less room to spend and save, which links to the worry of a weaker wealth effect. That is why the stock market is a variable in home-price outlooks. Two of seven board members also voted to hold this time, another sign of split views.

The low rates of 2020 are unlikely to return

The Bank of Korea cut its base rate by 0.50 percentage points at an emergency meeting on March 16, 2020, and lowered it to a record-low 0.50% on May 28 (Maeil Business). In May that year the average new mortgage rate was 2.52%, and it fell to 2.39% in August, the lowest on record (Yonhap; compiled from Bank of Korea statistics). The monthly average overnight call rate bottomed at 0.48% in June 2020. That was a period when the pandemic cut growth and prices together. Conditions differ now. The Bank of Korea raised rates on November 26, saying growth had strengthened and inflation was expected to stay above target for some time. Its consumer price forecasts are 2.7% for 2026, 2.4% for 2027 and 2.1% for 2028 (Bank of Korea Economic Outlook). Fifteen of 21 dots in the dot plot sit above the current 3.25%. The US Federal Reserve also raised its rate to 3.75-4.00% on September 16, 2026, its first increase in three years and two months, since July 2023 (Yonhap). A securities-firm expert who appeared on the Chosun Ilbo Money channel (October 19) described the pandemic period as exceptional and said long-term yields are more likely to stay near current levels for now than to return to earlier levels. This article judges that 2020-level rates are unlikely to return in the near term. Some expect cuts in the second half of next year, so cuts are not ruled out.

The case for prolonged high rates, and the case against

This magazine considers it likely that high rates will last longer than expected. This is a view, not a certainty. The grounds run on three tracks. First, monetary policy. The Bank of Korea raised rates on November 26, and 15 of 21 dots in its dot plot sit above the current rate. Consumer inflation is forecast to approach target only in 2028. Second, long-term yields. The US 10-year Treasury yield reached 5.364% intraday on October 7, a 24-year high, and the term premium, the extra reward for holding long bonds, rose to a 12-year high (JoongAng Ilbo, citing the Wall Street Journal). The securities-firm expert above explained the rise as driven more by fiscal conditions and bond supply and demand than by policy-rate increases. Third, domestic fiscal policy. The 2027 plan for Treasury bond issuance is 222.8 trillion won, 2.9 trillion won below this year's 225.7 trillion, but net new issuance is 96.3 trillion won (Yonhap Infomax, Etoday). The same expert held that welfare, interest and strategic-industry spending are rising structurally and that the supply burden attaches to yields. The issuance figures he cited (about 210 trillion won this year and around 220 trillion next year) differ from the government plan, so the plan figures are used here. The Bank of Korea estimated that a 0.25 percentage point rise in the base rate raises household interest costs by 3.3 trillion won and corporate costs by 3.7 trillion won (Yonhap, September 22). There are counterarguments. First, the dot plot is a conditional forecast and two board members voted to hold. The economist cited earlier expects a cut in the second half of next year. Second, long-term high rates do not necessarily mean high real rates persist. In the US the Fed kept a cap on Treasury yields from 1942 to 1951, and the government debt ratio fell from 106% in 1946 to 23% in 1974 (IMF Working Paper 24/5). The Bank of Japan also directly controlled long-term yields from September 2016 to March 2024. An analyst presented in a video the scenario that, as government interest burdens grow, nominal rates may stay high but be held below inflation, a hypothesis with precedent. Third, current mortgage rates are below those of 1998 and October 2008.

The housing-bubble debate and scenarios

This magazine regards the present as a historical period in which home prices carry a bubble. The grounds are as follows. In its September financial stability report the Bank of Korea assessed that financial imbalances are building from rising home prices and household loans, and its financial vulnerability index rose to 46.5 at the end of the second quarter, to the long-term average. The ratio of household debt to nominal GDP was 85.3% at the end of the first quarter, above the long-term average of 84.0% (Yonhap). The price-to-income ratio (PIR, median price basis) for Seoul apartments rose from 12.87 in June 2025 to 15.67 in October 2026 by R2 Korea Real Estate Advisory's count (Yonhap Infomax). There are counterarguments. PIR differs by source. The Ministry of Land's housing survey shows PIR for owner-occupied Seoul households falling from 13.9 to 11.7, though a change in the sample toward lower-priced homes may be a factor (Yonhap), and KB Real Estate's Seoul PIR was 10.0 in the second quarter, 0.21 lower than the previous quarter. That decline is also read as the effect of more purchases of low- and mid-priced apartments (Korea Economic Daily). The household debt ratio is expected to fall to about 81% at the end of the second quarter (Bank of Korea). Even within Seoul, prices diverge, with the three Gangnam districts falling and outer areas rising. Whether there is a bubble, and how long high rates will last, is not yet settled by the statistics. As scenarios: if high rates continue and loan rules stay in place, an adjustment path becomes more likely. If rates fall and the shortage of new supply supports prices, the flat or re-rise paths remain. In either case, this article's view is that price formation premised on ultra-low rates like those of 2020 is difficult.

Outlook for next year: three paths

Institutional forecasts lean upward. The Construction & Economy Research Institute expects national home prices to rise 2.0% and jeonse prices 4.5% next year, and major brokerages and research institutes expect metropolitan prices up 3-5% and jeonse up about 8%. On that basis the editors lay out three paths. First, a correction path: if loan rates rise further, lending rules stay in place and stocks stay weak, corrections in high-priced areas such as the three Gangnam districts and in heavily leveraged areas spread to neighbors along the Han River. Second, a flat path: gains hover near zero, deals thin out and buyers who wait stay in jeonse and monthly rent. KB Real Estate reported that monthly rent makes up 70% of rental deals. Third, a renewed-rise path: a shortage of move-ins and income expectations around the semiconductor belt support prices again. A broad 2022-style fall is the path where rate hikes speed up and an income shock arrives together, and today's indicators do not show those conditions. The Chaebusim video sees high loan rates as downward pressure that blocks money from reaching property through next year, but says property would also get room to breathe if stocks improve. It reads the fork between the correction and renewed-rise paths through the same variables. Which path prevails should be checked against the indicators below. Views on the rate path differ. The economist in the video says this is the last hike of the cycle and the Bank of Korea could cut in the second half of next year. The Bank's six-month conditional forecast (dot plot), released the same day, had 7 of 21 dots at 3.75%, 8 at 3.50% and 6 at 3.25%, so 15 sit above the current rate. Brokerage forecasts for the final rate also range from 3.25% to 3.75% (Money Today). The cut forecast is one expert's personal view and points the opposite way from the dot plot. He sees the first half of next year as the hardest period and says those with cash on hand may find an opportunity, which is his opinion. Applied to home prices, the editors note that earlier expectations of cuts would favor the flat or re-rise paths, and further hikes would favor the adjustment path.

An article within the article: the mistaken idea that more M2 means higher home prices

"More liquidity lifts home prices" is an old belief. In the 1988-2026 table kept in Notion, Seoul apartment prices and M2 do move in the same direction over the long run. But in the same table M2 growth was never negative in 39 years, while Seoul prices fell in 11 of them: 1992, 1993, 1998, 2004, 2008, 2010-2013, 2022 and 2023. In 2022 M2 grew 5.3% and Seoul still fell 7.2% (table basis; the official apartment statistic shows a 7.70% fall). The video sees M2 as a figure that bundles corporate and household loans, so the money that actually enters housing depends on where household mortgage lending goes. Long-run levels and year-by-year direction should be read separately.

Indicators to watch

Watch Seoul's weekly rise and the number of falling districts in the Korea Real Estate Board survey, the Bank of Korea's new-loan household rates, the monthly increase in household loans, transaction volume, jeonse growth and metropolitan move-in volume. The first signal is the weekly rate turning from zero to negative and falling districts spreading beyond the Han River belt. Add the gap between three-year and ten-year Treasury yields, the leading index (cyclical component) in the National Data Agency's industrial activity release, and the Bank of Korea's dot plot and board remarks, and shifts in the rate direction can be read earlier. This article is not investment advice and does not recommend buying, selling or borrowing.
