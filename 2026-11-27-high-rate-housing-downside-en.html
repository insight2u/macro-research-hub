The high-rate era is here: what 2022 shows about a short, sharp fall in home prices

Not a confirmed decline, but the size of the risk as recorded in past data

KOENJAZHES

On November 26 the Bank of Korea raised its base rate from 3.00% to 3.25%. The same day, Korea Real Estate Board data showed Seoul apartment prices rising just 0.01% for the week, on the edge of flat. The record of how home prices behave when rates rise already exists. This piece uses the closest case, 2022, to test the chance of a short, sharp fall next year. It is a scenario, and it does not claim a decline is certain.

The signal the market is sending now

In the Korea Real Estate Board survey for the fourth week of November (as of the 23rd), Seoul apartment prices rose 0.01% from the previous week. The weekly gain has shrunk for six weeks from 0.13% in the third week of October, and it is the lowest since the third week of January last year. Songpa fell 0.50%, Gangnam 0.34% and Seocho 0.30%, and the three Gangnam districts have fallen for 12 straight weeks. Nine of Seoul's 25 districts declined. Seoul as a whole still holds its streak of 94 weekly gains, but the momentum has clearly weakened.

The record from the last high-rate period: 2022

The Bank of Korea began raising rates in August 2021 from a record low of 0.5% and reached 3.5% in January 2023. In 2022, within that span, Korea Real Estate Board data show apartment prices fell 7.56% nationwide, 9.68% in the Seoul metropolitan area and 7.70% in Seoul. Sejong fell 17.12% and Daegu 12.38%. Yonhap reported that the annual apartment decline was the largest since the 1997 currency crisis, and that nationwide housing prices fell 1.98% in December 2022, the biggest monthly drop since records began in 2003. The Seoul apartment transaction-price index fell more than 20%, the largest drop since the survey began in 2006. Actual deal prices fell further than the official index.

How 2022 differs from today

In 2022 the shock was a rate rising fast from 0.5%. Now it is one more step, from 3.00% to 3.25%. The starting burden is higher today, and the speed of the shock was greater then. The YouTube commentator Chaebusim noted that average mortgage rates are close to 5% and the highest exceed 7%. The Bank of Korea raised its growth forecast to 3.9% this year and 4.1% next year, a sign the economy is holding up, but the video also pointed to weak private consumption and construction investment. High growth does not settle what a home buyer can afford in interest.

An article within the article: the mistaken belief that more M2 lifts home prices

The idea that more liquidity lifts home prices is an old belief. In the Notion table for 1988 to 2026, Seoul apartment prices and M2 do move in the same direction over the long run. But in the same table M2 growth was never negative in 39 years, while Seoul prices fell in 11 of them: 1992, 1993, 1998, 2004, 2008, 2010 to 2013, 2022 and 2023. In 2022 M2 grew 5.3% and Seoul still fell 7.2% (table basis; official apartment data show 7.70%). The video treats M2 as the shadow of private credit. It combines corporate and household loans, so what reaches the housing market depends on how much household mortgage credit grows and where it is allowed to go. That reading is the video maker's claim. The editors do not say M2 is meaningless. They only note that the long-run level and the year-by-year direction should be read separately.

What has to hold for a short, sharp fall

The video argues that high loan rates through next year will block money from flowing into property, and that funds tied up in stocks cannot reach housing. For that scenario to work, loan rates must stay high, Seoul lending curbs must stay in place and stocks must not recover quickly. The video itself says that if stocks improve next year, property also gets room to breathe. Strong growth and the long-run upward trend could also continue. A fall is more likely to show up as a price correction in overheated areas, and even in 2022 the whole country did not fall at the same pace.

Indicators for readers to check

Watch the weekly Korea Real Estate Board figures for Seoul's rate of change and the number of falling districts, household loan rates on new lending from the Bank of Korea, monthly household loan growth and transaction volume. The first signal is whether the weekly rate turns from zero to negative and the falling areas spread beyond the Han River belt. This piece is not investment advice to buy, sell or borrow.
