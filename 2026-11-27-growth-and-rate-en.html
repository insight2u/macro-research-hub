Growth forecasts rose on the day interest rates did

A 3.25% policy rate and a 4.1% growth forecast measure different things

KOENJAZHES

On November 26, the Bank of Korea raised its policy rate from 3.00% to 3.25% and lifted its growth forecasts to 3.9% for 2026 and 4.1% for 2027. The decision came amid a sharp stock-market correction. To understand that combination, we need to stop treating asset prices and forecasts for the real economy as one measure of confidence.

Strength and anxiety can exist in different places

The bank expects exports and investment to support growth, while improved income conditions and higher fiscal spending help consumption. In international financial markets, however, concerns about returns on AI investment have increased risk aversion. Share prices incorporate expectations about distant profits and discount rates. A growth forecast estimates the increase in production over a period. Combining these different measures into a single score for the economy obscures more than it explains.

Growth was not the only reason for the increase

The policy statement also cited inflation likely to remain above target for a considerable period and financial-stability risks. The bank forecasts consumer-price inflation of 2.4% for 2027 and core inflation excluding food and energy of 2.6%. It sees both cost pressures passing into prices and pressure from demand. This does not mean every price will rise by the same amount, or that next year's outcome is already fixed.

Household interest bills adjust with a lag

A 0.25-percentage-point policy increase does not raise every loan rate by that amount on the same day. The effect depends on fixed or floating terms, benchmark market rates, reset dates and the contract. For borrowers, the next reset date and discount conditions in their own agreement matter more than a news headline about average rates. A stronger growth forecast does not immediately reduce a household's interest payment.

Read the conditions behind a higher forecast

The outlook identifies strong semiconductor exports and their spillovers as important drivers, while also noting uncertainty around global AI investment and geopolitics. The editors will watch how widely export gains reach wages, jobs and consumption. Neither celebrating the forecasts as completed achievements nor dismissing them solely because share prices fell answers that question. Subsequent data must show the connection. This is policy analysis, not investment advice.

Different reference sessions

Korea's November 26 closes were KOSPI 4,711.21 (+0.29%) and KOSDAQ 655.71 (+0.78%). USD/KRW was 1,342.7 at the 15:30 reference, down 0.5 won. The latest row in Cboe's daily VIX source is 28.29 for November 25. These are dated snapshots, not live quotes; the Korean session and the US November 26 stock holiday are separate.
