A week of sharp stock falls: the KOSPI record and a comparison with the 2000 dot-com period

Official market data from November 6 to 27, set against claims made in videos, by date

KOENJAZHES

The KOSPI closed down 6.16% at 4,697.51 on November 25 and up 4.34% at 4,915.68 on the 27th. During a week of daily swings above 6%, many video channels talked about further declines. This article separates confirmed market figures from claims in the videos, and sets out what is similar to and different from the 2000 dot-com period.

This week's market: figures by date

On November 6 the KOSPI closed down 6.02% at 5,059.69. It fell as far as 4,911.28 during the session, a first-stage circuit breaker was triggered, and foreigners net sold about 2.77 trillion won that day (Yonhap, Weekly Donga). It rose 6.32% to 5,006.03 on the 24th but closed down 6.16% at 4,697.51 on the 25th. Foreigners net sold about 1.9 trillion won that day, and this year's sidecar activations reached 102 (Herald Business). On the 26th the Bank of Korea raised the base rate to 3.25% and the KOSPI ended up 0.29% at 4,711.21. On the 27th foreigners net bought 1.1256 trillion won, the KOSPI closed up 4.34% at 4,915.68 and the KOSDAQ up 8.13% at 708.99. Hopes for market-stabilization measures were cited as a background (Block Media, Yonhap).

The fall from the peak

The KOSPI's record close is 9,114.55 on June 22. The close on the 25th was 48.46% below it (Money Today). Calculated the same way, the close of 4,915.68 on the 27th is about 46.1% below the peak. Hyundai Motor Securities put the fall at 46.6% as of the 20th and compared it with 54.5% in the 2008 financial crisis and 55.7% in the 2001 dot-com collapse and September 11 period (Korea Economic Daily).

Checking claims made in videos

One economic channel's video said the index was down about 44.5% from its peak as of the November 6 close. Using the official close (5,059.69), that is correct. The same video's figure of foreign net selling "over 250 trillion won" was not confirmed. Reports citing Korea Exchange data give 223.6348 trillion won on the main market through November 18 (ETNews), and the Financial Supervisory Service gave about 240 trillion won in listed shares through October (KBS). The statement that the National Pension Service raised its domestic equity target to 20.8% matches press reports (Korea Economic Daily). The figures "13 trillion won in six sessions", "33 trillion won in a month" and "36 trillion won for all of 2008" could not be sourced and are left out.

Another video said that since 2005 a two-month fall of more than 20% happened only twice, both in 2008; that was quoted from an article inside the video and was not checked directly. Levels such as 2,600 to 2,800 on the KOSPI offered by a video guest are the guest's own outlook and cannot be confirmed from official data.

What is similar to the 2000 dot-com period

In the United States the comparison kept coming up. On November 25 the Nasdaq fell as much as 4.6% during the session and closed down 0.62% at 23,585.80, and Nvidia fell 3.91% (Maekyung). According to an article citing the Wall Street Journal, Barclays estimated that 25% of U.S. economic growth over the past year came from AI investment, and stocks make up 35% of U.S. household net worth, above 26% at the dot-com peak (Asia Today). A video also said seven AI-related stocks make up about 33% of U.S. market capitalization, but that figure was relayed in the video and was not checked separately. Concentration, investment funded by borrowing and rising rates are the points compared with the dot-com period.

What is different from the 2000 dot-com period

The Nasdaq Composite closed at 5,048.62 on March 10, 2000 and fell about 77.9% to 1,114.11 on October 9, 2002, and it took 15 years to pass the earlier peak (Bubblepedia, FRED series). That decline took two years and seven months. The KOSPI's current fall is about 46 to 48% within five months of its June peak. The pace is different, and it has not reached the final size of the dot-com fall. Whether the present decline will last as long as the dot-com one or recover is not known. In the 1929 case mentioned in a video, the Dow fell nearly 13% on October 28, 1929 (Federal Reserve History); the video said 12%. The period and institutions differ, so a direct reading across to today is difficult.

Indicators to watch and uncertainty

Figures that can be followed are the direction of foreign net trading, whether market-stabilization measures are actually carried out, market rates after the Bank of Korea decision, and borrowing costs tied to U.S. technology stocks and AI investment. Korea Economic Daily reported that a stabilization fund has not actually been used since 2008. When daily swings above 6% continue as they did this week, checking official closing data by date comes first.
