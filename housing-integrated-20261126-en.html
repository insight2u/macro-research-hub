A 3 million won salary and the 2027 jeonse renewal

Housing affordability is a household cash-flow question, not only a property-price debate

Translated reader edition of the integrated Korean feature. The argument, checked figures and limits are preserved in shorter wording.

KO　EN　JA　ZH　

The salary is not trivial; the price benchmark is distant

The National Data Office reports average monthly employment income of 3.75 million won and median income of 2.88 million won in December 2024. A monthly salary of 3 million or 5 million won should not be dismissed. These are employee-job income statistics, not total household income, take-home pay or an exact percentile for either salary.

Divide one billion won by income

For an illustrative home price of one billion won, monthly income of 3 million and 5 million gives annual income of 36 million and 60 million won. Saving everything would take about 27.8 and 16.7 years; saving 20% would take about 138.9 and 83.3 years. This unrealistic example assumes no tax, living costs, interest, existing assets, borrowing or family help, and unchanged prices and wages. It is not an official PIR or an observed average Seoul price.

The survey also shows a high threshold

The 2024 Housing Survey gives owner-household median price-to-income ratios of 6.3 nationally. Yonhap’s report gives 13.9 for Seoul and 8.7 for the capital region. These are 2024 household survey figures, not 2026 asking prices or the actual saving period of a first-time buyer.

A house-price gain is not everyone’s income gain

Some owners can benefit from valuation gains and consume more. It is therefore wrong to say every rise hurts every person at every moment. But new buyers, growing households and parents helping children face the next higher bill. More expensive exchanges of existing homes do not automatically mean an equal increase in goods and services.

Monthly cash matters more than the valuation screen

A Bank of Korea study released in November 2025 models how accumulated household credit may have constrained consumption. In its counterfactual,2024 private consumption could have been 4.9–5.4% higher had household debt been managed at its 2012 level. This is a conditional model result, not a directly counted loss caused solely by house prices.

1.67 million won of interest beside a 3 million salary

A hypothetical 500 million won loan at 4% costs 20 million won annual interest, about 1.667 million monthly, before principal and fees. This is not a current loan quote or a claim that this income qualifies for such borrowing. Debt payments can reduce money available for shops, books, travel and children.

The case for normalizing holding taxes

This column argues for restoring the role of recurrent property holding costs. Public transport, education, safety and infrastructure contribute to location value. A persistent gap between asset value and its public cost can favour waiting with property over earning through work. No single tax rate is presented as the answer, and taxes alone do not guarantee falling prices.

International research offers more than a tax increase

The OECD’s 2022 Housing Taxation report discusses updated property values, a stronger role for recurrent taxes and lower transaction taxes. Old valuations can distort fairness and mobility. It does not establish Korea’s 2026 international tax ranking; such comparisons need the same tax scope and denominator.

Protect cash-poor owners and consider tenants

Low-income older homeowners face genuine liquidity constraints. Deferral or instalments can be considered instead of blanket relief for all valuable assets. Deferral is not exemption, and a specific design needs legal review. Rent pass-through depends on market conditions; neither full nor zero pass-through should be assumed.

Affordability, not a disorderly collapse

Normalization should narrow the gap from income, rents and housing services, not abandon vulnerable borrowers or financial stability. Supply, locations, public rental housing and tenant security belong in the same discussion.

Respect the weight of wages

A gain on one home is not synonymous with national prosperity. When debt and housing costs absorb spending, tenants, small businesses, companies and the next generation lose options. Policy should let earned income sustain ordinary life.

Three videos, different interpretations

The three additional videos checked on November 25 evening do not reach one conclusion. The first emphasizes auctions and debt-service pressure; the second warns Gangnam weakness may not spread uniformly and discusses renewal costs; the third predicts further high-end adjustment from transactions and a cash-flow model. We read supplied transcripts and compared the material below, not watched every minute. Forecasts are not adopted as facts.

3,851 auctions is not every price collapsing

Financial News cites GG Auction’s October 2026 report:3,851 apartment auction proceedings nationally, the most since 4,063 in March 2014, not an all-time record. The national sale rate was 33.9% and price-to-appraisal ratio 85.2%; Seoul had 261 proceedings,21.8% and 100.1%. In Seoul the latter ratio was 103.6% for appraisals at or below 1.5 billion won and 97.1% at or above 2.5 billion. The share sold and the price among successful sales are different measures.

At 5%,600 million won costs 2.5 million monthly interest

For interest only,600 million won costs 1.5 million per month at 3%,2.5 million at 5%,and 3 million at 6%. Do not pair 5% and 3 million under the same assumptions. Actual debt service depends on term and repayment method. Unverified video estimates of vulnerable borrowers and balances are omitted.

Gangnam is not the Seoul average

Hankyoreh’s November 19 report of Korea Real Estate Board data says Seoul apartment prices rose 0.03% in the week to November 16,with seven districts declining. Weakness in some high-end areas and gains in cheaper outer areas coexisted. This is that week’s index, not all November 25 transactions. Individual deals need area, floor, date, cancellations and related-party checks; a peak-to-one-sale difference is not an entire complex’s fall.

A tax proposal is not enacted law

The government’s August 3 tax-reform explanation proposes raising the assessed-value deduction for one owner-occupied home from 1.2 billion to 1.4 billion won while increasing burdens on ultra-expensive or nonresident properties. Final passage, promulgation and implementation are not established here. It must not be treated as the current final rate. Video tax examples may be conditional simulations.

Renewal bills belong in the discussion

The second video asks what deposit and rent tenants must afford next. Renewal-right use, negotiated terms and local supply must be examined. Unverified rental-growth, moving-household and future-supply figures are not repeated. Sales-price stability, rent burdens, repayment capacity and reliable rental supply need joint scrutiny.

2027 turns the price debate into a bill

Nonowners meet the same market through renewal deposits and rent. Policy success should include the additional cash needed, rights actually available and monthly money left, not only a sale-price index.

5% is not an automatic increase

The Housing Lease Protection Act provides a one-time renewal request and a two-year renewed term. Increases under the relevant provisions are subject to Article 7’sone-twentieth ceiling. This does not require every contract to rise 5%. Distinguish a renewal using the right from a newly agreed contract without using it. A 2023 contract and 2025 recontract alone do not establish every household’s 2027 legal position.

Calculate scenarios, not a forecast

Additional deposit equals the next agreed deposit minus the old deposit. A hypothetical 500 million deposit becoming 600 million requires 100 million more;700 million requires 200 million more. These are examples, not 2027 prices. Required borrowing also depends on cash, existing-loan terms and new credit approval. Compare monthly rent and other costs.

A complex name is not a matched comparison

Official transaction services provide dates, areas, floors and new/renewed contract information, including relevant renewal-right and former-deposit fields. Match those before comparing. Asking prices differ from concluded prices. Apartment rental data omit unit identifiers for privacy, so two public transactions cannot be presumed to follow one household. Future 2027 prices remain assumptions or forecasts.

Expiry does not automatically mean eviction

The law provides tacit renewal under conditions and continued lease relations pending deposit return in relevant cases. Notice dates, exercised rights, wording and facts affect each contract. A market forecast cannot decide an individual tenant’s legal position.

Reduce the shock of moving

Measure new and renewed rental burdens separately, improve deposit-return and dispute information, and widen rental options linked to work, education and transport. These are policy proposals, not predictions of a price surge or mass eviction. Preparation needs verified contracts, applicable rights and affordable funding on one table.
