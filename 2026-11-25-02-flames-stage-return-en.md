# Beyond the film's twist: encountering Incendies on stage

November 25, 2026 edition | Theatre and film | Performance dates: November 14 to December 6, 2026

Two letters left by a mother unsettle everything her family thought it knew. Her will asks that one be delivered to a father and the other to a sibling the twins never knew existed. Their journey with those letters is the starting point shared by the play Incendies, staged in Korea as Hwayeom, and the film released in Korea as Geueullin Sarang.

The November 24 culture segment of News Factory recommended seeing the play without first looking up its central revelations. That advice respects the audience's discovery. This article likewise avoids the ending and the secret relationships. Instead, it considers why the same story can raise different questions in cinema and theatre.

## Returning from film to play

The film's English title is Incendies. The description accompanying the official trailer published by distributor Sony Pictures Classics identifies it as an adaptation of Wajdi Mouawad's play. The film did not come first. The original play became familiar to many people through cinema.

In the distributor's description, twins Jeanne and Simon set out to investigate their family history after receiving their mother Nawal's will. Seoul's official tourism information gives the current production the same starting point. However, the two descriptions differ in how they describe the duration of her silence. It is more accurate not to transfer a detail from the film's description directly to the stage production.

This is more than an exercise in spotting a small discrepancy. Adaptations can share their central events while arranging characters and time differently. Rather than expecting remembered film scenes to appear unchanged on stage, audiences can ask how a familiar question is presented through another form.

## What waiting only for the twist can obscure

There is a paradox in works known for a shocking ending. Once viewers watch only for that ending, the characters' words and silences may shrink into a list of clues. Alongside “Who is related to whom?” another question matters: “How did a person survive what happened?”

The official film description also stresses the consequences of extremism and violence in an individual life. A family secret brings war out of distant international news and into memory and relationships. That is why the work is difficult to introduce as a mystery alone.

A News Factory contributor described the closeness of actors and audience in the current staging and praised Ye Soo-jung's performance for conveying a character's strength within tragedy. That is the contributor's assessment, not a review based on this publication attending the production, nor an experience guaranteed to every viewer. It can nevertheless be read as a suggestion that even audiences who know the ending may find a separate point of interest in the moment an actor speaks or falls silent.

## Before deciding to attend

Seoul's culture portal and official tourism information list the production at Daehakro Theatre QUAD from November 14 through December 6. The regular schedule is 7:30 p.m. on weekdays and 3 p.m. on weekends, with no Monday performances. The opening-day exception has already passed and should not be confused with the regular schedule.

The theatre's own information lists admission for ages 16 and above (high-school students and older), with a running time of 190 minutes. It warns of content involving war, violence, torture and sexual violence. Some seats may have a partially obstructed view, so read the seating notes when booking.

The listed standard price is KRW 50,000 for all seats. Discounts have different eligibility and documentation requirements. The early-booking discount that ended in early October should not be presented as still available. A published schedule and price do not confirm that seats remain for a particular date. Check the actual performance, availability, age rules and discount conditions before booking.

The work deals with war, violence and wounds within a family. Rather than recommending it casually as entertainment with a shocking twist, it is better to choose with an understanding of that weight. New audiences may experience discovery; those who have seen the film may hear the story anew through actors' bodies and voices. In either case, the centre of the experience may lie less in solving the twist than in following a person who lived in silence as she leaves her story behind.
