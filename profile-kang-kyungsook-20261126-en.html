26 November 2026 | DailyShot Innovation News | Current and former lawmakers, profile 4

Special education is the starting point for reading Kang Kyung-sook's parliamentary work. Experience as a teacher, education researcher and professor has led to proposals asking who misses out on classroom support. In the digital-textbook debate, she has also demanded evidence from government. Those positions need not conflict. The test of her expertise is whether she applies the evidence standards she demands from others to her own surveys and legislative record.

This profile starts with NamuWiki's career and legislative entries, then compares official biographies and bill records, a publisher's account of a jointly authored book, public YouTube subtitles, the corresponding official parliamentary minutes and reporting. It is not based on reading the entire book or independently interviewing individual students.

Teacher, researcher and professor, then a first-term lawmaker

The Assembly biography lists work as a special-education teacher at Eunpyeong Elementary School from 1993 to 2000, an education researcher at the National Institute of Special Education from 2000 to 2007 and a professor of secondary special education at Wonkwang University from 2007. It also records roles with national education and disability policy bodies and a research professorship at the University of Washington. The current party roster places her on the Education Committee.[1][2]

NamuWiki describes her teaching and research career, unsuccessful proportional-representation candidacy in 2020 and election in 2024 as number 11 on the Rebuilding Korea Party list. Private family and residential information is unnecessary here and is not reproduced. An anecdote presented by the wiki as a motive for entering politics is not treated as an established motive without separately checking the subject's original account.[3]

The number of titles is not the main point. Teaching a student, studying policy, training teachers and writing law involve different responsibilities. Identifying a classroom need does not immediately create a system every school can deliver. Staff, budgets and regional conditions still need connecting.

The jointly authored book: from assessment to support across life stages

Hakjisa's official description lists the third edition of Education of Students with Intellectual Disabilities, a descriptive translation of the Korean title, as jointly written by ten authors including Song Jun-man and Kang. Published on 25 March 2022, its description and contents cover diagnosis, assessment and placement, individualized education plans, teaching strategies, communication, behavioral support and adult education.[4]

It is not her sole-authored book. A public description cannot establish her authorship of every chapter or responsibility for every detailed argument. The verified points are coauthorship and the book's scope.

The publisher emphasizes evidence-based practice, research support and cautions when applying methods. That provides questions for her current work: which support helps which students, and on what evidence? Are identification of need and safeguards against labeling or excessive intervention designed together?

The first bill: official progress matters more than a nickname

The wiki and 2024 coverage identify a bill supporting students in emotional and behavioral crisis as her first proposal. In an Aju Business Daily interview, Kang said school difficulties could not be resolved by counseling teachers or support agencies alone and called for teachers, specialists and parents to work together.[3][5]

Bill 2200110 was introduced by Kang and 16 others on 4 June 2024. The government portal records referral to Education on 11 June and introduction at a meeting and subcommittee referral on 24 July. The page did not establish passage or promulgation, so the proposal is not described as an implemented system.[6]

Its summary covers responsibilities of state and local authorities, five-year plans, support committees, selection through diagnostic assessment, specialist teachers and centers. Its rationale identifies the burden and inconsistency of relying on individual teachers.

Calling it simply a law controlling problem students misses its support purpose. Calling it merely well-intentioned misses rights questions in diagnosis and selection. Assessment should follow actual provisions, amendments, staffing and funding, not a nickname. This profile checked the public summary, not every remedy provision in the complete bill.

Who supports students with sensory disabilities in mainstream schools?

Bill 2219042, introduced on 21 May 2026, would amend special-education legislation. Its official rationale explains that learning Braille, sign language and assistive technology requires sustained instruction by skilled teachers, many of whom work in special schools, limiting support for students elsewhere.[7]

The proposal would create a legal basis for special schools for visual or hearing disabilities to provide itinerant instruction to relevant students in mainstream schools. The record checked shows Education referral on 22 May and consideration at a full committee meeting on 16 September. Introduction is not passage.

The proposal treats inclusion as more than placement in the same building. The test is whether students receive the teaching and communication support they need. But legal authority and adequate staffing are different. Travel time, caseloads and gaps created inside special schools also need examination. These are implementation questions, not claims that the proposal has already caused such problems.

A November forum should not become one politician's personal achievement

Slow Learner News reported on a special-support education forum held in Parliament on 5 November, describing interview research by a disability-education organization and cases of infringed educational rights. It reported concerns that registered disability categories and test scores do not encompass diverse needs. Kang participated through the parliamentary research group Eyes of the Vulnerable.[8][9]

That must not become a claim that she personally investigated every case or resolved the problems. Researchers, presenters, co-organizers and participating lawmakers have different roles. The number of cases presented is not a nationwide count of harm.

Read alongside her proposals, the question is whether diagnostic selection connects support while excluding students who do not fit the criteria. Staff expertise and wider eligibility should complement each other. A researcher-lawmaker must go beyond conveying testimony to explain which criteria and resources should change.

What survives comparison of YouTube subtitles with official minutes?

Public video subtitles about a digital-textbook survey provide context but contain errors in figures and speaker identification. This profile did not quote them alone; it compared Kang's questions and then education minister Lee Ju-ho's responses with the official Education Committee minutes of 18 December 2024.[10][11]

In the minutes, Kang argued in substance that a response count exceeding 100,000 made a sampling-bias objection difficult. The minister raised representativeness and possible duplicate responses. Kang responded that the ministry's survey of trained lead teachers also had representativeness limits. She also demanded objective evidence for personalized learning and narrowing educational gaps.

Those arguments need separate assessment. Requiring evidence of effectiveness and safety from government matters. But a large response count does not eliminate selection bias. Who had an opportunity to encounter and join the survey, how participants differ from nonparticipants, and how duplicates and weighting were handled all matter.

The American Association for Public Opinion Research's report on nonprobability sampling explains recruitment, voluntary selection, assumptions and adjustment. It is a methodological reference, not a direct audit of this survey. On that standard, neither a ministry survey of lead teachers nor a voluntary lawmaker-sponsored survey can be assumed to represent all parents and teachers merely because of its size.[12]

A policy position and methodological accuracy are different issues. Respondents' concerns should not be dismissed, but their proportions cannot simply be assigned to everyone nationwide. Evidence used to scrutinize government deserves particular care.

Opposition to technology, or questions about conditions for learning?

Newsis reported in June 2025 that Kang and education organizations sought a comprehensive review of digital textbooks, their conversion into educational materials schools could choose voluntarily and recalculation of fees. Percentages in that report describe respondents to the particular survey, not national estimates independently calculated here.[13]

In a March 2026 EToday contribution, Kang also acknowledged the need to understand and use new technology, discussing teachers' skills, infrastructure, safety and policy foundations. Kuki News reported on 23 November that she advocated changing teaching and assessment rather than focusing only on preventing cheating.[14][15]

The public record therefore does not support portraying her simply as a consistent opponent of technology itself. She has questioned implementation and preparation while calling for different educational conditions. Whether oral, portfolio and other process-based assessment is fairer, and how workload and private-tutoring inequalities are managed, needs separate evidence. Declaring a new assessment method does not itself reduce inequality.

Expertise must next meet implementation and evidence

The wiki follows a career from classroom to Parliament. Official biographies confirm the foundation; legislative records show the distance between proposals and institutions. Minutes preserve sharp questions to government and statistical limitations in her own argument.

The scorecard is not the label education expert. Are students outside existing categories supported? Does specialist help reach mainstream schools? Do bills produce systems with staff and money? Does she accept rigorous scrutiny of her own surveys?

Special education has long faced a gap between saying there is an opportunity to learn and providing the conditions to do so. The reason to follow her work is how specifically she uses parliamentary authority to narrow that gap.
