A split in October housing indicators: Gangnam down, outer districts up, completed unsold homes remain

Prices, district gaps, unsold homes and permits side by side, from Korea Real Estate Board and Ministry of Land statistics

KOENJAZHES

In the October national housing price trends released on November 16 by the Korea Real Estate Board, Seoul house sale prices rose 0.65%, but Gangnam-gu and Seocho-gu fell. In the Ministry of Land's September statistics, total unsold homes fell while completed unsold homes in the capital area rose. The indicators do not point one way. We set out the official figures with their categories kept apart.

Prices: Seoul up 0.65%, slowing for a third month

In October, Seoul house sale prices across all housing types (apartments, row houses, detached) rose 0.65% from the month before. After 1.09% in July, 0.97% in August and 0.84% in September, that is a third straight slowdown. Seoul apartments alone rose 0.54%, against 0.74% the month before. Gyeonggi rose 0.66%, slightly ahead of Seoul, and the national all-types figure rose 0.28%. All-types and apartments cover different things and should not be mixed in one table.

District gaps: Gangnam -1.24%, Nowon +1.34%

Gangnam-gu fell 1.24%, a bigger drop than the previous month (-1.14%), centered on Apgujeong and Daechi. Seocho-gu fell 0.75%, more than the previous month (-0.60%), and Songpa-gu fell 0.18%. Seodaemun-gu rose 1.35%, Nowon-gu 1.34%, Seongbuk-gu and Gwangjin-gu 1.29%, and Dongdaemun-gu 1.22%. The direction splits even within Seoul. A district average is not the price of an individual complex.

Unsold homes: down overall, but completed units rose in the capital area

According to the Ministry of Land, unsold homes nationwide at the end of September were 65,941, down 4.6% from the month before. The capital area had 18,057 (-5.6%) and the rest of the country 47,884 (-4.2%). Completed but unsold homes were 27,549, down 1.9%. In the capital area, however, completed unsold homes rose 2.6% to 4,482 (Chosun Biz). These figures cover all housing, not only apartments.

Permits and starts: sharp falls in September

In September housing permits were 17,620 in the capital area and 8,318 elsewhere, down 15.2% and 68.9% from a year earlier. Starts were 9,308 and 6,738, down 43.3% and 50.2% (Ministry of Land data as summarized by KDI). Supply figures are the basis for move-in volume some years later, but a single month swings widely.

How to read this

These are records for one month or one quarter. In months with few deals, an index depends on a handful of them. The base rate rose to 3.25% on November 26, and its effect may not yet be fully in the October data. The direction may change in the next release. This article is not investment advice and makes no judgment about trading in any area or complex.

Scope of verification

Figures in this article were checked against public agency statistics and dated press reports. Figures taken from an article are as reported there and may need further checking against the original data. Interpretations give grounds and limits together and are not settled forecasts.
