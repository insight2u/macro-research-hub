Appointing a CEO, activism, and why dividends and cancellations must be told apart

Published November 24, 2026 · Public materials rechecked November 24, 2026 KST

The independent question: Between an activist fund's campaign and the largest shareholder's opposition, KT&G changed its CEO in 2024 and went on to formally promise "5 trillion won in shareholder returns over the next four years." In governance, what changed and what remains?

1. The issue: an old problem in the 24th year of privatization

KT&G has been cited as the textbook case of an "ownerless company" in Korea's capital market. After passing through the Korea Monopoly Corporation and Korea Tobacco & Ginseng Corporation systems and being privatized in 2002, its structure without a controlling shareholder has lasted more than 20 years, and the largest shareholder reported at the time of the 2024 CEO appointment was the state-run Industrial Bank of Korea. The current largest-shareholder ranking is not fixed from that past reporting alone. Since privatization, the chief executive has consistently been an internal appointee (Yonhap News). The absence of a specific controlling shareholder does not mean the board's or institutional investors' checks do not exist. By whose standards management is evaluated and through what procedures it can be replaced — that is this structure's question. The causes of the stock's sluggishness are not pinned on governance alone.

Activist private equity fund Flashlight Capital Partners (FCP) targeted exactly this point. On its own website, FCP scored KT&G's governance "38 out of 100" and problematized the so-called "lost 15 years," saying "KT&G's share price is still at its level of 15 years ago." FCP, which has left open letters and briefing records on its official campaign page since 2022, argued at a March 2024 online briefing that "if the governance problem is solved, the roughly 11.9 trillion won market capitalization could rise to 1.7 times just from a peer-level valuation, and to up to four times by 2028 with an excellent CEO and an independent board." FCP also pointed out that "about 58% of market capitalization is cash-like assets, making capital allocation inefficient" and that "operating profit fell from 1.5 trillion won in 2016 to 1.2 trillion won in 2023," and argued that management had effectively controlled about 12% of shares through so-called "self-donation." KT&G pushed back that the base figures FCP presented (export stick counts and revenue) contained errors, saying it would "consider legal measures if falsehoods continue." The FCP figures and assessments quoted here are the fund's claims; the company pointed to errors in base figures such as export volumes and revenue used in the briefing. That rebuttal is not expanded into a legal judgment on every individual governance allegation.

How appointment procedures and return policy moved during the same period as this dispute is this article's subject. That events followed in time does not prove activism was the sole cause of every policy change.

2. The 2024 turning point: the first CEO change in nine years, and cumulative voting

The procedure leading to the turning point is also worth recording. FCP left a public campaign record from 2022 and sent a letter to the board on December 1, 2023. The company's board formalized the appointment process in late December of that year by resolving screening criteria for the next CEO candidate. Ahead of the appointment, an unusual alignment formed in which both the largest shareholder, Industrial Bank of Korea, and the activist fund were critical of the company (Newspim).

The turning point was the 37th annual general meeting on March 28, 2024. KT&G replaced its CEO for the first time in nine years, and internal candidate Bang Gyeong-man was appointed. The process differed from before. At shareholders' request, the company ran cumulative voting that combined the CEO appointment agenda item with other director appointments to protect minority shareholder rights, and Bang was elected with the most votes. The then-largest shareholder, Industrial Bank of Korea, expressed opposition but did not change the outcome.

The candidate screening and confirmation process was also disclosed. The board resolved the screening criteria in late 2023 and ran the process publicly, confirming Bang Gyeong-man as the final candidate on February 22, 2024. Bang joined Korea Tobacco & Ginseng Corporation through open recruitment in 1998 and served as head of brand (launching Esse Change) and head of global (expanding presence from about 40 to about 100 countries and opening the era of 1 trillion won in overseas cigarette revenue). The company introduced him as having "played a leading role in pursuing shareholder-return policies at the highest level at home and abroad."

On the same day, outside director Son Dong-hwan, recommended by Industrial Bank of Korea, was also appointed. FCP had initially recommended its head Lee Sang-hyun as an outside director candidate, then withdrew to prevent vote splitting and shifted to supporting the bank's candidate. The day after the AGM, March 29, 2024, FCP sent a letter to the board demanding that five requests, including the introduction of share-price-linked performance compensation, be implemented by the end of June, and citing past investigations, called for the voluntary resignation of existing directors (per FCP). The activist campaign continued after the AGM.

3. Shareholder returns in numbers, three stages: execution record and long-term plans

KT&G's shareholder-return policy must be read by separating the execution record and plans on the company's investor-information page. Because the periods and scopes differ, no same-basis growth rate is calculated from the three figures' sizes alone.

Stage 1 (2021–2023): total returns of around 2.75 trillion won. About 1.75 trillion won in dividends over three years (FY2021 575.9 billion won, FY2022 581.4 billion won, FY2023 590.8 billion won) and about 1 trillion won in share buybacks (FY2021 348.3 billion won, FY2022 356.8 billion won, FY2023 302.6 billion won). The total shareholder return ratio over the period was 93%, which the company said exceeded the global large-cap tobacco average. In 2023 it also paid an interim dividend for the first time. FCP's rebuttal, however, was that the share price stagnated despite these returns.

Stage 2 (2024–2027): At the November 2023 "Value Day," the company presented three-year returns of about 2.8 trillion won (1.8 trillion in dividends + 1 trillion in buybacks) and a plan to cancel about 10 million treasury shares, half of its holdings (about 7.5% of total issued shares). In the November 2024 corporate value enhancement plan it enlarged this, promising "total cash returns of 3.7 trillion won + α over four years and a total shareholder return ratio of 100%+." On the company's published materials, execution came to FY2024 dividends of about 588.4 billion won with buybacks of about 546.7 billion won and cancellations of about 861.7 billion won, and FY2025 dividends of about 627.4 billion won with buybacks of about 560 billion won and cancellations of about 926.3 billion won. Then in April 2026 it said it had cancelled all 10,866,189 treasury shares it held (9.5% of total issued shares, about 1.8516 trillion won in value), achieving the 2024–2027 plan's cancellation target early.

Stage 3 (2027–2030): At the November 5, 2026 Capital Markets Day and in a voluntary filing of its corporate value enhancement plan, the company presented a new plan. The core: ▲ cumulative basic shareholder returns of 5 trillion won for 2027–2030 ▲ 2026 dividend per share of 8,000 won (up 33% from 6,000 won in 2025) ▲ a target payout ratio of at least 75% by 2030 (57.6% in 2025) ▲ 1.2 trillion won of treasury share purchases and cancellations for 2027–2030 with a shift to year-round continuous purchasing ▲ review of special returns when additional returns are needed. The voluntary-filing figures below were cross-checked against Seoul Economic Daily's KOSCOM filing table. The original cautions that plans may change with future conditions and that actual implementation is decided through board and shareholder-meeting resolutions. As a capital-allocation principle it pinned down: "use all cash secured after ordinary investment for basic shareholder returns, and select new growth investments by strict criteria." CEO Bang Gyeong-man called it "shareholder returns at the highest global level."

Buyback amounts and cancellation amounts are not simply added together. Shares bought in the past can be cancelled this year, so an equal amount of cash does not go out again at the time of cancellation. The April 2026 cancellation of all held shares in particular was an event that changed share counts and rights, not the payment of new cash equal to its valuation that day. Dividends and buybacks as cash flows must be separated from cancellations as changes in share count, or policy size gets double-counted.

4. The source of funds: do results back it up?

Bigger promises require funding to follow. Look first at the operating results and annual outlook the company presented. KT&G recorded its best-ever quarter in Q3 2026 with consolidated revenue of 1.9052 trillion won (+4.3% year over year) and operating profit of 530.1 billion won (+13.9%), and presented annual guidance of 7 trillion won in revenue and 1.5 trillion won in operating profit. In Q2 it had raised annual guidance to revenue +5–7% and operating profit +10–13%, and raised the interim dividend from 1,400 won to 2,000 won. The November voluntary filing projected 2026 operating profit to grow about 28% versus 2023.

However, the payout ratio means dividends as a share of net income, so funding cannot be judged from operating profit alone. A 75% payout ratio can hold even if profit falls — but the absolute dividend amount would then differ. While the ratio is raised from 57.6% in 2025 to at least 75% in 2030, if profit declines it must be reviewed together with the company's principle of "returning all cash left after ordinary investment." Miss the difference among operating cash flow, working capital, capital expenditure and net income, and the funding for returns can be overestimated. The priority question between returns and investment is a tension built into the 5 trillion won plan.

5. Institutional change: cumulative voting, e-voting and the board

Procedures changed alongside return size. KT&G discloses that it introduced cumulative voting to strengthen minority shareholders' rights and electronic voting for voting convenience. Cumulative voting lets shareholders concentrate votes on one director agenda item instead of spreading them, amplifying minority influence. At the 2024 AGM, the CEO appointment item was combined with other director items by shareholder request and cumulative voting was held — a structure in which minority votes could actually be reflected in the outcome operated. A structure in which a CEO appointment passes through a board-led public screening and is confirmed by shareholder vote allows the internal-origin outcome and the transparency of the procedure to be evaluated separately. The fact that an internal candidate was appointed does not by itself settle that the process was not independent, nor does the existence of a public process settle that independence was completed.

That said, introducing institutions and their effectiveness are separate matters. How far the part of FCP's five demands concerning management compensation, such as share-price-linked performance pay, has been reflected cannot be confirmed from public materials alone (unconfirmed). Assessments of board composition independence can also diverge across institutions and are hard to settle.

6. Remaining issues: FCP's unfinished demands and structural tensions

Unfinished demands remain on FCP's campaign list. On its own page the fund argues that Jung Kwan Jang (KGC) "must leave the shadow of a tobacco company and become a global K-food company," calling for a restructuring amounting to a business separation, and demands "focus" on businesses beyond the core. Whether these claims will be reflected in company strategy is unconfirmed.

Structural tensions also remain. The fact that Industrial Bank of Korea expressed opposition and proposed a director candidate in the 2024 CEO appointment shows that a shareholder of a public nature also participated in major decisions. The case in which the largest shareholder and a majority of voting rights diverged in the 2024 process suggests this structure can become an issue again at any time. FCP's claims about treasury-share donations and voting rights are the campaign party's claims. This article does not judge their legal merits or the results of any follow-on litigation.

Even when measuring policy effects by comparing past and current share prices, overall market moves and the influence of profit, exchange rates and regulation must be separated out. This article does not use share-price gains as confirmed evidence of governance improvement.

7. Risks and conditions: what it takes to keep the 5 trillion won

First, the regulatory environment of the core business. The tobacco industry is exposed to tax and regulatory tightening in each country; if overseas cigarette growth slows, the source of return funding shakes. Second, the premise of the 75% payout target. If profit stagnates or falls, choices become necessary between absolute dividend totals and investment capacity. Third, the uncertainty of "special returns." The company has said only that it will review them flexibly considering share-price levels and business and financial conditions; the basic 5 trillion won is itself a mid-to-long-term plan, and special returns beyond it are a separate review item. Fourth, the possibility of activism reigniting. Some of FCP's core demands remain unresolved, so a stagnant share price could intensify the campaign again. Fifth, changes in execution method. The company disclosed a shift to year-round continuous treasury share purchases from 2027, but continuous purchasing does not necessarily lower the average purchase price. Results vary with actual prices and timing, acquisition limits and executed volumes, so it must be evaluated from post-implementation records.

8. Conclusion: how to evaluate checks and returns together

In KT&G's public record, activism's challenge, the 2024 cumulative vote and CEO change, and the subsequent expansion of return policy are observed together. But connecting the three events with arrows alone does not prove causation. Operating results, the policy environment and pre-existing plans must also be examined.

The criteria for judgment are two. First, is the 2027–2030 plan executed as scheduled? What was achieved early in 2026 was the cancellation target within the existing plan, which differs from completing the whole cash-return plan. Second, do expanded returns avoid damaging growth investment? Both are good starts opened by 2026 results (a record quarter), but not completion. The final report card on governance improvement will be graded not by the 5 trillion won promise but by its execution rate and the durability of the shareholder return ratio. These indicators can be verified continuously through the corporate value enhancement plan filings the company renews every year (the copy confirmed this time: November 5, 2026).
