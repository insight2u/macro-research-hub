December 12: those who invoked military order destroyed it

Putting the state’s armed forces at the service of private power cannot be dressed up as resolve or comradeship. December 12 was more than an internal military dispute. Removing its established name, military rebellion, blurs the betrayal of the constitution by those charged with defending it.

Seizing command under the cover of an arrest

Case 96Do3376 treated the arrest of Army Chief of Staff Jeong Seung-hwa without presidential approval or lawful procedure as resistance to military command authority. Later control of the government did not cure the earlier illegality. The national military did not belong to a faction of officers.[1]

Start with those who issued criminal orders

The Court held that following an unlawful order did not remove the unlawfulness of a criminal act. That principle must not become a device for dumping responsibility on subordinates. Those with the power to deploy troops, overpower commanders and direct the joint operation must be questioned first.[1][2]

What the phrase successful rebellion conceals

The judgment upheld punishability despite the later seizure of government and constitutional changes. We reject a scorecard that treats a rebellion’s outcome as historical merit. The issue is the destruction of political neutrality and constitutional command. Civilian control matters more than a tale of loyalty and betrayal.[1]
