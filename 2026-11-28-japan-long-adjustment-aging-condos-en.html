Japan's long adjustment and its ageing condominiums: what the official statistics show

Rate rises, falling land prices and a growing stock of housing over 40 years old, checked against Japanese government data

KOENJAZHES

Japan went through an asset-market adjustment in the 1990s and the ageing of its apartment blocks before Korea. Statistics from Japan's Cabinet Office and Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism show the scale in numbers. Korea differs in rules and population structure, so they cannot be applied directly. We set out the confirmed records and their limits together.

Rates: from 2.5% to 6.0% in 15 months

From May 1989 to August 1990 the Bank of Japan raised its official discount rate from 2.5% to 6.0%. The August 30, 1990 move lifted it from 5.25% to 6.0%, by 0.75 points (Maeil Business). The rises overlapped with the period after asset prices peaked. Rate rises were not the only cause of the long adjustment.

Land prices: down 86.7% for commercial land, 67.1% for residential

According to the Cabinet Office of Japan, land prices in the six largest cities fell from the September 1990 peak to September 2012 by 86.7% for commercial land and 67.1% for residential land. One analysis says Japanese real estate prices fell every year for 15 years from autumn 1991 to 2005 (KDI). These are land prices in large cities, not prices of individual apartments. The same report adds that Japan's long stagnation is not explained by the real estate bubble alone.

Ageing condominiums: 1.588 million homes over 40 years

According to Japan's Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism, there were about 7.236 million condominium homes for sale at the end of 2025. About 1.588 million of them were over 40 years old. In ten years, at the end of 2035, that is expected to be about 3.131 million, around 2.0 times, and at the end of 2045 about 5.036 million, around 3.2 times. Japan holds the problem that Korea's rebuilding candidates face, at a larger scale and earlier.

Rebuilding record: 347 cases in total

On November 13, 2026 the ministry announced that condominium rebuilds totaled 347 cases, about 27,700 homes. The previous count was 338. The number has grown since 2003. Set against 1.588 million ageing homes, the scale of completed rebuilds is small. Rebuilt homes are not keeping up with the growth in old ones.

What differs from Korea

Japan's land price figures center on commercial land, and population decline and ageing overlapped. Korea's base rate is 3.25% now. Taxes, lending, ownership structure and rebuilding vote requirements also differ. Japan's records do not show the direction of Korea's market. They show how deep and long an adjustment can be and how slowly ageing housing can be handled. This article is not investment advice.

Scope of verification

Figures in this article were checked against public agency statistics and dated press reports. Figures taken from an article are as reported there and may need further checking against the original data. Interpretations give grounds and limits together and are not settled forecasts.
