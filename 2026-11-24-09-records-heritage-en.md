# When manuscripts and climbing gear become heritage

Exhibition opening announced November 23, 2026 · Culture

## Records beyond finished works

The Korea Heritage Service announced a photographic exhibition, "Records of Our Lives, Heritage of the Future," from November 23 through December 6 in the first-floor special exhibition room of the National Palace Museum of Korea. It introduces six preliminary cultural heritage selections made this year. This is an introduction based on the announcement, not a review from a visit.[1]

The format matters. A photographic exhibition does not establish that every original manuscript and object will be on display. Nor should preliminary cultural heritage selection be rewritten as a completed designation or registration under another system.

## From comics to democratization

The selections include manuscripts for the comic "Pigeon Chorus," scripts for the television animation "Dokgo Tak's Pigeon Chorus," and reporting manuscripts and declarations from the Busan-Masan Democratic Uprising. The announcement describes an opening section on ordinary people during industrialization and urbanization, followed by people who took to the streets for democracy.[1]

A manuscript preserves work before a finished creation. A script leaves traces of production and communication that the screen alone cannot show. Considering these records as heritage differs from pricing a famous work. This article does not assess their market value or transfer of rights.

## Traces of people acting together

The exhibition also introduces Lee Ungno's "People" series and associated belongings, memorabilia from the joint Korean team at the 41st World Table Tennis Championships in Chiba, and mountaineer Kim Hong-bin's equipment and records. The organizer connects ordinary lives, democratization, solidarity, harmony and challenge across five sections.[1]

That structure is the organizer's interpretation. Without visiting, this article cannot say how the actual arrangement and photographs affect viewers. It can ask how the activities and people behind each record are connected.

## Preservation begins after a list

Selecting an object for attention is not the end of heritage work. Questions remain about whose records survived, what disappeared, and how research and protection will continue. The service identifies systematic investigation and protection, community cooperation and programs using the heritage as future directions. Those plans should not be reported as fully achieved results.[1]

Check the museum's latest opening hours and visitor guidance before going. The place and dates in the November 23 announcement are a reference, not a guarantee of every later operating condition. The exhibition's question is how everyday objects and records become shared memory.
