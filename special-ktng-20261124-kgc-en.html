As the red ginseng throne shakes, why did KGC declare itself a "global total health food company"?

DailyShot Macro Research | KT&G Special 5

Published November 24, 2026 · Data rechecked: November 24, 2026 KST

This article's question: While domestic red ginseng spending shrinks, how did KGC, the owner of Jung Kwan Jang, redefine the market it competes in? And do its 2026 results support that new definition?

1. The issue: cracks in the red ginseng throne

KGC (formerly Korea Ginseng Corp.) and its Jung Kwan Jang brand have long kept the equation "red ginseng = Jung Kwan Jang" in Korea's health functional food market. But the market underlying that equation is shaking.

On the Korea Health Functional Food Association's survey basis, the domestic red ginseng market shrank about 35% in four years, from 1.471 trillion won in 2021 to 953.6 billion won in 2025. In the association's "2026 Health Functional Food Market Status and Consumer Survey," released this November, this year's red ginseng purchases were projected at 931.5 billion won. Because the sample differs from the previous edition's 2025 purchase figure, no direct growth-rate calculation is made. Red ginseng's share of the total health functional food market also fell from 15.6% (2025) to 15.2% (2026 projection). Reporting on the survey published in 2025 said red ginseng's share of purchases fell from 25.9% in 2021 to 16% in 2025. That is a share of spending, not of buyers. The 2026 report enlarged its sample and recalculated the 2025 figures, so the old and new editions' ratios are not joined into one continuous series.

The symbolic event already happened last year. In the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety's tally, vitamin and mineral products overtook red ginseng for the first time to become the top-selling category in the country — a signal that the status of a long-dominant category has changed. The point is that this is not a temporary cyclical fluctuation but a structural shift. So the first question to ask: did KGC see this change coming, and how is it responding?

2. Redefining the market: erasing "Ginseng Corp."

KGC's will to transform shows in its corporate identity (CI). A CI change should not be confused with a registered corporate name change. On April 1 this year, KGC Ginseng Corp. changed its CI for the first time in 14 years, changing its CI mark to "KGC." Marking its 127th anniversary, the company said it would "leap beyond ginseng and red ginseng to become a global top-tier total health food company." The market the company defines for itself has changed from the "red ginseng market" to the "global total health food market."

This redefinition has three layers.

First, category expansion — from a single red ginseng ingredient to a broad health functional food portfolio. KGC's official materials say it is commercializing non-red-ginseng ingredients such as agarwood and mushrooms to its quality standards. Products made from ingredients other than red ginseng have begun to feature in KGC's growth story.

Second, the addition of an ingredient business (B2B). In early May this year, KT&G set up a dedicated global nutrition business center and said it would pursue business-to-business sales of red ginseng ingredients to global food, beverage and cosmetics companies. At its third-quarter earnings call it confirmed it is discussing technology partnerships and joint product development with global F&B and beauty companies. This widens the market beyond the finished-goods brand Jung Kwan Jang to being a "red ginseng ingredient supplier."

Third, a reset of geographic boundaries. KT&G has set a goal of raising the overseas revenue share of its health functional food business to at least 30% by 2030. This is a future target set by the company, not an established market position or achieved share. The goal carries a premise of "localization" rather than "export expansion" — a turn from export-led growth toward locally tailored product development and local distribution networks. The quality of overseas growth should be judged not only by the number of export destinations but by whether revenue and profit recur locally.

3. Before the new market's size, ask about its scope first

Market research firm Grand View Research estimates the nutritional supplements market at $517.1 billion in 2025, projecting growth to $550.7 billion in 2026 and $862.5 billion in 2033, a projected compound annual growth rate of 6.6% for 2026–2033. This is a private research firm's estimate and projection, not a government-verified revenue total. The figure cannot be read as secured future revenue or treated as a market KGC will capture.

The more important difference is definition. That report's "nutritional supplements" covers not only dietary supplements but also functional foods and beverages. It is not the same scope as the health functional food market that Korean law and consumer surveys define. So dividing the global estimate by Korea's roughly 6 trillion won in purchases and calling it a "100x market" may keep the units straight while distorting the comparison's meaning. With no exchange-rate basis, no won conversion was added.

Calling a market big is an entry point to a business plan, not a conclusion. Scientific evidence for ingredients, country-by-country labeling and sales rules, and brands and distribution channels local consumers recognize are all required. Red ginseng brand trust built in Korea does not automatically transfer to every consumer abroad. Turning broad demand for health into a specific product's revenue is the core of the business.

KGC's move to widen from Jung Kwan Jang finished goods to red ginseng ingredient B2B can also be read this way. Building brand awareness with consumers and supplying ingredients to other companies' products involve different counterparties and negotiation terms. It can widen the supply chain, but it cannot be assumed to have the same profit structure as finished goods. That is why technology partnerships still under discussion are not written as signed contracts.

So the question for evaluating a market redefinition must be more concrete than "how big a market is out there." Which consumers and buyers in which countries will it serve, with what? Does it have evidence and distribution that meet local standards? Can it recover selling and research costs? Explaining a larger market size and actually widening a company's accessible market are different things.

4. Structural change in domestic consumption: a flat market, diverging spending

The association's 2026 consumer survey (based on purchase records of 8,000 households nationwide, with an enlarged sample) summarizes the domestic market as follows.

  • Market size: 6.128 trillion won (2026 projection). Newsis, reporting the survey, said this was down 0.7% year over year on the recalculated comparison basis. It is not directly compared with the previous edition's 5.9626 trillion won for 2025.
  • Purchase experience rate: 82.7%, up from 81.6% a year earlier and the highest in three years.
  • Average annual spending per purchasing household: about 334,000 won, down from a year earlier.

The key is that the three figures point in different directions. More households buy health functional foods, but each spends less. As consumption shifts from "family shared intake" to "individual customized intake," smaller, practical products tailored to personal needs are growing faster than expensive, large gift sets, the association analyzes. Purchases for individual intake rose especially among those aged 20 and under and 61 and over.

The ingredient landscape is changing too. Red ginseng keeps the top spot in purchases (931.5 billion won) but its share is falling, while probiotics (762.6 billion won) chase in second place. Multivitamins and single vitamins turned to decline this year, while EPA/DHA oils, magnesium and coenzyme Q10 are growing. Magnesium rose from 10th to 8th in the purchase rankings. Asiae's report on the 2025 survey showed red ginseng's spending advantage among those aged 51 and over and higher shares of vitamins and probiotics among younger consumers. That 2025 survey is not mixed with the 2026 survey when reading. The younger the consumer, the more they choose vitamins, probiotics and functional ingredients over red ginseng.

Distribution has shifted as well. Of direct purchases excluding gifts, 71.6% happen in online malls. Offline, the shares of big-box discount stores and pharmacies rose slightly. Jung Kwan Jang's traditional strength — the "offline stores + gift demand" model — faces a head-on challenge.

5. KGC's domestic equation: price, format, cost

In a flat domestic market, KGC has chosen three equations.

First, the balance of price and value. In a market where spending per purchasing household is falling, a brand cannot be assumed to defend revenue on price alone. The value consumers feel and the burden they bear must be read together. Even if a price increase lifts unit price, lower volume and purchase frequency can change the effect. This article does not present unverified past price tags of individual products as if they were current consumer prices.

Second, diversification of formats and brands. Everytime, launched in 2012, is a stick-type portable format that moved red ginseng's consumption context from "gifts and holidays" to "everyday and personal." KGC's official August 31 statement put cumulative revenue at about 1.7 trillion won or more, and Goodkyung reported on November 9 that it had surpassed 2 trillion won. These are cumulative figures announced at different times, not a single year's revenue. Following sticks with ampoule and film formats and continuing new ad campaigns this year, KGC is effectively building Everytime into a second pillar — a response that locks precisely into the consumption shift toward individual intake (section 4).

Third, cost. In 2025, KGC (separate basis) saw revenue fall 6.8% to 1.0301 trillion won, but operating profit rose 48.2% to 98.9 billion won. Managing selling and administrative expenses down by 45.3 billion won year over year to 456.8 billion won contributed heavily. Revenue and profit moving in different directions means cost and channel mix must be read together. Organizationally, in March 2025 the company introduced a co-CEO system for domestic (Lim Wang-seop) and overseas (Ahn Bin) operations, separating domestic defense from overseas offense, and in April last year it signed an MOU with Shilla Duty Free to strengthen duty-free channels including Incheon, Changi and Hong Kong airports.

6. What the 2026 results say

The quarterly flow this year (KT&G filings and press releases, consolidated health functional food segment) lines up as follows.

  • Q1: revenue 332.6 billion won (+5.8%), operating profit 27.9 billion won (+53.3%). Strong Lunar New Year promotions at home and the Cheonnok and Everytime campaigns.
  • Q2: revenue 223.8 billion won (domestic 174.2 billion won, +7.8%; overseas 49.6 billion won, down 9.4 billion won on China distribution inventory adjustments), operating profit 10.0 billion won (+61.3%).
  • Q3: revenue 372.2 billion won (+3.4%), operating profit 80.1 billion won (+12.2%). Overseas revenue of 55.5 billion won (+0.7%), with the U.S. down but China, Japan and Taiwan up.

A simple sum through Q3 gives revenue of 928.6 billion won and operating profit of 118.0 billion won. This is a calculated sum of the quarterly figures above, not a number reconciled against a separate cumulative disclosure. Its scope also differs from the KGC separate-basis results in the previous section, so no direct comparison is made. The core of the 2026 results: revenue is in an early recovery stage, but profitability has clearly improved. The company cited expansion of high-margin channels and profitability-focused management. It does not decompose each factor's contribution numerically or assert how much a price increase lifted profit.

At group level, KT&G posted its best-ever quarter in Q3 with consolidated revenue of 1.9052 trillion won (+4.3%) and operating profit of 530.1 billion won (+13.9%), and presented full-year group guidance of 7 trillion won in revenue and 1.5 trillion won in operating profit. In Q2 it had already raised its revenue growth outlook from 3–5% to 5–7% and its operating profit outlook from 6–8% to 10–13%. Health functional foods have taken a place as one of the group's three growth pillars (overseas cigarettes, NGP, health functional foods), but in both absolute size and growth rate it is clearly still a supporting pillar compared with the tobacco business.

7. Risks and conditions: what the redefinition must clear

For the new market definition to be completed by results, several conditions must be cleared.

First, the structural slump in red ginseng exports. On Korea Agro-Fisheries & Food Trade Corporation (KATI) data, red ginseng exports fell four straight years, from $71.72 million in 2022 to $62.67 million in 2023, $58.16 million in 2024 and $40.94 million in 2025, and this year's first half was $20.21 million, down 4.2% from a year earlier. Ginseng exports also fell over the same period from $269.72 million to $203.48 million, with this year's first half down 33.2% to $61.7 million. The premise of "red ginseng globalization" is not yet supported by export numbers. Whether the localization strategy converts into actual local revenue is the key.

Second, management of ingredients and costs. For a brand to keep trust, ingredient quality and the production base must be maintained together. That said, a price change at a specific point in a growing region cannot be read as identical to the company's average purchase cost. Inventory, contract terms, product mix and selling prices change how fast costs pass through to profit and loss. This material alone does not predict KGC's future cost inflation rate.

Third, the volatility of overseas revenue. The Value News reported 2025 overseas health functional food revenue of 252.1 billion won, down 33.0% from a year earlier, with China-region revenue also down. That report must be read distinguishing separate-entity figures from group business-segment figures. The 0.7% year-over-year increase in Q3 2026 overseas revenue of 55.5 billion won is one quarter's change, not evidence that an overseas turnaround is complete. Regulation, inventory adjustments and the distribution environment are conditions to check together, and no single regulation is asserted as the sole cause of a revenue decline.

Fourth, the limits of the domestic market. The domestic health functional food market is flat at 6.1 trillion won, and the red ginseng market is shrinking. What KGC can expect at home is not market growth but share defense and profitability management. That is exactly where the significance of the 30% overseas share goal lies.

8. Conclusion: market definition is half; execution is the rest

Back to the opening question. KGC has redefined the market it competes in from a "roughly 1 trillion won, stagnant domestic red ginseng market" to "broader health food demand including overseas finished goods and ingredient supply." Given the domestic consumption shifts (individual intake, the move online, red ginseng's falling share), this redefinition reads as a persuasive direction of response.

The 2026 results support half of this redefinition. Rising quarterly operating profit, improving domestic revenue and portfolio diversification through stick-type products and non-red-ginseng ingredients are positive. But the other half — substantive growth overseas — remains conditional. Falling red ginseng exports, the durability of China's recovery and cost pressure are variables to watch. The criterion for judgment is simple: how much closer the company gets each year to its 2030 goal of a 30% overseas revenue share, and whether that growth does not damage profitability.
