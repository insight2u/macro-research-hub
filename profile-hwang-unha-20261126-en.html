Hwang Un-ha: after dividing prosecutorial power, who protects citizens' rights?

26 November 2026 | DailyShot Innovation News | Current and former lawmakers, profile 12

One question runs through Hwang Un-ha's politics: should one institution control both investigation and prosecution? The police reform official became a lawmaker and continued that argument through years as a defendant. Yet moving powers does not automatically enlarge rights. Reform must also explain who corrects mistakes by police and new investigative bodies.

This profile distinguishes his final acquittal in the Ulsan election case from his memoir's explanations, compares both sides of the supplementary-investigation debate, and examines housing and capital-relocation proposals. Reducing him to a former officer fighting prosecutors misses both daily-life policy and the question of controlling all power.

1. From police investigative reform to legislation

His Assembly biography lists a Korea National Police University bachelor's, a Korea University public-administration master's and a Sungkyunkwan University law doctorate, alongside heading police investigative-structure reform, the Daejeon police agency and the Police Human Resources Development Institute. It also records 21st Assembly industry, trade, energy and small-business committee work. His expertise is broader than criticism of prosecutors, though investigative institutions remain its center.[1]

NamuWiki surveys police service, party changes and disputes, but is not the final verification step. An earlier conviction must always be read with later appellate outcomes. An old headline cannot substitute for present legal status.[2]

2. Ulsan: from first-instance conviction to final acquittal

Yonhap and Dong-A Ilbo reported on 14 August 2025 that the Supreme Court finalized acquittals for Hwang and former mayor Song Cheol-ho. The first court sentenced Hwang to a combined three years for election-law and abuse-of-authority charges. The appeal court reassessed key testimony and the official scope of preparing and transmitting intelligence, and acquitted him; the Supreme Court found no legal error requiring reversal.[3][4]

Calling him finally convicted in that case is wrong. Extending acquittal to every defendant is also wrong: the same reports describe final convictions on separate charges for former deputy mayor Song Byeong-gi and others. People and charges must remain distinct.[3][4]

Final acquittal is a major criminal-law conclusion, not judicial confirmation of every institutional motive described in his memoir or every surrounding dispute. This profile compares detailed reporting rather than the complete judgments. Respecting acquittal and endorsing an entire political narrative are different acts.

3. His third whale-meat memoir: strength and limits of recollection

《검찰은 왜 고래고기를 돌려줬을까 3》, published by Haeyo Media on 13 February 2026, is a 288-page final volume. Youngpoong's public contents and extracts cover Ulsan litigation, joining the Rebuilding Korea Party and legislation separating investigation and prosecution. Only that public material was reviewed, not the full book.[5]

The page 107 extract recalls telling his family that finding an old phone might help clear his name. Pages 179-180 recount opposition from some local supporters and lawyers to his party choice. Page 252 recalls persuading Democratic Party lawmakers about reform while encountering electoral concerns.[5]

These scenes show how he experienced political risk. Other people's remarks and investigators' inner intentions remain his account, not independently confirmed facts. Supportive publisher descriptions and endorsements must not be counted again as independent verification.

The consistent theme is distrust of concentrated investigative power. His task is to turn it into checks applying to any institution, not just one organization.

4. Conducting supplementary investigation is not the same as requesting it

A May 2026 Sidae joint interview set Hwang against Kwak Kyu-taek. Hwang argued that abolishing prosecutors' direct supplementary investigation while retaining requests to police was sufficient. Horizontal communication could address even approaching limitation periods; exceptions risk reopening and expanding powers.[6]

Kwak countered that the institution deciding evidentiary sufficiency for charges needs limited supplementary investigation. Victims dissatisfied with police might otherwise lose another institution's review. This is more than professional rivalry between former police and prosecutors: dispersed power, speed and victims' objections are all involved.[6]

Horizontal cooperation is a principle; enforcement when it stalls is design. Deadlines, refusals to investigate, independent review of non-referral or non-prosecution, access to records and notices need detail. These are conditions his reform must answer, not a declaration that his argument is wrong. The interview documents the debate then, not the content of current law.

5. Police power needs controls too

Sports Seoul's September 2026 report on an Assembly forum covers investigative capacity and checks alongside separation. Specialist personnel, review, training, quality, assignment and delay appeared in presentations and discussion. It did not end with unconditional trust in police after reducing prosecutorial powers.[7]

A forum proposal is not implementation or demonstrated results. This profile checked the reported range of discussion, not adoption, funding and execution of every suggestion.

The hardest test is whether Hwang will restrain police abuse and poor investigation as forcefully as prosecutorial power. Institutional balance is a means. Citizens escaping wrongful investigation and victims obtaining timely answers are the ends.

6. Housing vulnerability: settlement after allocation

Housing-support bill 2222881, introduced on 17 November 2026 by Hwang, Moon Jung-bok and thirteen colleagues, concerns people in inadequate accommodation such as tiny subdivided rooms and gosiwon lodging, and people struggling to secure stable homes after leaving institutions.[8][9]

The official explanation covers eligibility decisions and objections, maintaining tenancies, community settlement and inspection of defects with requests for correction. It reserves at least 30% each of new annual purchased and lease-backed public rental supply, and at least 3% of new and relet construction-type rentals, for the housing-vulnerable. It also proposes a national survey every three years and Assembly reports on plans, results, occupants and average waits.[8][9]

Ratios alone miss half the proposal. Income, health and care problems can make someone lose housing again after allocation. Connecting homes and settlement matters. Effects on other vulnerable applicants' waits, local location and quality, staffing and finance still need scrutiny.

The Assembly notice records referral to the land committee on 18 November and consultation until 27 November. These are proposals under review, not existing supply obligations. A welfare publication carrying the party's press-conference account supports the stated political purpose, not independent proof of results.[9][10]

7. Sejong: deleting a branch designation does not complete relocation

Yonhap News TV reported on 23 November 2026 that Hwang proposed deleting the Assembly Act provision defining the Sejong complex as a branch, to align it with an administrative-capital special-law plan. It also described transitional provisions preserving the existing basis for installation and operation until relocation is completed.[11]

Calling it immediate abolition of Sejong construction contradicts that account. Calling full relocation settled because a bill was introduced is equally wrong. Related laws, constitutional issues, costs and plans remain. The bill number and complete original were not directly obtained; discussion is limited to reported purpose and transition.

Spatial decentralization may resemble his concern over concentrated investigative power. But moving an institution does not guarantee better daily life. Commuting, housing, education, services, relocation costs and overlapping projects must be assessed. These questions connect purpose to implementation rather than presupposing failure.

8. From relocating powers to securing usable rights

His record, memoir and interviews show a consistent former-police critique of concentrated investigative and charging powers. Final acquittal in Ulsan is essential to his current legal description.

A long fight is not a completed result. How new agencies are checked, whether housing recipients settle securely and how relocation changes citizens' costs are the next tests. Does rearranging institutions become rights people can actually use? That is the next scorecard.
