Remove the rioter stigma from Gwangju. Hold the violent rulers responsible.

An account of Gwangju as a clash between two sides is not fair if it erases who controlled the guns and state machinery. The Supreme Court found the citizens’ resistance legitimate in defence of the constitutional order. Remove the state’s stigma before assigning responsibility for its violence.

Martial law cannot legalise insurrection

The judgment assessed the nationwide expansion of martial law at midnight on May 17, 1980, and coercion of constitutional institutions as part of the insurrection. Martial-law measures intended to subvert the constitution are subject to judicial review. An emergency is not unlimited permission to trample the constitution.[1][2]

Do not make resistance and repression equivalent

The Court found that brutally suppressing legitimate protests, further intimidating the president and cabinet and preventing their exercise of authority, constituted constitutional subversion. Leaving out Chun’s convictions for insurrection and murder for that purpose while treating everything as an unresolved wording dispute about individual orders reverses an established judgment.[1][2]

Put harm and resistance at the centre of memory

There is no need to combine incompatible casualty counts or invent unverified orders. Accurate records already carry the weight of state violence. We reject accounts that use caution as a reason to blur the difference between perpetrators and resistance. Citizens’ deaths must not become administrative costs. Demand that the state respect the rights they defended.
