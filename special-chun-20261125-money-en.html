220.5 Billion Won Forfeiture: Pardon and Property Liability Are Separate Issues

Do not use a finalized amount as if it were the current unpaid balance. Different legal boundaries for punishment, forfeiture, and third-party property.

2026.11.25 · English edition · translated from the Korean source edition checked at 20:10 KST

Attaching a Benchmark in Front of a Number

The finalized forfeiture amount for Chun Doo-hwan is 220.5 billion won. This is confirmed in the appellate holdings and Supreme Court dismissal of appeals records provided by the National Institute of Korean History. This number is a historically finalized amount, not the unpaid balance remaining today or the scale of current family assets. Writing a balance requires separate, up-to-date enforcement data. This article does not present a current figure for which that verification has not been completed.

Pardon Was Not Another Word for Forfeiture Exemption

The special pardon and release of December 1997 was a decision related to the execution of punishment. Contemporary MBC reporting explained that enforcement of forfeiture continued even after the pardon of Chun Doo-hwan and Roh Tae-woo. Finalized conviction, pardon, and monetary enforcement cannot be lumped together into a single line of success or failure. Receiving a pardon does not mean the prior conviction did not exist.

Third-Party Property Has Separate Requirements

In Ruling 2019Du63447 of July 28, 2022, the Supreme Court judged whether forfeiture enforcement against a third party presupposes enforcement against the offender and whether it can be enforced after the offender's death. The judgment that enforcement under that legal doctrine could not be carried out when the offender had died is material showing limits of the recovery system. However, this ruling is not extended as a blanket determination on 2026 law and every individual asset. Subsequent legislation and case-by-case enforcement must be checked separately.

Evidence of Funds and Acts Instead of Bloodline

Criminal liability cannot be imposed merely because someone is a descendant, nor can all property be called illegal merely because it is in a family member's name. Conversely, the character of funds cannot go unexamined merely because the name is different. What is required is proving how assets were acquired and legal requirements. When apology, moral responsibility, and legal property liability are read separately, discussions of recovery can become a public question distinct from emotional guilt by association.

Sources and scope of verification

Court judgments, public investigation decisions and institutional records have different authors and dates. Current unpaid balances, individual assets and subsequent changes in law are outside the scope of this verification. This is not a full-book or complete-film review.
