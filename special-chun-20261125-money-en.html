KRW220.5 billion: economic achievements cannot erase bribery

Chun’s money is not an entertaining story about his retirement household. The final KRW220.5 billion forfeiture order records criminal dealings between public authority and corporate money. Discounting corruption as the price of growth grants another privilege to a ruler who deprived citizens of their rights.

Independent editorial criticism · Revised 26 November 2026

한국어 · English · 日本語 · 简体中文

Read the amount in the judgment accurately

The judgment reproduced by the National Institute of Korean History orders forfeiture of KRW220,500,000,000 from Chun. It became final with his life sentence on April 17, 1997. This is not today’s unpaid balance. We do not assert a current recovery total or beneficial ownership of relatives’ assets without verification.[1][2]

Power received corporate money while citizens could not scrutinise it

Money received by a ruler with influence over policy, licensing and allocation is more than personal greed. It damages trust in public decisions and fair competition. Under dictatorship, citizens’ freedom to scrutinise and change those decisions was also constrained. This is our institutional criticism, grounded in the established bribery responsibility.

A pardon cannot close the account of responsibility

The December 22, 1997 special pardon was not an acquittal. The dismissal in constitutional case 97HeonMa404 concerned the applicants’ direct and personal rights, not moral absolution. Our question concerns neither unverified property rumours nor inflated figures, but how persistently the state pursued enforcement of established responsibility.[3]
