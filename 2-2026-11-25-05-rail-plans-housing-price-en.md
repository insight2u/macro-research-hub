# A railway on the map: when does it reach home prices?

## How to distinguish announcements, construction and opening in GTX and Seoul rail plans

November 25, 2026 | Everyday finance
Lead image: a conceptual 3D illustration contrasting planned tracks and actual service. Not an actual route map or project photograph.

![A conceptual 3D illustration contrasting planned tracks and actual service](https://pomf2.lain.la/f/os2o6w9h.png)

A line connecting two places on a railway map looks simple. Running trains requires passing different hurdles: planning, feasibility, funding, design, construction and safety checks. For housing decisions, the question is not merely whether a line has been drawn, but which hurdles the project has cleared.

GTX and Seoul's urban rail proposals include services already in use, projects moving through construction and ideas still under review. Treating them all as the same "railway benefit" risks valuing a distant possibility as a benefit already secured today.

## Inclusion in a plan is a starting point, not an opening guarantee

On November 10, 2026, the transport ministry stated that the Fifth National Railway Network Plan had not yet been finalized.[1] This research verified the official posting's title and date, but did not read the attached full clarification. Reporting on November 24 still described South Gyeongsang Province seeking inclusion of proposed rail projects.[2] That does not mean every subsequent notice had been checked as of November 25. This research did not confirm a final adoption notice.

Reports that a particular line will be included or excluded should therefore not be treated as final decisions. The national railway plan and Seoul's urban railway network plan are separate plans. A city announcement, ministry approval and completion of a project's subsequent procedures are different milestones.

The related Hankyung video distinguishes past connection concepts that never materialized from lines included in earlier plans.[3] Appearance on a video map does not establish a currently approved project. The old proposal to connect Lines 3 and 4 directly, for example, should not be recast as news of new construction.

## Through-running on GTX-A is different from stopping at Samseong

Transport ministry material carried by the Korea National Railway's railway information center states that Suseo–Dongtan began commercial service on March 30, 2024.[4] The ministry announced December 28, 2024 as the opening date for Unjeong Jungang–Seoul Station.[5] For the latter, the official posting's title and date were checked; November 2026 Newspim reporting was used to cross-check operation in two separate sections.[6]

Three things must be separated: connecting services between Seoul Station and Suseo, actually stopping at Samseong, and transferring to other lines. They are not one event necessarily completed on one date. Passing through Samseong without stopping provides different benefits from being able to use the station.

Newspim reported on November 5 that the ministry had said the previous day that safety verification made non-stop passage within 2026 difficult.[6] This research did not directly obtain that November 4 announcement. Other reporting mixes language about passage and transfers, so this article does not present a 2027 connection or a 2028 station stop as a confirmed schedule. Anyone making housing decisions based on actual travel should check current ministry and operator notices for through-running, stops and transfers separately.

## GTX-B and C: look between the ceremony and actual progress

GTX-B held a construction ceremony in March 2024, but repeated unsuccessful contractor tenders for the Yongsan–Sangbong section contributed to delays, Hankyung reported on November 10, 2026.[7] A contractor's forecast is not a government-guaranteed opening date. This research did not directly verify current contracts or schedules for every construction section, so it does not promise an opening year.

For GTX-C, an official ministry posting dated September 14, 2026 carried a title announcing construction on the 15th.[8] Hankyung reported that a ceremony had been held in January 2024 but excavation began in September 2026.[7] A ceremony alone therefore does not establish equal progress across every section. This research did not verify the full ministry attachment or the current completion percentage.

Readers should watch contracts, financing, progress by section, safety verification and operational preparations rather than ceremony photographs. This is the article's assessment framework, not a legal statement that every railway follows an identical sequence.

## Seoul's six lines: the numbers describe the scale of a plan

On June 10, 2026, Seoul announced work on its Third Urban Railway Network Plan, covering six lines, 68.5 km and a planned cost of KRW9.1996 trillion.[9] The city's full press-release text was checked. The cost is a planning figure, not spending already completed or a final construction bill.

- Gangbuk Cross Line: Mok-dong–Cheongnyangni, 25.79 km. The city described removing two stations and reflecting future development plans to improve project viability.
- Nangok Line: Boramae Park–Nanhyang-dong. The city described changes including reducing six stations to five.
- Seonam Line: a main route from Magongnaru to Gasan Digital Complex and a branch from the Western Truck Terminal to Dangsan.
- Seobu Line: options including a new private-investment tender and conversion to public funding.
- Southern Seobu extension: Seoul National University Station to the university's main gate.
- Northern Sillim extension: Saetgang to Yeouido.

These are alignments in the announcement, not confirmed station entrances or completion dates. Seoul projected that implementation would reduce average station access time from 9.97 to 8.03 minutes, adding 360,000 people to the new lines' catchment population for a total of 7.83 million.[9] These are modeled benefits of implementation, not travel-time improvements already observed.

On Nangok, The Economist Korea reported on November 3 that a KDI preliminary feasibility study was under way, with a result expected in December.[10] This research did not obtain a current original KDI result. An expected result month must not be turned into confirmed approval or a construction-start date.

## Price gains near stations are not the railway's isolated effect

Korea Economic TV reported on September 7 that apartment prices within 500 meters of three southern GTX-A stations rose strongly after opening.[11] This research did not directly obtain the underlying data, exact comparison window, sample composition or control group. The reported increase is therefore not presented as an investment return caused by rail opening or a forecast for another area.

Newspim's November reporting described different price trends in Paju and Dongtan and also discussed semiconductor-related demand, regulation and other factors.[6] Its Korea Real Estate Board figures were not independently recalculated here. The report is used only as a warning that outcomes can differ along one line according to employment, supply, finance, regulation and expectations already priced in.

Observing price growth near a railway is different from concluding that the railway caused that amount of growth. Check whether expectations were reflected before approval, how the wider market behaved and whether homes of comparable size, age and location were compared. Adding an announced benefit again after opening can count the same expectation twice.

## Undergrounding and regional rail: requests are not approvals

Enews Today reported on November 23 that Geumjeong–Dangjeong on the Gyeongbu Line and Geumjeong–Daeyami on the Ansan Line were classified for "further review" at a railway-undergrounding plan hearing.[12] The report centers on Gunpo City's position; this research did not directly verify the ministry's full plan. Further review means neither confirmed cancellation nor confirmed construction. Gunpo's positive assessment of implications for GTX-C is also a local government's interpretation, not an opening guarantee.

Outside the capital region, requests for inclusion and actual inclusion in a national plan must likewise be separated. Reporting on South Gyeongsang's proposals demonstrates regional requests, not approval of all those projects.[2] Evaluating regional inequality requires checking adopted routes, funding, schedules and user benefits. This article does not claim to have verified every regional rail project or its effect on home prices.

## In 2027, watch more than a more colorful map

Homebuyers need specific questions. How long is the actual walk from home to the entrance? Is the journey still useful after adding platform access, waiting, transfers and the final leg? What opening assumption is embedded in the home's price? Could housing costs still be carried if the project were delayed?

For policy, progress to the next milestone matters more than the number of new lines. Watch the final national plan notice, Seoul approvals and subsequent procedures, actual GTX through-running and stopping notices, section contracts and progress, and Nangok's feasibility result. This is a suggested order for evaluating information, not an investment recommendation or price forecast.

Rail can reduce constraints on movement. But a line on a map is not yet a train, and better accessibility is not a guarantee of higher property prices. Start by separating announced possibilities from services people can actually use.
