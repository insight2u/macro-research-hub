Five calendars to check before naming the winners

Infrastructure growth and company cash flow are different stories

Published 22 November 2026 · Policy announcement: 20 November

한국어English简体中文日本語

Announced demand does not all become revenue

The IEA describes data-centre investment and electricity demand as sensitive to financing, market expectations and equipment and chip bottlenecks. Treating every announcement as committed demand can also double-count applications and contracts for the same project. Our first check is the status of demand: a memorandum, a contract with cancellation conditions, or a real operating load.[4]

Align connections, permits and delivery

The second calendar is grid-connection terms and supply timing. The third covers sites, permits and local consultation. The fourth covers key equipment delivery, construction and commissioning. KEPCO's plan addresses reinforcement and construction delays together; the IEA identifies power-equipment supply chains and grid access as constraints. Progress on one item does not establish completion of the whole project.[4][5]

The last calendar is cash

The fifth check concerns costs and cash flow: the gap between initial investment and recovery, who bears cancellation or delay, input and financing costs, and working-capital terms. These are analytical questions, not allegations of problems at any company. Even in an industry with rising orders, revenue recognition and cash receipts depend on individual contracts.

Separate industrial necessity from the share price

The IEA does not find AI demand to be a uniform valuation boost across the whole energy sector. This feature likewise offers no stock list or target prices. Growing need for grids and deciding which company to buy at which price are different decisions. After policy announcements, check final rules; after company announcements, check contracts and construction.[4]
