When a machine decides, whom do we ask to explain?

Separating the convenience of automated decisions from accountability

KOENJAZHES

Calling both recommendation lists and lending decisions 'algorithms' hides important differences. News Factory's November 27 interview discussed automation's effects on judgment and relationships. Technical complexity does not make social responsibility disappear. Nor does every kind of automation carry the same risk.

Explaining a mechanism and assessing an impact differ

Explaining why a model produced one particular result is distinct from assessing whom its outputs disadvantage and how. The interview discussed technical difficulty, possible regulation and proposals for monitoring impacts. It did not show that regulation is already complete or that all regulation is impossible. A policy proposal from a broadcast must not be presented as a scheme already in force.

A starting point for data-subject rights

In its 2024 explanatory material, Korea's Personal Information Protection Commission distinguished requests for explanation or review of fully automated decisions using personal information without substantive human involvement from rejection rights for decisions with significant effects. The current statutory provision requires necessary measures when a request is made unless there is a justifiable reason not to do so. However, the commission's explanation distinguishes personalized advertising or news recommendations where people make the final choice, and simple identity checks, from covered automated decisions. Statutory exceptions involving consent or contracts also require checking the character of the particular decision.

Personal habits and institutional responsibility

Before accepting an outcome, users can ask about errors in the input data, human review and an appeal channel. An organization cannot erase the person responsible simply by saying it used an algorithm. Using a convenient service is not the same as losing the ability to challenge a decision. This article explains institutions and debate; it does not replace legal assessment of an individual case.
